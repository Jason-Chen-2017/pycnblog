### 2.1.1 Performance Metrics Theory

Performance metrics are quantitative measures used to evaluate the performance of machine learning models. These metrics help data scientists and engineers assess the accuracy, reliability, and efficiency of models in real-world applications. Common performance metrics include accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and area under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC).

**Accuracy**: Accuracy is the proportion of correct predictions out of the total number of predictions. It is calculated as:

$$
Accuracy = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}
$$

where TP is true positive, TN is true negative, FP is false positive, and FN is false negative. Accuracy is a straightforward metric but can be misleading in imbalanced datasets.

**Precision**: Precision is the proportion of correct positive predictions out of the total number of positive predictions. It is calculated as:

$$
Precision = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}
$$

Precision focuses on the quality of positive predictions and is particularly useful in scenarios where false positives are costly.

**Recall**: Recall is the proportion of correct positive predictions out of the total number of actual positives. It is calculated as:

$$
Recall = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}
$$

Recall emphasizes the ability of a model to capture all the positive instances and is important in applications where false negatives are more critical.

**F1 Score**: The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall. It provides a balanced measure of precision and recall, particularly useful in applications where both false positives and false negatives are important. The F1 score is calculated as:

$$
F1 Score = 2 \times \frac{Precision \times Recall}{Precision + Recall}
$$

**Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC-ROC)**: The AUC-ROC is a metric that measures the model's ability to distinguish between positive and negative classes. It is calculated as the area under the ROC curve, which plots the true positive rate (TPR) against the false positive rate (FPR) at various threshold settings. A higher AUC-ROC value indicates a better model.

$$
AUC-ROC = \int_{0}^{1} (1 - FPR) \times TPR \, dFPR
$$

### 2.1.2 Trend Analysis Theory

Trend analysis involves analyzing and interpreting data to identify patterns or trends over time. In the context of model performance trend analysis, this involves monitoring the performance of machine learning models over extended periods to detect changes in performance.

**Time Series Analysis**: Time series analysis is a statistical method used to analyze time-stamped data. It helps in understanding the underlying patterns and trends in the data. Common techniques in time series analysis include moving averages, exponential smoothing, and ARIMA models.

**Regression Analysis**: Regression analysis is used to identify the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables. In the context of performance trend analysis, regression analysis can be used to model the relationship between performance metrics and various factors like data drift or model degradation.

**Correlation Analysis**: Correlation analysis measures the strength and direction of the relationship between two variables. In performance trend analysis, correlation analysis can be used to identify variables that may influence the performance metrics.

**Anomaly Detection**: Anomaly detection is the process of identifying unusual patterns or outliers in data. In performance trend analysis, anomaly detection can be used to identify sudden changes in performance metrics that may indicate issues with the model or the data.

### 3.2 Mathematical Models and Formulas

The following mathematical models and formulas are crucial for understanding the performance evaluation and trend analysis components of our algorithm:

**Performance Metrics**

- **Accuracy**:
  $$
  Accuracy = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}
  $$

- **Precision**:
  $$
  Precision = \frac{TP}{TP + FP}
  $$

- **Recall**:
  $$
  Recall = \frac{TP}{TP + FN}
  $$

- **F1 Score**:
  $$
  F1 Score = 2 \times \frac{Precision \times Recall}{Precision + Recall}
  $$

- **AUC-ROC**:
  $$
  AUC-ROC = \int_{0}^{1} (1 - FPR) \times TPR \, dFPR
  $$

**Trend Analysis**

- **Moving Average**:
  $$
  MA = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i}{n}
  $$
  where \(C_i\) represents the closing value of the time series at time \(i\).

- **Exponential Smoothing**:
  $$
  S_t = \alpha \times C_t + (1 - \alpha) \times S_{t-1}
  $$
  where \(S_t\) is the smoothed value at time \(t\), \(C_t\) is the closing value at time \(t\), and \(\alpha\) is the smoothing factor.

- **ARIMA Model**:
  $$
  Z_t = \phi_1 Z_{t-1} + \phi_2 Z_{t-2} + ... + \phi_p Z_{t-p} + \theta_1 \varepsilon_{t-1} + \theta_2 \varepsilon_{t-2} + ... + \theta_q \varepsilon_{t-q} + \varepsilon_t
  $$
  where \(Z_t\) is the time series at time \(t\), \(\varepsilon_t\) is the white noise error term, and \(\phi_i\) and \(\theta_i\) are the parameters of the model.
