## Chapter 1: Introduction to AI Agent and Multi-Agent Systems

### 1.1 Background of AI Agents

#### 1.1.1 Problem Background

The emergence and rapid development of artificial intelligence have brought about significant changes in various fields. AI agents, as a key concept in artificial intelligence, have become an essential part of modern technological advancements. AI agents are designed to operate autonomously, perceive their environment, take actions, and achieve specific goals. This ability to act independently makes them particularly useful in dynamic and complex environments.

#### 1.1.2 Problem Description

While individual AI agents are powerful, the complexity of real-world problems often requires the collaboration of multiple agents. These agents must work together to achieve a common goal, often in the presence of uncertainty, competition, and limited communication. The ability of AI agents to collaborate and communicate effectively is crucial for solving complex problems that are beyond the capabilities of a single agent.

#### 1.1.3 Problem Solution

The study and development of multi-agent systems, which involve the collaboration and communication among AI agents, have become crucial in addressing complex problems. Multi-agent systems allow for distributed problem-solving, where multiple agents can work together to achieve a common objective. This approach enables the handling of large-scale problems and the development of intelligent systems that can adapt to changing conditions.

### 1.2 Core Concepts and Principles of AI Agents

#### 1.2.1 Concept Definition

An AI agent is a computational entity that perceives its environment through sensors, processes this information using its internal model, and acts upon the environment through actuators to achieve specific goals. These agents operate autonomously and are capable of adapting to changes in their environment.

#### 1.2.2 Core Characteristics

- **Autonomy**: AI agents operate independently without human intervention.
- **Social Ability**: AI agents can communicate and collaborate with other agents.
- **Adaptability**: AI agents can adapt their behavior based on changes in the environment.
- **Perception and Action**: AI agents perceive their environment through sensors and take actions through actuators.

#### 1.2.3 Differences from Traditional AI

Traditional AI systems are rule-based and cannot adapt to changes in their environment. In contrast, AI agents are designed to be more flexible and adaptive. They can learn from their experiences, adjust their behavior based on feedback, and collaborate with other agents to achieve complex goals.

### 1.3 Overview of Multi-Agent Systems

#### 1.3.1 Definition

A multi-agent system (MAS) is a system composed of multiple interacting agents, each with its own goals and capabilities. These agents communicate and collaborate with each other to achieve a common objective or set of objectives.

#### 1.3.2 Core Structures and Components

- **Agent Interaction**: The interaction between agents is a fundamental component of a multi-agent system. Agents communicate with each other through various communication channels and collaborate to achieve their goals.
- **Environment**: The environment in which agents operate is another important component. The environment can be dynamic and can change over time, requiring agents to adapt their behavior.
- **Goals and Objectives**: Each agent in a multi-agent system has its own goals and objectives. These goals can be similar or different, leading to both cooperation and competition among agents.
- **Organization**: The organization of agents within a multi-agent system can take various forms, such as hierarchical, decentralized, or centralized. The choice of organization depends on the specific problem and objectives of the system.

#### 1.3.3 Types of Multi-Agent Systems

- **Coordinator-Based Systems**: In these systems, one or more agents act as coordinators, guiding the behavior of other agents.
- **Decentralized Systems**: In these systems, agents operate independently and make decisions based on local information, without a central coordinator.
- **Hybrid Systems**: These systems combine elements of both coordinator-based and decentralized systems, providing a balanced approach to problem-solving.
