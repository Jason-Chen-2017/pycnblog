#### 1.2 Basic Concepts of Group Theory

##### 1.2.1 Definition and Properties of a Group

- **Definition**: A group is a non-empty set G equipped with an operation that combines any two elements a and b of G to form another element of G, denoted as a * b.
- **Properties**: A group must satisfy the following properties: closure, associativity, existence of an identity element, and existence of inverse elements for each element.

##### 1.2.2 Subgroups and Homomorphisms

- **Subgroups**: A subset H of a group G is a subgroup if it is itself a group under the same operation.
- **Homomorphisms**: A function φ: G → H between two groups is a homomorphism if it preserves the group operation.

##### 1.2.3 Representation Theory and Structural Theorems

- **Representation Theory**: This theory studies the way groups can be represented as linear transformations of vector spaces.
- **Structural Theorems**: These theorems provide insights into the structure of groups, such as the Fundamental Theorem of Finite Abelian Groups.

#### 1.3 Relationship Between Symmetry and Group Theory

##### 1.3.1 Symmetry in Geometry

- **Symmetry Groups**: In geometry, symmetry groups describe the transformations that leave a geometric object unchanged.
- **Examples**: The rotation group of a square, the reflection group of a line.

##### 1.3.2 Role of Groups in Physics and Chemistry

- **Symmetry Principles in Physics**: Symmetry principles are used to derive conservation laws and to simplify the formulation of physical theories.
- **Molecular Symmetry in Chemistry**: The symmetry of molecules is crucial for understanding their properties and reactions.

##### 1.3.3 Connections to Other Mathematical Branches

- **Topology**: Symmetry groups are used to study topological spaces and their properties.
- **Algebraic Geometry**: Group actions on algebraic varieties provide a deep connection between group theory and geometry.
