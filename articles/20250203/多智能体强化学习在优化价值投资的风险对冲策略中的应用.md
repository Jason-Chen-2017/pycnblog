### **2.1.2. Basic Concepts of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning**

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) is a type of reinforcement learning where multiple agents learn to achieve a common goal through interaction. The core concepts include:

- **State**: The current situation or configuration of the agents and the environment.
- **Action**: A decision or move taken by an agent in response to the current state.
- **Reward**: Feedback received by the agent after taking an action. Positive rewards indicate progress towards the goal, while negative rewards signify setbacks.
- **Policy**: A strategy that maps states to actions.

#### **Table 1.2: Attributes and Comparisons of MARL Concepts**

| **Concept** | **Attribute** | **Comparison** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **State** | Current situation or configuration | Dynamic and complex |
| **Action** | Decision taken by an agent | Based on state and policy |
| **Reward** | Feedback received | Incentivizes or penalizes actions |
| **Policy** | Strategy mapping states to actions | Guides agent behavior |

    B --> C[Perceive State]

### **2.1.3. Challenges and Solutions in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning**

MARL presents several challenges, such as coordination, communication, and convergence. Here are some solutions:

- **Coordination**: Use global reward functions or decentralized learning algorithms to encourage cooperative behavior.
- **Communication**: Implement communication protocols or shared memory to facilitate information exchange between agents.

## **Understanding Value Investing**

### **3.1.1. Basic Principles of Value Investing**

Value investing is based on the idea that the market often overvalues or undervalues certain assets. Here are the core principles:

- **Intrinsic Value**: The true value of a stock or company based on its assets, earnings, and future prospects.
- **Market Value**: The current price of a stock or company as determined by the market.
- **Undervaluation**: Occurs when the market value is less than the intrinsic value.

#### **Table 3.1: Comparison of Intrinsic and Market Value**

| **Concept** | **Intrinsic Value** | **Market Value** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition** | True value based on fundamentals | Price determined by market |
| **Significance** | Long-term investment horizon | Short-term market sentiment |

### **3.1.2. Core Indicators of Value Investing**

Several indicators are used to assess the value of an investment, including:

- **Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio**: Compares the current market price of a stock to its earnings per share (EPS).
- **Dividend Yield**: Indicates the return on investment from dividends relative to the stock price.
- **Book Value per Share**: The net assets per share owned by common shareholders.

#### **Table 3.2: Core Indicators of Value Investing**

| **Indicator** | **Definition** | **Importance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P/E Ratio** | Market price / EPS | Measures market sentiment |
| **Dividend Yield** | Dividends / Market price | Assesses income potential |
| **Book Value per Share** | Total assets - total liabilities / number of shares outstanding | Indicates asset value |

### **3.1.3. Strategies and Implementation of Value Investing**

Value investing involves several strategies, including:

- **Stock Picking**: Identifying undervalued stocks with strong fundamentals.
- **Long-Term Investing**: Holding stocks for an extended period to benefit from long-term growth.
- **Diversification**: Spreading investments across different sectors and asset classes to mitigate risk.

## **Application of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning in Risk Hedging**

### **4.1.1. Basic Concepts of Risk Hedging**

Risk hedging is a strategy used to minimize potential losses in investments. It involves taking offsetting positions in other assets to balance out the risk. Here are the core concepts:

- **Hedge**: An investment taken to reduce the risk of adverse price movements in another investment.
- **Hedging Ratio**: The proportion of the original investment that should be hedged.
- **Offset**: The use of a gain in one investment to offset a loss in another.
