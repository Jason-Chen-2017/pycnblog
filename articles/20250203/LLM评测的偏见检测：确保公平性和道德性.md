**Chapter 2: Fundamental Concepts and Theories**

**2.1 Definition and Types of Bias**

**2.1.1 Statistical Bias**

Statistical bias refers to systematic errors in statistical analysis that lead to inaccurate or misleading results. In the context of LLMs, statistical bias can occur when the training data is not representative of the real-world population, leading to biased model predictions.

**2.1.2 Sampling Bias**

Sampling bias occurs when the sample used to train the model is not representative of the target population. This can lead to biased predictions, as the model has not been exposed to diverse and varied data.

**2.1.3 Selection Bias**

Selection bias occurs when the data used to train the model is not selected randomly, leading to a biased representation of the target population. This can result in biased model outputs.

**2.1.4 Data Bias**

Data bias refers to the biases inherent in the data used to train the model. This can include biases in the data collection process, data representation, or data annotation.

**2.2 Theoretical Foundations of Bias Detection**

**2.2.1 Fairness Notions**

Fairness notions are essential for understanding bias detection. They include concepts such as equal opportunity, equal treatment, and proportionality. These notions provide a framework for evaluating the fairness of LLMs.

**2.2.2 Statistical Significance Testing**

Statistical significance testing is used to determine whether a observed effect is likely due to chance or a genuine relationship. This is a crucial tool for bias detection, as it helps identify statistically significant differences in model outputs.

**2.2.3 Machine Learning Models and Their Biases**

Machine learning models can exhibit various types of biases, including representational bias, confirmation bias, and selection bias. Understanding these biases is crucial for developing effective bias detection methods.
