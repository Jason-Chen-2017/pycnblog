#### 1.1 Zero-Shot Learning Overview

##### 1.1.1 Definition and Characteristics of Zero-Shot Learning

- **Definition**: Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) is a machine learning approach that enables models to learn and make predictions for novel classes without prior exposure to these classes during training. It is particularly relevant in scenarios where annotated data for new classes are scarce or non-existent.
  
- **Characteristics**:
  - **Novel Class Prediction**: ZSL allows models to predict classes that have not been seen during training.
  - **Generalization**: It promotes generalization ability by not relying solely on the number of examples per class.
  - **Transfer Learning**: ZSL often utilizes transfer learning techniques to leverage knowledge from related domains.

##### 1.1.2 Applications of Zero-Shot Learning in AI

- **Vision**: ZSL has been applied in image recognition tasks where models need to identify objects belonging to unseen categories.
- **NLP**: In natural language processing, ZSL is used for zero-shot text classification, where models classify text into categories that were not seen during training.
- **Audio and Speech**: ZSL is applied in audio and speech recognition for identifying sounds and voices that have not been previously encountered.
