**Key Concepts and Background**

### 1.1 What is Zero-Shot CoT?

Zero-Shot Core-Set Training (CoT) is an AI learning method that allows models to learn and generalize without being exposed to explicit examples of all possible classes during training. This approach is particularly useful in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or expensive to obtain. The core idea behind Zero-Shot CoT is to generate a small, representative set of examples (core-set) from the training data that can be used to guide the learning process.

### 1.2 The Importance and Potential of Zero-Shot CoT

The importance of Zero-Shot CoT lies in its ability to address the limitations of traditional machine learning methods that heavily rely on large, labeled datasets. By reducing the dependency on extensive data, Zero-Shot CoT can lower the barriers to entry for AI research and development, enabling the creation of intelligent systems in domains where data collection is challenging or not feasible. The potential of Zero-Shot CoT extends to various applications, including natural language processing, computer vision, and healthcare.

### 1.3 Historical Background and Development of Zero-Shot CoT

The concept of Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) has been around for over a decade, with the earliest works in this area dating back to the early 2010s. However, it was not until the advent of deep learning and the development of core-set methods that Zero-Shot CoT gained traction. Key milestones in the development of Zero-Shot CoT include the introduction of the core-set generation algorithm and the integration of transfer learning techniques.
