### Chapter 1: Introduction to New Urban Shared Garages

#### 1.1 What are New Urban Shared Garages?

**Origin and Development of Shared Garages**

The concept of shared garages can be traced back to the early 2000s when urban planners and technologists began to recognize the inefficiencies in traditional parking systems. These inefficiencies were particularly evident in densely populated urban areas where parking spaces were scarce and underutilized. The idea of shared garages was born out of the need to maximize the use of available parking spaces while minimizing waste.

**Definition and Characteristics**

New urban shared garages are structured parking spaces designed for shared use among multiple individuals or entities. These garages are typically located in urban areas where parking demand is high, and they serve as a solution to the parking space crunch. Key characteristics of new urban shared garages include:

- **Shared Use**: Parking spaces are allocated on a rotational basis, allowing multiple users to utilize the same space at different times.
- **Technology Integration**: Shared garages are often equipped with smart technologies such as sensors, automation systems, and mobile apps to facilitate efficient usage and management.
- **Sustainability**: By reducing the number of private cars on the road and promoting the use of shared vehicles, new urban shared garages contribute to reducing carbon emissions and traffic congestion.
- **Affordability**: Shared garages can offer more affordable parking solutions compared to traditional private parking spaces.

**Different Types of Shared Garages**

There are various types of shared garages, each designed to cater to different needs and contexts. Some common types include:

- **Community Shared Garages**: These are shared garages that serve a specific community, such as a residential neighborhood or an office complex.
- **Smart Shared Garages**: Equipped with advanced technologies such as IoT devices, AI, and automation systems, these garages offer a seamless and efficient parking experience.
- **Seasonal Shared Garages**: These are shared garages that operate during specific seasons, such as summer or winter, to cater to increased demand during those periods.
- **Event-Based Shared Garages**: Designed for temporary use during special events or festivals, these garages provide parking solutions for large gatherings.

#### 1.2 The Integration of Car Culture and Sharing Economy

**Understanding Car Culture**

Car culture refers to the set of beliefs, values, and behaviors associated with the use and ownership of cars. It encompasses a wide range of aspects, including the desire for personal mobility, the status symbol of owning a car, and the influence of automotive industries on society. Key elements of car culture include:

- **Automobility**: The freedom and convenience of personal transportation provided by cars.
- **Consumerism**: The car industry's impact on consumer behavior, promoting the purchase of new cars and related products.
- **Automotive Media**: The influence of movies, television shows, and advertisements that celebrate car ownership and driving.

**The Concept of Sharing Economy**

The sharing economy is an economic system built around the sharing of resources to maximize utility and minimize waste. It challenges the traditional ownership model by encouraging the use of shared resources instead of individual ownership. Key principles of the sharing economy include:

- **Collaborative Consumption**: The act of sharing resources, such as cars, homes, or tools, among individuals or groups.
- **Peer-to-Peer Lending**: The practice of borrowing or lending money or goods directly between individuals without the involvement of traditional financial institutions.
- **Platform Economy**: The use of digital platforms to connect resource providers with users, facilitating the sharing of resources.

**The Synergy between Car Culture and Sharing Economy**

The integration of car culture and sharing economy in the form of new urban shared garages represents a significant shift in how we think about car ownership and usage. This synergy is driven by several factors:

- **Environmental Concerns**: The shared use of cars can significantly reduce carbon emissions and traffic congestion, aligning with the growing global emphasis on sustainability.
- **Economic Benefits**: Shared garages offer more affordable parking solutions for both individuals and businesses, promoting economic efficiency.
- **Technological Advancements**: The integration of smart technologies in shared garages enhances the user experience and operational efficiency, making shared garages a more attractive alternative to traditional parking.
- **Cultural Shift**: The changing societal attitudes towards car ownership, with an increasing preference for flexibility, convenience, and cost-effectiveness.

### Chapter 3: Principles and Algorithms in Shared Garage Management

#### 3.1 Principles of Shared Garage Operation

**Basic Principles**

The operation of shared garages is guided by several basic principles aimed at maximizing efficiency and minimizing waste. These principles include:

- **Rotational Allocation**: Parking spaces are allocated on a rotational basis, allowing multiple users to access the same space at different times.
- **Real-time Availability**: Information on the availability of parking spaces is provided in real-time to users, enabling them to make informed decisions about parking.
- **Data-Driven Management**: The use of data analytics to optimize garage operations, allocate parking spaces efficiently, and predict user demand.

**Management Strategies**

Effective management strategies are crucial for the successful operation of shared garages. These strategies include:

- **Dynamic Pricing**: Adjusting parking fees based on demand to balance supply and demand and optimize revenue.
- **Maintenance and Upkeep**: Regular maintenance and upkeep of garage infrastructure, including lighting, ventilation, and security systems.
- **User Feedback**: Incorporating user feedback to continuously improve the garage experience and address user concerns.

**Optimization Methods**

Optimization methods are employed to enhance the efficiency of shared garages. These methods include:

- **Queue Management**: Implementing queuing systems to manage the flow of users entering and exiting the garage.
- **Space Allocation Algorithms**: Using algorithms to optimize the allocation of parking spaces based on real-time availability and user preferences.
- **Energy Efficiency**: Incorporating energy-efficient technologies, such as LED lighting and solar power, to reduce energy consumption and operating costs.

#### 3.2 Common Algorithms and Technologies

**Overview of Common Algorithms**

Several algorithms are commonly used in the management of shared garages. These algorithms include:

- **Space Allocation Algorithms**: These algorithms are used to allocate parking spaces efficiently based on real-time availability and user preferences. Common space allocation algorithms include First-Come, First-Served (FCFS) and Shortest Remaining Time (SRT).
- **Queue Management Algorithms**: These algorithms are used to manage the flow of users entering and exiting the garage, ensuring a smooth and efficient experience. Common queue management algorithms include Priority Queue and Round-Robin.
- **Data Analytics Algorithms**: These algorithms are used to analyze user data and gain insights into user behavior and demand patterns. Common data analytics algorithms include Regression Analysis and Clustering.

**Algorithm Analysis and Comparison**

The following table provides an analysis and comparison of common algorithms used in shared garage management.

| Algorithm                  | Purpose                      | Advantages                                       | Disadvantages                                                                                                     |
|----------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| First-Come, First-Served (FCFS) | Space Allocation            | Simple and easy to implement                     | May result in inefficient allocation during peak times |
| Shortest Remaining Time (SRT)  | Space Allocation            | Prioritizes shorter waiting times                | May lead to starvation of long waiting users         |
| Priority Queue              | Queue Management             | Prioritizes users based on priority levels       | May result in inefficient queuing during low demand   |
| Round-Robin                 | Queue Management             | Distributes processing fairly among users         | May result in long waiting times during peak times    |
| Regression Analysis         | Data Analytics               | Identifies relationships between variables         | Assumes linear relationships between variables       |
| Clustering                 | Data Analytics               | Groups similar data points together               | May lead to overfitting and reduced generalizability   |

#### 3.3 Mathematical Models and Formulas

**Introduction to Mathematical Models**

Mathematical models are used to represent and analyze the behavior of shared garages. These models help in understanding the relationships between various factors and making informed decisions. Common mathematical models used in shared garage management include:

- **Queueing Models**: These models are used to analyze the behavior of users in queues and optimize queue management.
- **Markov Models**: These models are used to model the state transitions in shared garages and predict user behavior.
- **Optimization Models**: These models are used to optimize the allocation of parking spaces and other resources in shared garages.

**Key Formulas and Their Derivation**

The following are some key formulas used in mathematical models for shared garage management:

- **Service Rate (μ)**: The rate at which users are served in a queue.
  $$ \mu = \frac{1}{\lambda + \rho} $$
  where $\lambda$ is the arrival rate and $\rho$ is the utilization rate.

- **Waiting Time (W)**: The average time a user spends waiting in a queue.
  $$ W = \frac{\rho}{\mu} $$

- **Queue Length (L)**: The average number of users in a queue.
  $$ L = \frac{\rho^2}{\mu} $$

- **System Utilization (ρ)**: The ratio of the average number of users in the system to the maximum capacity of the system.
  $$ \rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu} $$

**Example Applications**

Consider a shared garage with an arrival rate of $\lambda = 10$ users per hour and a service rate of $\mu = 15$ users per hour. Using the formulas mentioned above, we can calculate the waiting time, queue length, and system utilization:

- **Waiting Time (W)**:
  $$ W = \frac{\rho}{\mu} = \frac{\frac{10}{15}}{15} = \frac{2}{3} \text{ hours} $$

- **Queue Length (L)**:
  $$ L = \frac{\rho^2}{\mu} = \frac{\left(\frac{10}{15}\right)^2}{15} = \frac{4}{45} \approx 0.09 \text{ users} $$

- **System Utilization (ρ)**:
  $$ \rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu} = \frac{10}{15} = \frac{2}{3} $$

These calculations provide insights into the performance of the shared garage and can help in making informed decisions about resource allocation and queue management.
