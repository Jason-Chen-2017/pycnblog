## Abstract

The quest to enhance the quality of generated text by AI systems, particularly in the realms of Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Generative AI, remains a significant challenge. This article introduces the concept of **Self-Consistency CoT** (Conceptual Graph), a novel approach designed to address this issue. By ensuring that the concepts generated by AI are internally consistent, CoT aims to produce higher-quality text outputs. This article delves into the fundamental concepts of CoT, its algorithmic principles, mathematical models, and system architecture, providing a comprehensive exploration of this innovative technique.

## First Part: The Basics of Self-Consistency Conceptual Graph (CoT)

### Chapter 1: An Overview of Self-Consistency Conceptual Graph (CoT)

#### 1.1.1 Background

In the field of Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly in Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Generative AI, one of the most persistent challenges is how to improve the quality of the text generated by AI systems. **Self-Consistency Conceptual Graph (CoT)** represents a new approach to tackle this problem.

#### 1.1.2 Problem Description

Generative AI models, such as GPT, often produce texts that contain semantic inconsistencies and factual errors, directly affecting the quality of AI outputs.

#### 1.1.3 Problem Solution

CoT addresses this issue by introducing self-consistency checks within the conceptual graph, ensuring that every concept in the generated text is internally consistent, thereby enhancing text quality.

#### 1.1.4 Boundaries and Extensions

CoT is not only applicable to NLP but can also be extended to other scenarios requiring concept consistency, such as knowledge graph construction and question-answering systems.

#### 1.1.5 Core Concept Structure

- **Concept**: Every noun or phrase mentioned in the text.
- **Conceptual Graph**: A graphical structure representing the concepts and their interrelationships.
- **Self-Consistency**: The absence of logical contradictions among the concepts in the conceptual graph.

#### 1.1.6 Core Components of CoT

- **Concept Extraction**: Extract all concepts from the text.
- **Relationship Construction**: Build interrelationships among the concepts.
- **Consistency Check**: Check the self-consistency of the conceptual graph.
- **Text Generation**: Generate high-quality text using the self-consistent conceptual graph.

### Chapter 2: Core Concepts and Relationships of CoT

#### 2.1.1 Core Concepts of CoT

- **Entity Concept**: Specific objects or entities, such as "apple," "car."
- **Attribute Concept**: Describes the characteristics of entities, such as "red," "fast."
- **Relationship Concept**: Describes the relationships between entities, such as "is," "belongs to."

### Chapter 4: Mathematical Model and Formulas of CoT

#### 4.1.1 Mathematical Model of CoT

The mathematical model of CoT can be represented as:

$$
\text{CoT} = f(\text{Text}, \text{Conceptual Graph}, \text{Consistency Function})
$$

Where $f$ is a composite function, $f(\cdot)$ represents processing the text to generate self-consistent text.

#### 4.1.2 Detailed Explanation

- **Text**: The original input text.
- **Conceptual Graph**: The graphical structure representing all concepts and their interrelationships.
- **Consistency Function**: A function used to check whether the conceptual graph is self-consistent.

#### 4.1.3 Example

Suppose we have a text: "Apples are red and sweet." Through the CoT algorithm, we can extract the concepts "apple," "red," and "sweet," and construct a conceptual graph. Then, through the consistency function, we can determine that "apple" is both "red" and "sweet," which is a self-consistent conceptual graph.
