### Step 2: Defining Core Concepts and Terms

#### Core Concepts and Terminology

- **Urban Planning**: The process of shaping the development and management of urban areas, including land use, transportation, public utilities, and community services.
- **Livability**: The quality of urban life, encompassing accessibility, safety, health, social interaction, and environmental quality.
- **Functionality**: The efficiency and purposefulness of urban systems, including infrastructure, economic activities, and public services.

#### Problem Background

- **Post-Industrial Era**: A phase characterized by the decline of manufacturing industries and the rise of services and knowledge-based economies.
- **Urbanization**: The rapid growth of urban areas, leading to increased density, diversity, and complexity in urban environments.
- **Challenges**: Managing urban sprawl, promoting sustainable development, ensuring equitable access to resources, and enhancing the quality of life.

### Step 3: Analyzing Urban Livability and Functionality

#### Principles of Urban Livability

- **Accessibility**: Ensuring easy movement within and between urban areas for all residents.
- **Safety**: Providing a secure environment free from crime and hazards.
- **Health**: Ensuring access to clean air, water, and nutritious food.
- **Social Interaction**: Encouraging community engagement and social cohesion.
- **Environmental Quality**: Maintaining green spaces, reducing pollution, and preserving natural resources.

#### Principles of Urban Functionality

- **Economic Efficiency**: Supporting diverse economic activities and fostering job growth.
- **Public Services**: Delivering efficient and reliable services such as transportation, healthcare, and education.
- **Infrastructure**: Developing robust and adaptable urban infrastructure.
- **Technological Integration**: Leveraging technology to enhance urban systems and services.

#### Relationship Between Livability and Functionality

- **Synergy**: When urban areas are designed to support both livability and functionality, they can create a positive feedback loop, enhancing the overall quality of life and economic prosperity.

- **Effect**: Increased commute times, environmental degradation, and loss of open spaces.

- **Challenge**: Balancing economic growth with environmental protection and social equity.
- **Strategy**: Implementing sustainable urban planning practices, such as green building, renewable energy, and efficient resource management.

#### Equitable Access

- **Challenge**: Ensuring all residents have equal access to resources and opportunities.
- **Strategy**: Addressing social disparities through inclusive urban policies and investments.
