#### 1.2 Key Technologies of 6G

##### Millimeter-Wave Communications

Millimeter-wave communications (mmWave) are a critical technology for 6G. They operate in the frequency range from 24 to 300 GHz, providing much higher bandwidth than traditional cellular frequencies.

```mermaid
graph TD
A[Millimeter-Wave Communications] --> B[High Bandwidth]
A --> C[Low Latency]
A --> D[Multitude of Connections]
B --> E["> 1 Tbps per cell"]
C --> F["< 1 ms latency"]
D --> G["> 100 billion connections"]
```

The benefits of mmWave communications include high data rates, reduced latency, and the potential to support a massive number of simultaneous connections. However, they also pose challenges such as signal attenuation and limited coverage.

##### Massive MIMO

Massive MIMO (multiple-input multiple-output) is another cornerstone of 6G technology. It leverages a large number of antennas at both the transmitter and receiver to improve efficiency and capacity.

$$
\text{Capacity} = \sum_{i=1}^{N_t} \sum_{j=1}^{N_r} \text{log}_2(1 + \text{SNR}_{ij})
$$

where \( N_t \) is the number of transmit antennas, \( N_r \) is the number of receive antennas, and \( \text{SNR}_{ij} \) is the signal-to-noise ratio for the \( i \)-th transmit and \( j \)-th receive antenna pair.

```mermaid
graph TD
A[Massive MIMO] --> B[Increased Capacity]
A --> C[Improved Efficiency]
A --> D[Enhanced Reliability]
B --> E["Higher throughputs"]
C --> F["Energy savings"]
D --> G["Better coverage"]
```

In summary, mmWave communications and Massive MIMO are fundamental to the capabilities of 6G, offering significant improvements in bandwidth, latency, and connectivity. However, their successful deployment requires overcoming technical challenges and ensuring robust system design.
