### 1. Introduction to Zero-Shot Learning

Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) is a machine learning paradigm that allows models to classify unseen classes without any prior exposure. This is particularly useful in domains where data is scarce or impossible to obtain, such as climate science. ZSL leverages prior knowledge and generalization to make predictions for new classes.

**Background**:
- **Traditional Machine Learning**: Traditional machine learning models require labeled data for each class to perform well. This is a limitation in fields like climate science, where labeled data can be difficult or impossible to obtain.
- **Zero-Shot Learning**: ZSL addresses this limitation by generalizing from a set of known classes to new, unseen classes. This is achieved by learning class relationships and using these to predict the properties of new classes.

**Core Concepts**:
- **Class Relationships**: ZSL models learn the relationships between known and unknown classes to infer properties of the latter.
- **Knowledge Transfer**: ZSL leverages knowledge transfer techniques to utilize information from known classes to improve predictions for new classes.

**Applications**:
- **Climate Science**: ZSL can be applied to climate prediction tasks where the vast amount of data is primarily in numerical form, making it challenging to obtain labeled examples for all possible climate conditions.

### 2. Introduction to AI Global Climate Models

AI-driven global climate models are essential tools for understanding and predicting Earth's climate. These models integrate various scientific disciplines to simulate the complex interactions between atmospheric, oceanic, and terrestrial processes.

**Background**:
- **Climate Models**: Climate models are sophisticated computer programs that simulate the Earth's climate system. They are used to study past climates, understand current climate dynamics, and project future climate changes.
- **AI in Climate Science**: AI techniques, including ZSL, are increasingly being integrated into climate models to enhance their predictive capabilities and address the limitations of traditional modeling approaches.

**Core Concepts**:
- **Model Types**: Climate models can be classified into global climate models (GCMs), regional climate models (RCMs), and downscaling models.
- **Challenges**: The challenges in climate modeling include the complexity of the Earth's climate system, the need for high-resolution data, and the computational costs associated with simulations.

**Applications**:
- **Climate Prediction**: AI-enhanced climate models are used to predict future climate conditions, identify climate risks, and inform climate policies.
