### Step 2: Background of Space Exploration and Robotics Technology

**Space Exploration Overview**

**Space Exploration Development History:**
The journey of space exploration began with the launch of Sputnik by the Soviet Union in 1957. This was followed by the moon landing by NASA's Apollo program in 1969, and the subsequent missions that have explored the moon, Mars, and other celestial bodies. The goals of space exploration have evolved over the years, ranging from scientific research to the search for extraterrestrial life and the establishment of human colonies in space.

**Goals and Challenges:**
The primary goals of space exploration include understanding the origins of the universe, studying the effects of space on humans, and identifying potential resources for future space colonization. The challenges include the harsh environment of space, the high cost of space missions, and the technical complexity of operating in an uncontrolled environment.

**Robotics Technology Overview**

**Robotics Definition:**
Robotics is the field of study and application of technology that deals with the design, construction, and use of robots. Robots are automated machines that can perform tasks in place of or as companions to humans.

**Types of Robotics:**
There are several types of robotics, including industrial robotics, service robotics, and space robotics. Space robotics specifically deals with robots designed to operate in the space environment, which presents unique challenges such as vacuum, radiation, and temperature extremes.

**Robotics Technology Development:**
Robotics technology has seen significant advancements over the years, with key milestones including the development of the first industrial robot in the 1960s, the deployment of robotic rovers on Mars in the 2000s, and the use of autonomous drones in space missions.

**Autonomous Applications Concept and Principles**

**Autonomous Application Definition:**
Autonomous applications refer to systems that can operate independently without human intervention. In the context of space exploration, this means robots that can navigate, make decisions, and perform tasks on their own.

**Key Technologies:**
The development of autonomous applications for space exploration relies on several key technologies, including artificial intelligence, machine learning, computer vision, and advanced sensors.

**Application Prospects:**
The prospects for autonomous applications in space exploration are promising. These systems can significantly reduce the need for human intervention, improve mission efficiency, and enable exploration of more challenging environments.

**Example Explanation**

**Example: PID Control Algorithm**
Imagine a robot navigating through a tunnel. The goal is to maintain a constant speed. The PID control algorithm will calculate the error between the desired speed and the actual speed, and adjust the robot's actions to minimize this error.

- **Initial Setup**: The robot has a setpoint speed of 1 m/s, and the actual speed is initially 0.8 m/s.
- **Error Calculation**: The error is the difference between the setpoint speed and the actual speed.
  $$ e(t) = 1 - 0.8 = 0.2 $$
- **Proportional Component**: The proportional component adjusts the action based on the current error.
  $$ K_p \cdot e(t) = 0.5 \cdot 0.2 = 0.1 $$
- **Integral Component**: The integral component accumulates the error over time to ensure long-term stability.
  $$ K_i \cdot \int_{0}^{t} e(\tau)d\tau = 0.2 \cdot \int_{0}^{t} e(\tau)d\tau $$
- **Derivative Component**: The derivative component adjusts the action based on the rate of change of the error.
  $$ K_d \cdot e'(t) = 0.1 \cdot e'(t) $$
- **Final Control Signal**: The final control signal combines the proportional, integral, and derivative components to adjust the robot's actions.
  $$ \text{控制信号} = 0.1 + 0.2 \cdot \int_{0}^{t} e(\tau)d\tau + 0.1 \cdot e'(t) $$

By continuously updating the control signal based on the current error and its rate of change, the robot can maintain a constant speed despite any disturbances or changes in the environment.

**Navigation Module**
The navigation module uses simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) algorithms to enable the robot to build a map of the environment while simultaneously keeping track of its own position. The SLAM module is implemented using the `SLAM++` library in Python.

```python
from slampp import SLAMPP
slam = SLAMPP()
slam.initialize()
while not slam.is_done():
    slam.step()
slam.save_map("map_data.pgm")
```

**Sampling Module**
The sampling module includes a robotic arm equipped with a sampling tool. The arm's motion is controlled using a PID control algorithm to ensure precise positioning and operation.

```python
import control

# Define PID parameters
Kp = 1.0
Ki = 0.1
Kd = 0.05

# Create PID controller
controller = control.PID(Kp, Ki, Kd)

# Set initial position
current_position = 0.0

# Define target position
target_position = 1.0

# Control loop
while abs(current_position - target_position) > 0.01:
    error = target_position - current_position
    control_signal = controller.update(error)
    current_position += control_signal
```
