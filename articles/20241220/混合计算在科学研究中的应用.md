**1.1 Definition and Characteristics of Mixed-Computing**

Mixed-computing is an advanced computing paradigm that combines the strengths of various computing models, such as cloud computing, edge computing, and distributed computing, to provide a more efficient and flexible solution for complex scientific research tasks. The core characteristics of mixed-computing include:

1. **Scalability**: Mixed-computing systems can easily scale up or down to meet the changing demands of scientific research, providing a high degree of flexibility.
2. **Efficiency**: By leveraging the advantages of different computing models, mixed-computing can achieve higher efficiency in processing large amounts of scientific data.
3. **Reliability**: Mixed-computing systems are designed to be highly reliable, ensuring that scientific research tasks can be completed without interruptions or failures.

**3.2 Mathematical Models and Formulas**

The mathematical models and formulas used in mixed-computing algorithms are essential for understanding their principles and applications. Here are some key models and formulas:

1. **Linear Regression**: Linear regression is a commonly used algorithm in mixed-computing for predicting numerical values. The mathematical model is given by:
   $$ y = \beta_0 + \beta_1x $$
   where $y$ is the predicted value, $\beta_0$ is the intercept, and $\beta_1$ is the slope.

2. **Logistic Regression**: Logistic regression is used for binary classification tasks. The mathematical model is given by:
   $$ P(y=1) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\beta_0 + \beta_1x)}} $$
   where $P(y=1)$ is the probability of the output being 1, and $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ are the model parameters.

3. **Support Vector Machines (SVM)**: SVMs are used for both classification and regression tasks. The mathematical model is given by:
   $$ w \cdot x - b = 0 $$
   where $w$ is the weight vector, $x$ is the feature vector, and $b$ is the bias term.

4. **Neural Networks**: Neural networks are used for complex pattern recognition and prediction tasks. The mathematical model is given by:
   $$ a_{i}^{(l)} = \sigma(z_{i}^{(l)}) $$
   where $a_{i}^{(l)}$ is the activation of the $i$-th neuron in the $l$-th layer, $\sigma$ is the activation function, and $z_{i}^{(l)}$ is the weighted sum of the inputs.
