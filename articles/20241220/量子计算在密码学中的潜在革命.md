## Introduction to Quantum Computing and Cryptography

### 1.1 Core Concepts of Quantum Computing

#### 1.1.1 Quantum Bits (qubits) vs Classical Bits

Quantum bits, or qubits, are the basic units of quantum information, which unlike classical bits, can exist in a superposition of states. This property allows quantum computers to perform certain calculations exponentially faster than classical computers.

#### 1.1.2 Quantum Superposition and Entanglement

Quantum superposition means a qubit can be in multiple states at once, while classical bits can only be in one state at a time. Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon where two or more qubits become interconnected such that the state of one qubit cannot be described independently of the state of the others.

#### 1.1.3 Quantum Gates and Basic Quantum Algorithms

Quantum gates are the building blocks of quantum circuits, similar to logic gates in classical computing. Quantum algorithms leverage these gates to perform complex computations efficiently.

### 1.2 Fundamental Principles of Quantum Cryptography

#### 1.2.1 Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)

Quantum Key Distribution is a method of sharing encryption keys between two parties in a way that is theoretically secure against eavesdropping.

#### 1.2.2 Quantum Encryption Algorithms

Quantum encryption algorithms leverage the principles of quantum mechanics to create cryptographic systems that are resistant to attacks from quantum computers.

#### 1.2.3 Quantum-Secure Protocols

Quantum-secure protocols are designed to ensure the security of communication systems in the presence of quantum computers.
