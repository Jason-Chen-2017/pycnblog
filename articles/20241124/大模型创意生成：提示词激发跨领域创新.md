### Step 3: Explain Mathematical Models and Formulas

Now, let's explore the mathematical models and formulas used in large model creative generation. We will provide detailed explanations with examples to illustrate their application.

#### Mathematical Models:

1. **Embeddings**: These are dense vectors that represent words or phrases in a high-dimensional space. They are often learned from large amounts of text data using techniques like Word2Vec or BERT.
   
   Example: Given a word "cat", its embedding vector might be represented as:
   $$ \text{cat\_embedding} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.1 & 0.2 & -0.3 & 0.4 \end{bmatrix} $$

2. **Neural Networks**: These are composed of layers of interconnected nodes (neurons) that transform input data through a series of mathematical operations.

   Example: A simple neural network with two input nodes, one hidden layer with three nodes, and one output node:
   $$ z_1 = x_1 \cdot w_1 + b_1 $$
   $$ z_2 = x_2 \cdot w_2 + b_2 $$
   $$ z_3 = f(z_1, z_2) $$
   $$ y = z_3 \cdot w_3 + b_3 $$
   where $x_1, x_2$ are input values, $w_1, w_2, w_3$ are weights, $b_1, b_2, b_3$ are biases, and $f$ is the activation function (e.g., ReLU).

3. **Probability Distributions**: These are used to model uncertainty and to sample from possible outcomes.

   Example: A Bernoulli distribution used to model the probability of generating a particular word:
   $$ P(\text{word}) = \sigma(w \cdot \text{word\_embedding} + b) $$
   where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function, $w$ is the weight vector, $\text{word\_embedding}$ is the embedding of the word, and $b$ is the bias.

#### Detailed Explanation and Examples:

1. **Embeddings**: Weights and biases are initialized randomly. As the model is trained on text data, the embeddings are updated to reflect the context and relationships between words. For instance, the word "dog" might become more similar to the word "cat" if they frequently appear in similar contexts.

2. **Neural Networks**: The network is trained to minimize the difference between the predicted probabilities and the actual next words in the text. This is typically done using backpropagation and optimization algorithms like gradient descent.

3. **Probability Distributions**: The output layer of the neural network generates a probability distribution over possible next words. By sampling from this distribution, the model can generate a sequence of words with coherent and innovative content.

In summary, the mathematical models and formulas are critical for enabling large models to generate creative content. They provide the foundation for encoding language, processing input, and generating outputs that are both coherent and innovative.
