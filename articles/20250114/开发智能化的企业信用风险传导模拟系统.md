### 3.3 Mathematical Model and Formula Explanation

The core of our risk assessment model is built on statistical analysis and machine learning algorithms. Let's explain the mathematical model and its key components.

#### 3.3.1 Statistical Measures

To assess the credit risk, we use the following statistical measures:

1. **Debt to Income Ratio (DIR)**: The ratio of a company's total debt to its total income.
   $$ \text{DIR} = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Total Income}} $$

2. **Credit Score (CS)**: A numerical representation of a company's creditworthiness.
   $$ \text{CS} = \frac{\text{Total Points}}{\text{Total Items}} $$

3. **Revenue Growth Rate (RGR)**: The rate at which a company's revenue is growing.
   $$ \text{RGR} = \frac{\text{Current Revenue} - \text{Previous Revenue}}{\text{Previous Revenue}} $$

#### 3.3.2 Machine Learning Model

Our risk assessment model is a Random Forest Classifier, which is an ensemble learning method. The core formula of the Random Forest Classifier is:

$$ \hat{y} = \text{majority\_vote}(\text{classification\_results}) $$

Where \( \hat{y} \) is the predicted class label, and \( \text{classification\_results} \) are the individual classification results from each decision tree in the forest.

#### 3.3.3 Risk Transmission Simulation

The risk transmission simulation involves predicting the risk of default for each entity in the financial network. The simulation formula is:

$$ \text{Risk} = f(\text{Features}, \text{Model Parameters}) $$

Where \( f \) is the risk assessment function that combines the extracted features and the model parameters to predict the risk of default.
