## Introduction

### 1.1 Background and Definition of Homomorphic Encryption

Homomorphic encryption, a term coined by Rivest, Shamir, and Adleman in 1978, is a cryptographic technique that enables computations to be executed on encrypted data, thereby preserving the confidentiality and integrity of the data. Traditional encryption methods require data to be decrypted before any computation can be performed, but homomorphic encryption allows computations to be executed directly on the encrypted data, thus maintaining data privacy even in the presence of unauthorized parties.

### 1.2 Importance of Homomorphic Encryption

The significance of homomorphic encryption lies in its ability to address several critical challenges in modern computing. One of the primary challenges is the need to protect sensitive data while enabling third-party service providers, such as cloud computing platforms, to perform computations on behalf of users. Homomorphic encryption ensures that data remains encrypted and secure throughout the computation process, thus preventing unauthorized access and data breaches.

### 1.3 Application Domains of Homomorphic Encryption

Homomorphic encryption has a wide range of applications in various domains, including cloud computing, healthcare, finance, and data analytics. In cloud computing, homomorphic encryption enables secure data processing and analysis without compromising data privacy. In healthcare, it ensures that sensitive patient data remains confidential while allowing healthcare providers to analyze and derive insights from the data. In finance, it protects sensitive financial information while enabling complex calculations for risk analysis and fraud detection.

Homomorphic encryption relies on specific algebraic structures that enable the encryption and decryption of data while preserving the original computation operations. The primary algebraic structures used in homomorphic encryption are groups, rings, and fields.

### 1.4.2 Modular Arithmetic

Modular arithmetic is a system of arithmetic for integers, where numbers "wrap around" after reaching a certain value, known as the modulus. It is a fundamental concept in cryptography and plays a crucial role in homomorphic encryption schemes.

### 1.4.3 Lagrange's Multiplier Method

Lagrange's multiplier method is a mathematical optimization technique used to find the maximum or minimum of a function subject to certain constraints. It is an essential tool in the construction and analysis of homomorphic encryption schemes.
