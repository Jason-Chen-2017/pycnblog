#### 1.1 Problem Background

Rare diseases, by definition, affect a very small percentage of the population. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), a rare disease is one that affects no more than 1 in 2,000 people. These diseases vary widely in their nature and symptoms, making their diagnosis a complex and often challenging task. Unlike common diseases, where extensive clinical research and established diagnostic criteria are available, rare diseases often lack sufficient research funding, standardized diagnostic tools, and specialized treatment protocols. This lack of resources leads to delayed or incorrect diagnoses, which in turn can severely impact patient outcomes.

Currently, the diagnostic process for rare diseases is fraught with several challenges. Firstly, the rarity of these conditions means that healthcare professionals, including doctors and researchers, may not have extensive experience in identifying or treating them. This lack of familiarity can lead to misdiagnoses or delayed diagnoses. Secondly, the clinical presentation of rare diseases can often overlap with symptoms of more common conditions, further complicating the diagnostic journey. Thirdly, the lack of specialized diagnostic tests and biomarkers for many rare diseases means that doctors must rely heavily on a process of elimination, which can be time-consuming and invasive.

The consequences of misdiagnosing or failing to diagnose a rare disease can be profound. Patients may undergo unnecessary treatments, experience significant pain and suffering, and in some cases, may even die from the disease due to a lack of timely intervention. Moreover, the financial burden of managing a rare disease without an accurate diagnosis can be immense, both for the patient and the healthcare system.

In summary, the current diagnostic landscape for rare diseases is characterized by significant challenges, including the lack of expertise among healthcare professionals, the overlapping symptoms with common diseases, and the scarcity of specialized diagnostic tools. Addressing these challenges requires innovative approaches that can improve the accuracy and efficiency of diagnosis, ultimately leading to better patient outcomes.

### 2. Core Concepts and Principles

#### 2.1 Definition of Zero-Shot CoT

Zero-Shot CoT (Zero-Shot Conceptual Zero-shot Transfer) is an advanced technique in artificial intelligence (AI) that enables machines to recognize and classify concepts or objects without prior exposure to specific examples. Unlike traditional machine learning approaches that require extensive training data to achieve high accuracy, Zero-Shot CoT leverages a deep understanding of the underlying concepts and their relationships, allowing for generalization to new, unseen categories.

At its core, Zero-Shot CoT is based on the principle of "transferring knowledge" from one domain to another. This is achieved by training an AI model on a large, diverse dataset from a source domain, which then captures the intrinsic relationships between different concepts. When a new, target domain is introduced for which there is limited or no training data, the model can leverage this learned knowledge to classify and recognize the concepts in the target domain with high accuracy.

The key components of Zero-Shot CoT include:

1. **Conceptual Embeddings:** These are high-dimensional vectors that represent the underlying concepts in the dataset. The embeddings capture the semantic relationships between concepts, allowing the AI model to understand and generalize from these relationships.
2. **Knowledge Transfer:** The process of transferring knowledge from the source domain to the target domain. This involves mapping the conceptual embeddings from the source domain to the target domain, enabling the AI model to apply the learned knowledge effectively.
3. **Metric Learning:** Techniques used to measure the similarity or distance between different concepts. Metric learning is crucial for accurately classifying new concepts in the target domain by comparing them to the learned embeddings.

The mathematical models and formulas underpinning the Zero-Shot CoT algorithm play a crucial role in its effectiveness in rare disease diagnosis. The following are key mathematical models and their corresponding formulas, presented using LaTeX for clarity and precision:

**1. Conceptual Embedding Generation:**

     where $\textbf{e}_i$ represents the embedding vector for the $i$-th entity in the dataset, and $\textit{vocabulary}$ is the set of unique entities.
   - **BERT Embedding:**
     $$ \textbf{e}_i = \text{BERT}(\textit{input_sequence}) $$
     where $\textit{input_sequence}$ is the sequence of tokens representing the $i$-th entity, and $\textbf{e}_i$ is the corresponding embedding vector.

**2. Knowledge Transfer:**
   - **Embedding Mapping:**
     $$ \textbf{e}'_i = \text{Mapping}(\textbf{e}_i, \textit{source_embeddings}) $$
     where $\textbf{e}'_i$ is the mapped embedding vector for the $i$-th entity in the target domain, $\textbf{e}_i$ is the original embedding vector, and $\textit{source_embeddings}$ are the embedding vectors from the source domain.
   - **Procrustes Analysis:**
     $$ \textbf{e}'_i = \text{Procrustes}(\textbf{e}_i, \textit{source_embeddings}) $$
     where $\text{Procrustes}$ is a statistical technique used to align embedding vectors by optimizing the rotation, translation, and scaling of the target embeddings to minimize the Euclidean distance between them and the source embeddings.

**3. Model Training and Evaluation:**
   - **Neural Network Training:**
     $$ \text{Loss} = \text{Cross-Entropy}(\textit{true_labels}, \textit{predicted_labels}) $$
     where $\textit{true_labels}$ are the ground truth labels for the target domain data, and $\textit{predicted_labels}$ are the predicted labels by the neural network model.
   - **Model Evaluation:**
     $$ \text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{Correct Predictions}}{\text{Total Predictions}} $$
     $$ \text{Precision} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{True Positives + False Positives}} $$
     $$ \text{Recall} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{True Positives + False Negatives}} $$
     $$ \text{F1-Score} = \frac{2 \times \text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}} $$
