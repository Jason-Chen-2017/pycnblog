## Zero-Shot Reasoning Overview

### 2.1 Definition of Zero-Shot Reasoning

Zero-Shot Reasoning (ZSR) is an AI technique that allows a model to perform inference on classes it has not seen during training. This is achieved by leveraging a set of "zero-shot learning" principles, which enable the model to generalize from a small set of examples or even no examples at all. ZSR is particularly useful in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or unavailable.

### 2.2 Application Scenarios

ZSR has numerous applications across various domains:

- **Healthcare**: Diagnosing rare diseases by leveraging existing medical knowledge and inference techniques.
- **Autonomous Driving**: Improving the robustness of AI systems by enabling them to generalize to new and unseen driving scenarios.
- **Robotics**: Enabling robots to interact with environments they have not encountered before.
- **Natural Language Processing**: Handling out-of-vocabulary words and phrases in text processing tasks.
- **Computer Vision**: Identifying objects and scenarios that are not present in the training data.

### 2.3 Development History

The concept of Zero-Shot Reasoning has been evolving over the past few decades. Early approaches focused on utilizing symbolic AI techniques, such as knowledge graphs and inference rules. More recent advancements have leveraged machine learning techniques, particularly unsupervised and self-supervised learning. The development of ZSR has been driven by the increasing need for AI systems to generalize from limited data or even no data at all, making it a critical area of research in AI.
