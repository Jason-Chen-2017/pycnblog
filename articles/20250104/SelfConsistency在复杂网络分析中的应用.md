### Part 1: Fundamentals of Self-Consistency

#### Chapter 1: Basic Concepts of Self-Consistency

**1.1.1 Definition of Self-Consistency**

Self-consistency, in a broad sense, refers to the property of a system or model where its internal states and behaviors are consistent with each other and with the external conditions or constraints. In the context of complex network analysis, self-consistency ensures that the network structure, properties, and behaviors are coherent and do not lead to contradictions or inconsistencies.

**1.1.2 Importance of Self-Consistency**

Self-consistency is crucial in complex network analysis for several reasons. Firstly, it ensures the reliability and accuracy of the models and algorithms used. Inconsistencies in the models can lead to incorrect conclusions and predictions. Secondly, self-consistency helps in identifying the key factors and mechanisms driving the network dynamics, enabling better understanding and control of the network behavior.

**1.1.3 Self-Consistency vs. Consistency**

While self-consistency and consistency are related concepts, they have distinct meanings. Consistency refers to the property of a system or model where its predictions or outcomes are consistent with each other and with the known facts or observations. In contrast, self-consistency focuses on the internal coherence of the system or model, ensuring that its internal states and behaviors are consistent with each other and with the external conditions or constraints.

#### Chapter 2: Principles and Characteristics of Self-Consistency

**2.1.1 Principles of Self-Consistency**

The principles of self-consistency are rooted in the fundamental laws of physics and mathematics. In physics, self-consistency is often associated with the principles of causality and determinism, which state that the behavior of a system is consistent with its initial conditions and the laws of nature. In mathematics, self-consistency is often related to the properties of equilibrium and stability, which ensure that the solutions of mathematical models are consistent with their own constraints and conditions.

**2.1.2 Characteristics of Self-Consistency**

The characteristics of self-consistency can be summarized as follows:

1. **Stability**: A self-consistent system or model exhibits stability, meaning that small perturbations in its internal states or external conditions do not lead to significant changes in its overall behavior.
2. **Flexibility**: Self-consistent systems or models can adapt to changes in their external conditions or internal states without losing their coherence or consistency.
3. **Coherence**: Self-consistent systems or models exhibit internal coherence, meaning that their internal states and behaviors are consistent with each other and with the external conditions or constraints.
4. **Accuracy**: Self-consistent systems or models provide accurate predictions and conclusions, as they satisfy their own constraints and conditions.
