## 1.1 量子计算机概述

### 1.1.1 量子计算机的起源  
Quantum computing originated in the early 20th century with pioneers like Paul Dirac and Alan Turing. The concept of quantum bits (qubits) was first introduced by Richard Feynman in 1982, leading to the development of quantum computing as a field in the 1990s.

### 1.1.2 量子计算机的基本原理  
Quantum computers use qubits, which can exist in superposition states, unlike classical bits. Qubits are manipulated using quantum gates, forming quantum circuits that perform computations based on quantum mechanics principles.

### 1.1.3 量子计算机与传统计算机的比较  
While classical computers use bits (0 or 1), quantum computers use qubits, enabling parallel processing and solving certain problems exponentially faster. However, quantum computers are still in the early stages of development and face challenges like decoherence and error correction.

## 1.2 自洽性原理

### 1.2.1 自洽性的基本概念  
Self-consistency refers to the consistency of a system's state with itself over time. In quantum computing, it ensures that the system's evolution aligns with its initial conditions.

### 1.2.2 自洽性在量子力学中的应用  
In quantum mechanics, self-consistency is used in models like the Hartree-Fock method, ensuring that the wavefunction evolves consistently with the system's interactions.

### 1.2.3 自洽性与量子计算机的关系  
Self-consistency is crucial for maintaining the integrity of quantum computations. It ensures that the output of a quantum algorithm aligns with the input and the system's state remains consistent.
