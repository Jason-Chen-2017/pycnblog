#### 1.2 Background

**The Evolution of Recommendation Systems**

Recommendation systems have evolved significantly over the past few decades. Initially, these systems relied on basic rule-based approaches that used predefined rules to generate recommendations. These early systems, while effective in some scenarios, were limited in their ability to handle large datasets and provide personalized recommendations.

With the advent of machine learning and data mining techniques, more sophisticated algorithms such as collaborative filtering and content-based filtering emerged. Collaborative filtering leverages the collective behavior of users to predict their preferences, while content-based filtering recommends items based on the semantic similarity of the items and the user's profile. These approaches have been widely adopted due to their effectiveness in providing personalized recommendations.

**The Role of Self-Consistency in Recommendation Systems**

Despite their success, traditional recommendation systems often face challenges related to data consistency. Users' preferences can change over time, and the system must adapt to these changes to maintain its relevance. Moreover, the system's internal state, such as the popularity of items or the availability of new content, can also influence the quality of recommendations.

Self-consistency addresses these challenges by ensuring that the recommendations are consistent with both the users' evolving preferences and the system's current state. This approach involves continuously updating the system's internal models and adjusting the recommendation algorithms to reflect the latest user behavior and system data. By maintaining data consistency, the self-consistency method enhances the accuracy and relevance of recommendations, leading to improved user satisfaction and system performance.

#### 1.3 Problem Statement

**The Limitations of Traditional Recommendation Systems**

Traditional recommendation systems, while effective in many cases, suffer from several limitations:

1. **Data Inconsistency:** Users' preferences can change rapidly, and traditional systems may not adapt quickly enough to these changes, leading to outdated recommendations.
2. **User Experience:** Users often encounter recommendations that do not align with their current interests or needs, resulting in frustration and a poor user experience.
3. **System Performance:** Traditional systems may struggle to handle large datasets and complex user interactions, leading to suboptimal performance and slower response times.

**The Necessity of Self-Consistency**

To overcome these limitations, there is a growing need for more adaptive and consistent recommendation systems. The self-consistency method addresses these challenges by ensuring that the recommendations are aligned with both the users' current preferences and the system's current state. This approach not only improves user satisfaction but also enhances the overall performance of the recommendation system.
