## Self-Consistency Contextual Theory (CoT) Overview

### 1.1 Problem Background and Definition

The problem of ensuring consistency in automated news writing arises from the complexity of the news content and the diverse sources from which the information is extracted. News reports often involve multiple interrelated facts, events, and opinions, making it challenging for automated systems to generate coherent and accurate content.

Self-Consistency Contextual Theory (CoT) is a framework that aims to address this challenge by ensuring that the output of an automated news writing system is internally consistent and coherent. The theory is based on the principle that a system should maintain consistency across all the data it processes and generate reports that are logically coherent.

### 1.2 Core Concepts and Elements

The core concepts of CoT include:

- **Contextual Information**: This refers to the background information that provides the context for a news report. It includes facts, events, and opinions related to the news topic.
- **Consistency Constraints**: These are the rules that ensure the consistency of the information within the report. For example, if a news report states that a person is living in a city, it should not later state that the person is deceased.
- **Coherence Constraints**: These are the rules that ensure the logical flow and coherence of the information in the report. For example, the report should not abruptly switch topics or present conflicting information.

### 1.3 Application Areas of CoT

While CoT was initially developed for automated news writing, its applications extend beyond this domain. It can be applied to other forms of automated content generation, such as generating summaries of articles, creating product descriptions, or even in natural language processing tasks like machine translation and question answering.

In summary, Self-Consistency Contextual Theory (CoT) is a framework designed to ensure the consistency and coherence of automated news writing systems. By maintaining internal consistency and coherence, CoT helps to improve the accuracy and credibility of the generated content.

## Self-Consistency Contextual Theory (CoT) Principles

### 2.1 Basic Principles

The basic principles of Self-Consistency Contextual Theory (CoT) revolve around the concept of maintaining consistency and coherence in the generated content. The theory is grounded in the following principles:

- **Data Consistency**: The system should ensure that the data it processes is consistent across all its sources. This involves checking for contradictions and resolving them to ensure that the information is accurate and reliable.
- **Contextual Consistency**: The system should maintain the consistency of the contextual information throughout the report. This means that the context provided at the beginning of the report should be maintained and referred to consistently throughout.
- **Logical Coherence**: The system should generate reports that are logically coherent. This involves ensuring that the information flows smoothly from one point to another and that there are no abrupt changes or contradictions.

### 2.2 Mathematical Model

The mathematical model of Self-Consistency Contextual Theory (CoT) is based on a set of constraints that ensure the consistency and coherence of the generated content. The model can be represented as follows:

$$
C = f(D, E, L)
$$

Where:

- \(C\) represents the consistency of the content.
- \(D\) represents the data processed by the system.
- \(E\) represents the contextual information.
- \(L\) represents the logical flow of the content.

The function \(f\) embodies the rules and algorithms that ensure the consistency and coherence of the content.

### 2.3 Algorithmic Principles

The algorithmic principles of CoT are based on the concept of context-aware consistency checks. Then, it applies a set of consistency checks to the draft to identify and resolve any inconsistencies or logical gaps.
