### Introduction and Background

#### 1.1 Introduction to the New Stoicism

Stoicism is an ancient philosophical system that originated in Athens around the 3rd century BCE. Developed by figures like Zeno, Epictetus, and Marcus Aurelius, Stoicism teaches that emotions are responses to judgments, and by controlling our judgments, we can control our emotions. The core principles of Stoicism include focusing on what is within our control, practicing virtuous living, and maintaining a rational perspective.

#### 1.1.1 Historical Background of Stoicism

Stoicism was initially associated with the Greek philosophical school of the Stoics, which emphasized the importance of reason and logic. Over time, Stoic principles were adopted by various cultures, including the Roman Empire, where figures like Seneca and Epictetus played significant roles. Stoicism has seen a resurgence in recent years, particularly in the fields of psychology and personal development.

#### 1.1.2 Principles and Philosophy of Stoicism

The main principles of Stoicism can be summarized as follows:

1. **Virtue**: Stoicism teaches that the pursuit of virtue is the ultimate goal in life. Virtue includes qualities like wisdom, courage, justice, self-control, and temperance.
2. **Cognitive Distortions**: Stoicism recognizes that our emotions are often the result of cognitive distortions—misinterpretations of events and situations. By recognizing and correcting these distortions, we can reduce our emotional suffering.
3. **Focus on the Present**: Stoicism emphasizes the importance of living in the present moment, rather than being consumed by past regrets or future anxieties.
4. **Acceptance**: Stoicism teaches that we should accept the things we cannot change and strive to change the things we can.

#### 1.1.3 The Relevance of Stoicism in Modern Society

In today's fast-paced, stressful world, Stoicism offers valuable insights for managing mental health and fostering resilience. Many people are turning to Stoicism as a way to cope with life's challenges, reduce anxiety, and cultivate a more positive mindset. The New Stoicism Diary App aims to harness the power of Stoic principles and make them accessible to a broader audience through the use of modern technology.
