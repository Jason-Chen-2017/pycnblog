### 2.2 Brand Value Assessment Model

- **Customer Value**: The perceived value of a brand by its customers, based on their experiences and emotions.

**Concept of Brand Value**

- **Brand Value**: Brand value is a multifaceted concept that captures the overall worth of a brand in the market. It includes both tangible and intangible elements, such as the financial value derived from brand-related revenue and the customer value created through brand loyalty and recognition.

**Components of Brand Value**

1. **Financial Value**:
   - **Concept**: The monetary value of a brand, often measured by the premium a brand commands over generic products or its contribution to a company's overall financial performance.
   - **Methods**:
     - **Market-Based Approach**: Estimate brand value by comparing similar brands in the market and analyzing their market capitalization or revenue multiples.
     - **Asset-Based Approach**: Calculate brand value based on the assets owned by the brand, including physical assets, intellectual property, and customer relationships.

2. **Customer Value**:
   - **Concept**: The perceived value of a brand by its customers, based on their experiences, emotions, and expectations.
   - **Methods**:
     - **Customer Surveys**: Collect data through surveys to understand customer perceptions and preferences.
     - **Net Promoter Score (NPS)**: Measure customer loyalty and likelihood to recommend the brand to others.

**2.2.2 Traditional Brand Valuation Models**

**Core Concepts and Terminology**

- **Market-Based Model**: A model that estimates brand value by comparing the market performance of similar brands.
- **Asset-Based Model**: A model that calculates brand value based on the assets associated with the brand.

**Traditional Models**

1. **Market-Based Model**:
   - **Concept**: Estimate brand value by analyzing the market capitalization or revenue multiples of similar brands.
   - **Formula**: Brand Value = Market Capitalization × Revenue Multiple

2. **Asset-Based Model**:
   - **Concept**: Calculate brand value based on the tangible and intangible assets owned by the brand.
   - **Formula**: Brand Value = (Physical Assets + Intellectual Property + Customer Relationships)

**Strengths and Limitations**

1. **Market-Based Model**:
   - **Strengths**:
     - Relatively straightforward and easy to implement.
     - Provides a tangible measure of brand value based on market performance.
   - **Limitations**:
     - Ignores intangible factors such as customer loyalty and brand equity.
     - May be influenced by market volatility and external factors.

2. **Asset-Based Model**:
   - **Strengths**:
     - Accounts for both tangible and intangible assets.
     - Provides a more comprehensive view of brand value.
   - **Limitations**:
     - May overestimate brand value by including assets that are not directly related to the brand.
     - Difficult to measure and value intangible assets accurately.

**2.3.3 Mathematical Models**

**Mathematical Model and Formulation**

1. **Neural Network Model**

   - **Concept**: A neural network is a machine learning model inspired by the human brain's structure and function. It consists of layers of interconnected nodes (neurons) that process input data through weighted connections.

   - **Mathematical Formulation**:
     $$ 
     Z^{(l)} = \sigma(W^{(l)} \cdot A^{(l-1)} + b^{(l)})
     $$
     where $Z^{(l)}$ is the output of the $l$-th layer, $\sigma$ is the activation function (often a sigmoid or ReLU function), $W^{(l)}$ is the weight matrix, $A^{(l-1)}$ is the input to the $l$-th layer, and $b^{(l)}$ is the bias vector.

2. **Regression Analysis**

   - **Concept**: Regression analysis is a statistical method used to model the relationship between a dependent variable and one or more independent variables.

   - **Mathematical Formulation**:
     $$ 
     y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot x_1 + \beta_2 \cdot x_2 + ... + \beta_n \cdot x_n
     $$
     where $y$ is the dependent variable, $x_1, x_2, ..., x_n$ are the independent variables, and $\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2, ..., \beta_n$ are the regression coefficients.

3. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)**

   - **Concept**: PCA is a dimensionality reduction technique that transforms the data into a new coordinate system, where the first few principal components capture the most significant variations in the data.

   - **Mathematical Formulation**:
     $$ 
     Z^{(l)} = U \cdot A^{(l-1)}
     $$
     where $Z^{(l)}$ is the transformed data, $U$ is the matrix of principal components, and $A^{(l-1)}$ is the original data.

**2.4.1 Data Partitioning and Model Training**

- **Model Training**: The process of feeding data into a model to adjust its internal parameters to minimize the difference between predicted outputs and actual outputs.

**Data Partitioning and Model Training**

1. **Data Partitioning**:
   - **Concept**: To ensure a robust evaluation of the model's performance, the data is typically partitioned into three subsets: training, validation, and testing.
   - **Methods**:
     - **Training Set**: Used to train the model and adjust its parameters.
     - **Validation Set**: Used to tune the model's hyperparameters and select the best model configuration.
     - **Testing Set**: Used to evaluate the final model's performance on unseen data, providing an unbiased estimate of its generalization ability.

2. **Model Training**:
   - **Concept**: The training process involves feeding the training set data into the model and iteratively adjusting the model's parameters (weights and biases) to minimize the prediction error.
   - **Methods**:
     - **Gradient Descent**: A common optimization algorithm used to minimize the prediction error by adjusting the model's parameters in the direction of the negative gradient of the loss function.
     - **Backpropagation**: An algorithm used to efficiently compute the gradients required for gradient descent in neural networks.

**2.4.2 Model Evaluation Metrics**

**Core Concepts and Terminology**

- **Evaluation Metrics**: Quantitative measures used to assess the performance of a model.
- **Accuracy**: The proportion of correct predictions out of the total predictions.
- **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**: The average squared difference between the predicted and actual values.
- **R-squared (R²)**: The proportion of the variance in the dependent variable that is predictable from the independent variables.

**Model Evaluation Metrics**

1. **Accuracy**:
   - **Concept**: Accuracy measures how often the model makes correct predictions.
   - **Formula**: 
     $$ 
     Accuracy = \frac{TP + TN}{TP + TN + FP + FN}
     $$
     where TP is true positive, TN is true negative, FP is false positive, and FN is false negative.

2. **Mean Squared Error (MSE)**:
   - **Concept**: MSE measures the average squared difference between the predicted and actual values.
   - **Formula**:
     $$ 
     MSE = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2
     $$
     where $y_i$ is the actual value and $\hat{y}_i$ is the predicted value, and $N$ is the number of observations.

3. **R-squared (R²)**:
   - **Concept**: R² measures the proportion of the variance in the dependent variable that is predictable from the independent variables.
   - **Formula**:
     $$ 
     R² = 1 - \frac{SS_res}{SS_tot}
     $$
     where $SS_res$ is the sum of squares of residuals and $SS_tot$ is the total sum of squares.

- **Ensemble Methods**: Techniques that combine multiple models to improve overall performance.

**Optimization Strategies**

   - **Methods**:
     - **Grid Search**: A systematic approach to search through a predefined set of hyperparameter values.
     - **Random Search**: A more flexible approach that samples hyperparameter values from a predefined range.

2. **Ensemble Methods**:
   - **Concept**: Ensemble methods combine multiple models to improve overall performance and robustness.
   - **Methods**:
     - **Bagging**: Techniques that train multiple models on different subsets of the training data and combine their predictions.
     - **Boosting**: Techniques that sequentially train multiple models, where each model tries to correct the errors made by the previous one.

   - **Concept**: Evaluate the final model's performance using the testing set to ensure its generalization ability.
