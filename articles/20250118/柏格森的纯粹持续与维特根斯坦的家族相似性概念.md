On the other hand, Ludwig Wittgenstein, an Austrian-British philosopher, proposed the concept of "family resemblance" in his seminal work "Philosophical Investigations." Wittgenstein argued that language and meaning are not fixed and can be understood through family resemblances, which are similarities that cut across different categories. This idea challenges traditional notions of definitions and classification, offering a more flexible and nuanced understanding of meaning.
