#### Background Introduction

##### Chapter 1: Problem Background

###### 1.1.1 Origin and Characteristics of Traditional Ethics

Traditional ethics can be traced back to ancient civilizations, where moral principles were primarily derived from religious teachings, cultural traditions, and philosophical ideas. These ethics were often centered around the concept of virtue and the pursuit of the good life.

###### 1.1.2 Emergence and Development of Modern Morality

Modern morality emerged as societies became more complex and interconnected. It is characterized by a shift from virtuous ethics to ethical principles that focus on human rights, justice, and individual autonomy. This evolution is influenced by philosophical movements such as utilitarianism, deontology, and existentialism.

###### 1.1.3 Turning Points from Traditional Ethics to Modern Morality

The transformation from traditional ethics to modern morality is marked by significant events and shifts in societal values. For instance, the Enlightenment and the rise of human rights movements played pivotal roles in shaping modern moral frameworks.

##### Chapter 1.2 Core Concepts and Connections

###### 1.2.1 Core Concepts of Traditional Ethics and Morality

Traditional ethics emphasize virtues like honesty, integrity, and respect for authority. On the other hand, modern morality revolves around ethical principles such as human rights, justice, and freedom.

###### 1.2.2 Comparison and Connection between Traditional Ethics and Modern Morality

While there are significant differences, traditional ethics and modern morality share common goals of promoting well-being and social harmony. Understanding these connections can help bridge the gap between the two.

###### 1.2.3 Driving Factors for the Transformation from Traditional Ethics to Modern Morality

Several factors have contributed to this transformation, including technological advancements, global interconnectedness, and the changing role of religion in society.

##### Chapter 1.3 Boundaries and Extensions

###### 1.3.1 Application Scope of Traditional Ethics and Modern Morality

Traditional ethics often have a narrow scope, focusing mainly on individual behavior and relationships within a specific community. Modern morality, on the other hand, encompasses a broader range of issues, including social justice and human rights.

###### 1.3.2 Differences in Moral Concepts across Cultures

Different cultures have distinct moral concepts influenced by their historical, religious, and social contexts. Understanding these differences is essential for fostering cross-cultural understanding and cooperation.

###### 1.3.3 Social Impact and Adaptability of Moral Concepts

Moral concepts have a profound impact on society, shaping individual behavior and societal norms. The adaptability of these concepts to changing circumstances is crucial for maintaining social harmony and addressing emerging challenges.
