#### Mathematical Models and Formulas

The Zero-Shot CoT algorithm is grounded in several mathematical models and formulas that enable it to leverage transfer learning and meta-learning. Here are some key mathematical models and their formulas:

1. **Transfer Learning**: The transfer learning model is designed to leverage knowledge from one domain to another. The formula for transfer learning can be represented as:
   \[
   f_{\text{transfer}}(x) = f_{\text{source}}(x) + \theta_{\text{transfer}}
   \]
   where \( f_{\text{source}}(x) \) represents the pre-trained model on the source domain, \( x \) is the input data from the target domain, and \( \theta_{\text{transfer}} \) represents the parameters adjusted for the target domain.

2. **Meta-Learning**: Meta-learning techniques, such as MAML, optimize the model's ability to quickly adapt to new tasks. The objective function for meta-learning can be represented as:
   \[
   \min_{\theta} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \ell(f_{\theta}(x_i^{(t)}), y_i^{(t)})
   \]
   where \( \ell \) represents the loss function, \( f_{\theta}(x) \) is the model parameters, \( x_i^{(t)} \) and \( y_i^{(t)} \) are the input and output pairs for task \( t \), and \( N \) is the number of tasks.

3. **Few-Shot Learning**: Few-shot learning techniques, such as Triplet Loss, enable the model to generalize from a small number of examples. The Triplet Loss formula can be represented as:
   \[
   L = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \max(0, m + d_{+} - d_{-})
   \]
   where \( m \) is the margin, \( d_{+} \) is the distance between the same-class examples, and \( d_{-} \) is the distance between the different-class examples.
