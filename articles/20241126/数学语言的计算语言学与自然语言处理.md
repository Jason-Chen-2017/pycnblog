## Part 1: Foundations of Computational Linguistics

### Chapter 1: Introduction to Computational Linguistics

#### 1.1 Definition and Scope of Computational Linguistics

Computational linguistics is the study of computational models of human language, focusing on the design and application of algorithms that can process and understand linguistic data. It encompasses a wide range of activities, from the development of natural language processing tools to the analysis of linguistic data.

#### 1.2 Historical Background

The field of computational linguistics has a rich history dating back to the 1950s, with the early pioneers including Alan Turing, who proposed the Turing Test to evaluate machine intelligence. Over the decades, significant advancements have been made in the development of algorithms and models for language processing.

#### 1.3 Core Concepts and Terminology

Key concepts in computational linguistics include syntactic parsing, semantic analysis, and discourse analysis. These concepts are essential for understanding the structure and meaning of language.

### Chapter 2: Formal Language Theory

#### 2.1 Grammars and Formal Languages

Grammars are rules that define the syntax of a language. Formal languages are sets of strings that can be generated by a grammar. We'll explore types of grammars, including regular, context-free, and context-sensitive grammars.

##### 2.1.1 Types of Grammars

- **Regular Grammars:** Define regular languages, which are those that can be expressed by regular expressions.
- **Context-Free Grammars:** Define context-free languages, which are more expressive than regular languages.
- **Context-Sensitive Grammars:** Define context-sensitive languages, which include all formal languages.

##### 2.1.2 Chomsky Hierarchy

Noam Chomsky's classification of grammars into a hierarchy (Type-0, Type-1, Type-2, Type-3) provides a way to categorize the expressiveness of formal languages.

#### 2.2 Regular Expressions

Regular expressions are a powerful tool for pattern matching and text processing. They are based on regular grammars and are used extensively in NLP tasks such as text parsing and search.

##### 2.2.1 Syntax and Usage

Regular expressions have a specific syntax that allows for complex patterns to be specified. We'll explore common operators and constructs used in regular expressions.

##### 2.2.2 Regular Expression Algorithms

Algorithms such as finite automata and backtracking are used to implement regular expression matching efficiently.

### Chapter 3: Automata Theory

#### 3.1 Finite State Machines

Finite state machines (FSMs) are a mathematical model used to describe systems that can be in a finite number of states. They are fundamental in understanding language processing and pattern recognition.

##### 3.1.1 Definition and Operations

FSMs consist of states, transitions, and an initial state. We'll discuss how FSMs operate and their applications in NLP.

##### 3.1.2 Applications in NLP

FSMs are used in various NLP tasks, such as part-of-speech tagging and named entity recognition.

#### 3.2 Pushdown Automata

Pushdown automata (PDAs) extend FSMs by adding a stack, allowing them to recognize more complex languages.

##### 3.2.1 Definition and Applications

PDAs are used to model context-free languages and are applied in parsing algorithms such as the CKY algorithm.

##### 3.2.2 Context-Free Grammars

Context-free grammars are defined and their role in NLP is explored.
