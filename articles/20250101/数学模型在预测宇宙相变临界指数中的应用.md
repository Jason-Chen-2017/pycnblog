#### 2.2 Critical Exponents

**Definition and Properties**

Critical exponents are parameters that describe the behavior of systems near phase transitions. They are related to the critical temperature and critical pressure at which a phase transition occurs. Critical exponents are typically denoted by Greek letters, such as α, β, γ, and δ.

- **α (Exponential Factor)**: Describes the divergence of correlation lengths near the critical point.
- **β (Order Parameter Exponent)**: Relates the order parameter (a measure of the transition) to the temperature.
- **γ (Inverse Critical Exponent)**: Describes the divergence of correlation lengths in the direction perpendicular to the order parameter.
- **δ (Dimensional Exponent)**: Describes the scaling of the correlation length with the system size.

**Role in Phase Transitions**

Critical exponents play a crucial role in understanding the behavior of systems near phase transitions. They provide insights into the nature of the transition, such as whether it is continuous or discontinuous, and can be used to predict the properties of the resulting phases. For example, the value of α determines whether the transition is mean-field or non-mean-field, while the value of β determines the critical temperature.

**Comparison of Critical Exponents**

Critical exponents can vary between different systems and phase transitions. For example, in the three-dimensional Ising model, the critical exponent α is approximately 0.36, while in the two-dimensional Ising model, it is approximately 0.5. Comparing critical exponents between different models and systems can provide valuable insights into the underlying mechanisms driving the phase transitions.
