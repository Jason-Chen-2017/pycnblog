#### Introduction

The importance of ensuring food safety cannot be overstated. Foodborne illnesses cause significant health issues and economic losses worldwide. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), approximately 420 million people fall ill each year due to foodborne diseases, resulting in over 250,000 deaths. In the United States, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) estimates that approximately 1 in 6 Americans gets sick each year from foodborne diseases. These statistics underscore the urgent need for effective food safety measures.

Food traceability, which refers to the ability to track the movement of food from the farm to the table, is a cornerstone of food safety. It provides transparency, helps in the rapid identification and containment of contaminated products, and builds consumer trust. However, the current systems for food traceability are often plagued by several challenges, including data integrity issues, inefficiencies, and high costs.

Blockchain technology, with its inherent transparency, immutability, and security features, offers a promising solution to these challenges. Blockchain is a decentralized ledger technology that enables the secure and transparent recording of transactions across multiple participants. By leveraging blockchain, the food supply chain can be made more transparent and efficient, thereby enhancing food safety and consumer confidence.
