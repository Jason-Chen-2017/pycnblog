#### 1.1 The Definition and Role of Prompt Words
**Prompt words are linguistic elements that guide the communication process by indicating the type or direction of the upcoming utterance. They are crucial in both pragmatics and semantics. In pragmatics, prompt words help in understanding the context and the intended meaning of a sentence. In semantics, they influence the interpretation of the words and phrases used.**

**Basic Concepts:**
Prompt words can be categorized as explicit or implicit. Explicit prompt words are clear indicators, such as question words ("who," "what," "why"), while implicit prompt words are more subtle and require inference from the context.

**Application in Pragmatics:**
In pragmatic studies, prompt words are used to understand the speaker's intention and the listener's interpretation of the message. They play a significant role in the cooperative principle, which emphasizes the need for communication to be both informative and relevant.

**Importance in Semantics:**
Semantic analysis benefits from the presence of prompt words, as they provide clues for the interpretation of ambiguous phrases. They help in disambiguating homonyms and ensuring the correct understanding of the speaker's message.

---

### 1.2 Basic Principles of Pragmatics
**Research Objectives:**
Pragmatics focuses on the study of language in context, aiming to explain how context influences meaning. It addresses questions such as how speakers convey implicatures, how they use politeness strategies, and how they adapt their language to different social contexts.

**Theoretical Framework:**
A key framework in pragmatics is the speech act theory, which explains how utterances function to perform certain actions (e.g., asserting, requesting, promising). Additionally, the cooperative principle, proposed by Grice, emphasizes the need for speakers to be both informative and relevant in their communication.

**Basic Principles and Guidelines:**
Pragmatic principles include the principles of relevance and the maxim of quantity, quality, relation, and manner. These principles guide speakers in choosing appropriate utterances and listeners in interpreting them correctly.

---

### 1.3 Basic Principles of Semantics
**Research Scope:**
Semantics is concerned with the study of meaning in language. It investigates how words, phrases, and sentences convey meaning and how meaning is understood by the listener. The scope includes lexical semantics (word meaning), compositional semantics (sentence meaning), and intensional semantics (context-dependent meaning).

**Methods and Techniques:**
Semantic analysis often involves techniques like distributional semantics, which analyze how words are used in different contexts, and formal semantics, which uses mathematical models to represent and analyze meaning.

**Basic Principles and Theories:**
Key principles in semantics include compositionality (the idea that the meaning of a sentence is determined by the meanings of its parts and the rules for combining them) and the principle of truth-conditional meaning (which states that the truth conditions of a sentence determine its meaning).

### 1.4 Cross-Disciplinary Analysis of Prompt Words
**Pragmatic Features:**
Prompt words in a pragmatic context guide the listener in understanding the speaker's intended meaning. They often indicate a question or a request for information, prompting the listener to provide a relevant response.

**Semantic Features:**
In semantic analysis, prompt words help in disambiguating phrases and ensuring the correct interpretation of the speaker's message. They are particularly useful in resolving homonymy and polysemy.

**Cross-Disciplinary Perspective:**
A cross-disciplinary analysis of prompt words reveals their dual role in both guiding communication and contributing to meaning interpretation. This perspective is essential for a comprehensive understanding of language use.

### 1.5 Application of Prompt Words in Different Linguistic Environments
**Chinese Usage:**
In Chinese, prompt words often follow a different syntactic structure compared to English. For example, the use of question particles like "ma" or "ne" at the end of sentences to indicate a question.

**English Usage:**
English prompt words are more explicit, with question words and sentence structures like wh-questions and yes-no questions being common. Additionally, the use of negation and modal verbs can serve as prompt words to indicate the speaker's stance or expectation.

**Comparative Analysis:**
A comparative analysis of prompt words in different languages highlights the variations in their usage and the underlying pragmatic and semantic principles that govern their function.
