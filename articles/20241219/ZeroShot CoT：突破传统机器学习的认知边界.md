#### 3.4.4.1 Bayesian Networks

Bayesian Networks (BNs) are a popular probabilistic graphical model used in causal inference. They represent the causal relationships between variables using a directed acyclic graph (DAG), where nodes represent variables and edges represent causal relationships. The probability distribution of each variable is modeled using conditional probability distributions.

**Key Formulas:**

1. **Conditional Probability Distribution:**
   $$ P(X|Y) = \frac{P(X, Y)}{P(Y)} $$

2. **Conditional Independence:**
   $$ P(X|Y, Z) = P(X|Z) \quad \text{if } X \perp Y | Z $$

3. **Bayesian Scoring:**
   $$ \text{Score}(B) = -2 \sum_{v} P(v) \log P(v) - 2 \sum_{e} P(e) \log P(e) $$

4. **Structure Learning:**
   $$ P(X, Y) = P(X) P(Y | X) $$

**Example:**
Consider a simple Bayesian Network with three variables: `X`, `Y`, and `Z`. The graph has edges `X -> Y` and `Y -> Z`. The conditional probability distribution for `X` given `Y` and `Z` can be represented as:
$$ P(X|Y, Z) = P(X|Z) $$

#### 3.4.4.2 Structural Causal Models (SCMs)

Structural Causal Models (SCMs) provide a more formal representation of causal relationships, incorporating both the causal structure and the effect mechanism. SCMs use directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) to represent the causal relationships and allow for the identification of causal structures from data.

**Key Formulas:**

1. **Do-Calculus:**
   - **Do Intervention:**
     $$ P(X|do(Y)) = P(X|Y) $$
   - **Conditional Do Intervention:**
     $$ P(X|do(Y), Z) = P(X|Z) $$

2. **Causal Mechanism:**
   $$ \text{Mech}(X \rightarrow Y) = P(X, Y) - P(X) P(Y) $$

3. **Causal Inference:**
   $$ \text{Identifiability} \iff P(X, Y, Z) = P(X|Z) P(Y|Z) P(Z) $$

**Example:**
Consider a SCM with three variables: `X`, `Y`, and `Z`. The graph has edges `X -> Y` and `Y -> Z`. Using Do-Calculus, we can perform interventions to infer causal relationships. For example:
$$ P(X|do(Y)) = P(X|Y) $$
$$ P(X|do(Z)) = P(X|Z) $$

Reinforcement Learning (RL) models are commonly used in Zero-Shot CoT algorithms to learn causal relationships from interactions with the environment. RL models use reward signals to guide the learning process and make adaptive predictions.

**Key Formulas:**

2. **Value Iteration:**
