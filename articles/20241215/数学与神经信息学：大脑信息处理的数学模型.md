#### 1.1 Research Background

**1.1.1 Development History of Mathematics and Neural Information Science**

The field of mathematics and neural information science has evolved significantly over the past few decades. Mathematics has always been a powerful tool in the realm of science and engineering, enabling precise descriptions and predictions of complex phenomena. Neural information science, on the other hand, is an interdisciplinary field that merges principles from neuroscience, cognitive science, and computational models to study the processing and transmission of information within the brain.

In the early 20th century, the works of neuroscientists such as Charles Sherrington and Donald Hebb laid the groundwork for modern neural information science. Sherrington's book "The Integrative Action of the Nervous System" introduced the concept of neural plasticity, while Hebb's theory of cell assemblies provided a framework for understanding how neurons can communicate and process information.

The development of mathematical models in neural information science began in the mid-20th century with the work of Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts, who introduced the first neural network model. Their work paved the way for the development of more sophisticated models that could capture the complexity of neural circuits and their information processing capabilities.

**1.1.2 Importance of Mathematical Models in Brain Information Processing**

Mathematical models of brain information processing are crucial for several reasons. Firstly, they provide a quantitative framework for understanding the underlying mechanisms of neural computation. By formulating the behavior of neurons and neural circuits in mathematical terms, researchers can gain insights into how information is encoded, transmitted, and processed in the brain.

Secondly, mathematical models can be used to make predictions about the behavior of neural systems under different conditions. For example, by simulating the activity of neural circuits, researchers can investigate how changes in synaptic strength or connectivity might affect information processing and cognitive functions.

Lastly, mathematical models have practical applications in various fields, such as medicine, artificial intelligence, and neuroscience. They can be used to develop algorithms for neural prosthetics, improve machine learning models, and gain a deeper understanding of brain disorders.

**1.1.3 Current Research Status and Challenges**

The field of mathematics and neural information science is rapidly advancing, with numerous breakthroughs in recent years. However, several challenges remain. One major challenge is the complexity of neural systems. The brain is a highly intricate network of billions of neurons, each interconnected in complex ways. Modeling such a system requires sophisticated algorithms and computational resources.

Another challenge is the lack of detailed data about the structure and function of neural circuits. While significant progress has been made in imaging techniques such as functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and electron microscopy, obtaining a comprehensive understanding of the brain's microcircuitry remains a significant challenge.

Additionally, there is a need for improved mathematical models that can accurately capture the dynamics of neural activity. Current models often rely on simplifications and assumptions that may not fully reflect the complexity of neural systems.

### Neural Information Science: Mathematical Models

#### 2.1 Basic Mathematical Models

**2.1.1 Introduction to Common Mathematical Models**

In neural information science, various mathematical models are used to describe the behavior of neural systems. These models can range from simple algebraic equations to complex differential equations and stochastic processes. Some of the most commonly used mathematical models include linear models, non-linear models, and probabilistic models.

**2.1.2 Applications of Mathematical Models in Neural Information Science**

Mathematical models are applied in various ways within the field of neural information science. They are used to study the dynamics of neural activity, simulate neural circuits, and develop algorithms for neural processing. For example, linear models are often used to study the input-output relationships of neural circuits, while non-linear models are used to capture the complex, non-linear dynamics of neural activity.

#### 2.2 Neural Network Models

**2.2.1 Basic Structure of Neural Networks**

Neural networks are a class of mathematical models inspired by the structure and function of biological neural systems. They consist of interconnected processing elements, or neurons, that work together to perform tasks such as pattern recognition, classification, and regression.

**2.2.2 Introduction to Common Neural Network Models**

There are several types of neural network models, including feedforward networks, recurrent networks, and convolutional networks. Each type has its own unique structure and properties, making it suitable for different types of tasks.

**2.2.3 Applications of Neural Network Models in Information Processing**

Neural network models are widely used in information processing tasks, such as image recognition, natural language processing, and speech recognition. They have proven to be highly effective in capturing complex patterns and relationships in data, enabling advances in various fields.

#### 2.3 Statistical Models in Neural Information Science

**2.3.1 Basic Concepts of Statistical Models**

Statistical models are used in neural information science to describe the probabilities of different outcomes based on a set of observed data. They are particularly useful for understanding the uncertainty and variability in neural systems.

**2.3.2 Applications of Statistical Models in Neural Information Science**

Statistical models are applied in various ways within the field of neural information science, including modeling neural responses, predicting behavioral outcomes, and analyzing experimental data.
