**Section 1.2: Basic Principles of Zero-Shot CoT**

**1.2.1 Definition of Zero-Shot CoT**

- **Contextual Translation in Natural Language Processing:**
  - Translation that takes into account the context of the words or sentences being translated.
  - Unlike traditional translation methods, it doesn't require parallel corpora or pre-trained models.

- **Zero-Shot CoT:**
  - A translation approach that can translate text from a source language to a target language without any prior training on the specific language pair or domain.
  - It leverages semantic embeddings and transfer learning techniques to bridge the gap between languages or domains.

**1.2.2 Working Principles of Zero-Shot CoT**

- **Semantic Embeddings:**
  - Represent words, phrases, or sentences in a high-dimensional space such that semantically similar elements are close together.
  - This helps in capturing the meaning and context of the text.

- **Transfer Learning:**
  - Utilizes pre-trained models that have been trained on large datasets to improve the performance of the Zero-Shot CoT system.
  - These models have learned general patterns and structures that can be adapted to new tasks.

- **Translation Process:**
  - Involves encoding the source text into embeddings, then generating target text embeddings that are contextually relevant to the target language.

**1.2.3 Key Technologies of Zero-Shot CoT**

* **Multilingual BERT Models:**
  - Pre-trained language models that support multiple languages, providing a unified representation of different languages.
  
* **Knowledge Graphs:**
  - Structured representations of knowledge that can be used to enhance the translation process by providing additional contextual information.

* **Inferential Methods:**
  - Techniques such as zero-shot learning, few-shot learning, and meta-learning to infer the correct translations without explicit training.
