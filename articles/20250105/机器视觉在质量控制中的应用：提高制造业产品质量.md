### Background of Machine Vision in Manufacturing Quality

Unlike human inspectors who can become fatigued or subjective, machine vision systems are objective and can operate 24/7 without breaks. ### Core Concepts and Principles of Machine Vision

**2.1. Definition of Machine Vision**

Machine vision is a technology that involves the use of optical sensors and digital image processing algorithms to provide equivalent functionality to the human visual system. It involves capturing images, processing them to extract meaningful information, and then using that information to make decisions or take actions.

**2.2. Key Principles and Technologies**

The key principles of machine vision include image acquisition, image processing, feature extraction, and decision-making. Image acquisition involves capturing digital images using optical sensors such as cameras or sensors. Image processing involves manipulating the acquired images to enhance or extract the desired information. Feature extraction is the process of identifying and extracting relevant features from the processed images. Decision-making involves using the extracted features to make decisions or take actions.

Common technologies used in machine vision include:
- **Image Acquisition:** Digital cameras, CCD/CMOS sensors, and 3D scanning technologies.
- **Image Processing:** Digital image processing algorithms such as filtering, enhancement, segmentation, and edge detection.
- **Feature Extraction:** Techniques such as pattern recognition, machine learning, and computer vision algorithms.
- **Decision-Making:** Rules-based systems, neural networks, and expert systems.

**2.3. Comparative Analysis of Machine Vision Systems**

There are various types of machine vision systems available, each with its own advantages and limitations. A comparative analysis of these systems can help determine the most suitable system for a specific application.

- **Camera Type:** CCD (Charged Coupled Device) and CMOS (Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor) cameras are the most common types. CCD cameras are known for their high image quality and low noise, while CMOS cameras are more affordable and offer higher frame rates.
- **Image Processing Hardware:** Dedicated machine vision controllers, FPGAs (Field-Programmable Gate Arrays), and general-purpose computers with high processing power can be used for image processing. Each option has its own set of advantages and limitations in terms of speed, memory, and flexibility.
- **Software:** Open-source software such as OpenCV and commercial software such as HALCON and MATLAB provide a wide range of tools and libraries for image processing and machine vision applications. The choice of software depends on the specific requirements of the application and the level of expertise of the developers.

By understanding the core concepts and principles of machine vision and comparing different machine vision systems, we can better appreciate the potential of this technology in enhancing manufacturing quality control.
