### Introduction

#### 1.1 Problem Background

Earthquakes are one of the most devastating natural disasters, capable of causing immense damage to infrastructure, loss of life, and long-term socio-economic disruption. Early warning systems play a crucial role in mitigating the impact of earthquakes by providing timely alerts to communities at risk. However, the current early warning systems are limited by several factors, including the time required for data processing and the accuracy of the predictions.

#### 1.2 Problem Description

The problem with existing earthquake early warning systems lies primarily in their reliance on traditional seismic sensors, which have inherent limitations in detecting and analyzing seismic waves. These sensors are often prone to noise, interference, and delays in signal transmission, leading to reduced accuracy and delayed alerts. This delay can be critical, especially in high-risk regions where seconds can mean the difference between life and death.

#### 1.3 Solutions and Methods

The advent of quantum sensors offers a promising solution to these challenges. Quantum sensors, based on the principles of quantum mechanics, possess extraordinary sensitivity and precision, far surpassing traditional sensors. They are capable of detecting extremely weak signals and distinguishing between different types of seismic waves, enabling more accurate and timely earthquake predictions.
