### Core Concepts and Terminology

Before diving into the intricacies of using prompt words to accelerate the literature review process, it is essential to establish a foundational understanding of key concepts and terminology. A literature review is a comprehensive summary and critical evaluation of published research on a specific topic. It serves as a foundational step in the research process, providing an overview of existing knowledge, identifying research gaps, and justifying the need for new research.

**Key concepts and terms include:**

- **Literature Review:** A systematic evaluation of published research on a specific topic.
- **Prompt Words:** Words or phrases that guide the researcher in the search for relevant literature.
- **Keywords:** Words or phrases that represent the main concepts of a research topic.
- **Search Strategies:** Methods used to locate relevant literature within databases and other resources.
- **Research Questions:** Specific questions that the literature review aims to answer.

### Background

Conducting a literature review is a critical step in the research process, as it helps researchers understand the existing body of knowledge on a particular topic. However, the process can be time-consuming and challenging, given the vast amount of information available. Researchers often face several challenges, including information overload, the need to evaluate the credibility of sources, and the task of synthesizing information from diverse sources.

**Challenges in Conducting Literature Reviews:**

1. **Information Overload:** With the increasing number of academic publications, researchers may struggle to sift through vast amounts of information.
2. **Credibility Assessment:** Evaluating the credibility and relevance of sources can be a complex task, as not all publications are created equal.
3. **Synthesis of Information:** Integrating information from multiple sources and synthesizing it into a coherent narrative can be challenging.
