## 2. Background and Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Introduction to Algebraic Topology

Algebraic topology is a branch of mathematics that combines concepts from algebra and topology to study the properties of spaces that are invariant under continuous deformations. The primary goal of algebraic topology is to classify and study topological spaces by associating them with algebraic structures such as groups, rings, and fields. These algebraic structures help in understanding the global properties of spaces, such as connectivity, shape, and structure.

#### 2.1.1 Basic Terminology and Concepts

To grasp the fundamentals of algebraic topology, it's essential to familiarize oneself with some key terminology and concepts:

- **Topological Space**: A topological space is a set equipped with a collection of subsets, called open sets, that satisfy certain axioms. These open sets allow us to define concepts such as continuity, convergence, and connectedness.

- **Continuous Function**: A function between two topological spaces is continuous if the preimage of every open set in the codomain is open in the domain. Continuous functions preserve the "closeness" of points in the space.

- **Homeomorphism**: A bijective function between two topological spaces that is both continuous and has a continuous inverse is called a homeomorphism. Homeomorphisms preserve the topological properties of spaces, meaning that two spaces are homeomorphic if they can be continuously deformed into one another.
