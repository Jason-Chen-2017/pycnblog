GANs are a type of deep learning model that consists of two neural networks: a generator and a discriminator. The generator creates content, such as images or text, while the discriminator evaluates the authenticity of the content. The generator and discriminator are trained simultaneously in a zero-sum game, where the generator tries to create content that is indistinguishable from real content, while the discriminator tries to identify the fake content. Over time, the generator improves its output, creating increasingly realistic content.

**Applications in Architectural Design:**

**3.2 Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**

**3.2.1 Mathematical Foundations**

GANs consist of two main components: a generator and a discriminator. The generator creates synthetic data, while the discriminator evaluates the authenticity of the generated data. The training process is based on an adversarial game where the generator tries to fool the discriminator by producing realistic data, and the discriminator tries to identify the real data from the generated data.

Mathematically, the training of a GAN can be described as follows:

- **Generator:**
  $$ G(z) = x \; ; \; z \sim p_z(z) $$
  Here, \( G \) is a function that takes a random noise vector \( z \) from a prior distribution \( p_z(z) \) and generates a data sample \( x \).

- **Discriminator:**
  $$ D(x) \; ; x \sim p_{data}(x) $$
  Here, \( D \) is a function that takes a data sample \( x \) and outputs a probability indicating how likely \( x \) is to be from the data distribution \( p_{data}(x) \).

The generator and discriminator are trained simultaneously using the following objective functions:

- **Generator Objective:**
  $$ \min_G \; \max_D \; V(D, G) = \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{data}(x)} [D(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z(z)} [D(G(z))] $$

- **Discriminator Objective:**
  $$ \min_D \; \max_G \; V(D, G) $$

Here, \( V(D, G) \) is the objective function that both networks aim to optimize. The generator tries to maximize the probability of the discriminator being fooled, while the discriminator tries to minimize this probability.

**3.2.2 Step-by-Step Guide to Implementation**

To implement a GAN, follow these steps:

1. **Define the Generator and Discriminator Networks:**
   - Design neural network architectures for the generator and discriminator.
   - Initialize the weights randomly.

2. **Set the Loss Function:**
   - Use binary cross-entropy as the loss function for both the generator and the discriminator.

3. **Train the Discriminator:**
   - Present real data samples to the discriminator.
   - Update the discriminator's weights using backpropagation and the calculated gradients.

4. **Train the Generator:**
   - Present synthetic data samples (generated by the generator) to the discriminator.
   - Update the generator's weights using backpropagation and the calculated gradients.

5. **Iterate the Training Process:**
   - Repeat the training process for a fixed number of epochs or until the performance converges.

**3.3 Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)**

**3.3.1 Mathematical Foundations**

RNNs are designed to handle sequential data by maintaining a "memory" of previous inputs. This is achieved through the use of loops in the network's architecture, allowing it to process data in a sequential manner.

The most common type of RNN is the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, which is capable of learning long-term dependencies. LSTMs use three gates (input gate, forget gate, and output gate) to control the flow of information within the network.

Mathematically, an LSTM unit can be described as follows:

- **Input Gate:**
  $$ i_t = \sigma(W_{ix}x_t + W_{ih}h_{t-1} + b_i) $$

- **Forget Gate:**
  $$ f_t = \sigma(W_{fx}x_t + W_{fh}h_{t-1} + b_f) $$

- **Output Gate:**
  $$ o_t = \sigma(W_{ox}x_t + W_{oh}h_{t-1} + b_o) $$

- **Cell State:**
  $$ c_t = f_t \odot c_{t-1} + i_t \odot \sigma(W_{cx}x_t + b_c) $$

- **Hidden State:**
  $$ h_t = o_t \odot \sigma(c_t) $$

Here, \( x_t \) is the input at time \( t \), \( h_t \) is the hidden state at time \( t \), \( c_t \) is the cell state at time \( t \), and \( \sigma \) is the sigmoid activation function. The weights \( W \) and biases \( b \) are learned during training.

**3.4 Transformer Models**

**3.4.1 Mathematical Foundations**

Transformer models are based on self-attention mechanisms, which allow the model to weigh the importance of different parts of the input data dynamically. The core component of the transformer model is the multi-head self-attention mechanism, which is followed by a feedforward network.

**Multi-Head Self-Attention:**
The multi-head self-attention mechanism can be described as follows:

$$
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V
$$

Here, \( Q, K, \) and \( V \) are queries, keys, and values, respectively, obtained by passing the input through different linear projections. \( d_k \) is the dimension of the keys and values. The result is a weighted combination of the values, where the weights are determined by the dot product between the queries and keys.

**Feedforward Network:**
The feedforward network is a simple two-layer neural network applied to the output of the self-attention mechanism. Each layer has a rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation function.

$$
\text{FFN}(x) = \max(0, xW_1 + b_1)W_2 + b_2
$$

3. **Build the Model:**

4. **Train the Model:**

5. **Evaluate the Model:**
   - Test the model's performance on unseen data to assess its generalization capabilities.
