#### 2.1 Fundamental Concepts of Humor Perception

##### 2.1.1 Humor Theories

Humor perception is a complex phenomenon that has been studied from various perspectives. Different theories attempt to explain the nature of humor and how it is perceived by humans. Some of the prominent humor theories include:

1. **Plutchik's Theory of Humor**: This theory suggests that humor arises from the recognition of incongruity, where the unexpected or incongruous elicits a humorous response. Plutchik identified six categories of humor: relief, play, teasing, aggression, self-enhancement, and incongruity.

2. **Snyder's Theory of Humor**: Snyder's theory focuses on the psychological aspects of humor, suggesting that humor serves as a coping mechanism to manage stress and negative emotions. This theory categorizes humor into social humor, intellectual humor, and Relief humor.

3. **Bryson's Theory of Comic Relief**: Bryson's theory emphasizes the role of incongruity and conflict in humor. According to this theory, humor arises from the recognition of a clash between two ideas or situations.

##### 2.1.2 Psychological Aspects of Humor

The psychological aspects of humor are essential in understanding how humans perceive and react to humor. Some key psychological aspects include:

1. **Cognitive Processing**: Humor involves cognitive processes such as perception, interpretation, and evaluation. People process humor differently based on their cognitive abilities and cognitive load.

2. **Emotional Response**: Humor often triggers emotional responses, such as laughter or amusement. Emotional responses to humor can vary based on individual personality, mood, and context.

3. **Cultural Factors**: Humor is deeply influenced by cultural factors, including language, customs, and traditions. What is funny in one culture may not be funny in another, highlighting the importance of cultural awareness in humor perception.

##### 2.1.3 Types of Humor

Humor can be classified into various types based on its characteristics and functions. Some common types of humor include:

1. **Self-Enhancing Humor**: This type of humor involves humor that is used to boost self-esteem or cope with stress. It often involves self-deprecation or self-mocking.

2. **Social Humor**: Social humor is used to establish social bonds or navigate social situations. It can involve teasing, joking, or playfulness.

3. **Intellectual Humor**: Intellectual humor relies on wit, cleverness, and the use of irony or sarcasm. It often appeals to the intelligence of the audience.

4. **Physical Humor**: Physical humor involves slapstick or physical comedy, using physical actions or mishaps to evoke laughter.
