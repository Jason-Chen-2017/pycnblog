**Mathematical Models and Formulas**

To further elucidate the workings of the Self-Consistency algorithm, we can delve into its mathematical models and formulas. These models provide a framework for understanding how the algorithm adjusts predictions and maintains consistency. The core formula for Self-Consistency involves calculating an adjustment factor based on the difference between the predicted values and the actual observed values. This factor is then used to adjust the model's parameters, ensuring that the predictions remain consistent with the new data.

**Adjustment Factor Calculation**

The adjustment factor (AF) is calculated using the following formula:

$$ AF = \frac{\sum{(P_i - O_i)^2}}{N} $$

Where:
- \( P_i \) represents the predicted value for observation \( i \).
- \( O_i \) represents the observed value for observation \( i \).
- \( N \) is the total number of observations.

The adjustment factor measures the squared error between the predicted and observed values, providing a quantifiable measure of prediction accuracy. A lower adjustment factor indicates a higher degree of consistency between predictions and observations.

**Prediction Value Update**

Once the adjustment factor is calculated, the model's predictions are updated using the following formula:

$$ P_{new} = P_{old} + AF \cdot \frac{P_{old} - O_{old}}{2} $$

Where:
- \( P_{old} \) represents the previous prediction value.
- \( P_{new} \) represents the updated prediction value.
- \( O_{old} \) represents the previous observed value.

The updated prediction value \( P_{new} \) is a weighted average of the previous prediction \( P_{old} \) and the observed value \( O_{old} \). This formula ensures that the new prediction \( P_{new} \) reflects the most recent observed data while maintaining a balance with the previous prediction.
