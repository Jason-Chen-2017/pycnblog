#### 1.1 Problem Background and Definition

Design thinking is a human-centered approach to innovation that combines empathy, creativity, and rationality to solve complex problems and create new solutions. It is a method that has been widely adopted in various fields, from business and engineering to healthcare and education. Despite its widespread application, there are ongoing debates about the most effective ways to integrate design thinking into practice.

In recent years, the philosophical ideas of Henri Bergson and Ludwig Wittgenstein have been increasingly recognized for their potential to inform and enhance design thinking. Bergson's focus on intuition and creativity, and Wittgenstein's emphasis on language and analysis, offer complementary perspectives that can help designers navigate the complexities of the design process.

The problem this book seeks to address is the integration of Bergson's and Wittgenstein's methodologies into design thinking. While there have been some explorations of these ideas in the context of design, there is a lack of systematic analysis and application. This book aims to fill this gap by providing a comprehensive examination of how Bergson's and Wittgenstein's philosophies can be applied to design thinking, offering practical examples and insights.

#### 1.2 Core Concepts and Their Relationship

**Intuition:** Intuition is the ability to understand something instinctively, without the need for conscious reasoning. In design thinking, intuition is often seen as a source of creative insight and a means of generating innovative solutions.

**Analysis:** Analysis is the process of breaking down a complex subject or concept into its basic parts to understand its nature or to determine its essential features. In design thinking, analysis is used to clarify problems, analyze user needs, and evaluate potential solutions.

**Design Thinking:** Design thinking is a process that uses intuition and analysis to explore and solve complex problems. It involves understanding the needs of users, generating creative ideas, and testing and refining those ideas through iterative cycles of prototyping and evaluation.

The relationship between these concepts is that intuition and analysis are complementary tools that can be used together to enhance the design thinking process. Intuition provides the creative spark and the ability to see the big picture, while analysis provides the structure and the detail necessary to refine and develop ideas.

**Intuition:** Intuition, in its most basic form, refers to the ability to understand or judge something instinctively, without the need for conscious reasoning. It is a form of knowledge that arises from deep within us, often bypassing the conscious mind. In the context of design thinking, intuition is particularly valuable as it provides a quick and creative way to grasp complex problems and generate innovative solutions.

**Characteristics of Intuition:**
- **Speed:** Intuition often operates quickly, allowing designers to make judgments and decisions rapidly.
- **Subconscious:** Intuition draws on subconscious processes, integrating information and insights from past experiences.
- **Contextual:** Intuition is highly contextual, meaning it is often tied to specific situations and the individual's understanding of those situations.
- **Error-Prone:** While intuitive judgments can be powerful, they are also subject to errors and biases, such as confirmation bias or overconfidence.

**Types of Intuition:**
- **Intuitive Insight:** A sudden and non-deductive understanding of a problem or solution.
- **Empathic Intuition:** The ability to understand the feelings and perspectives of others, often through non-verbal cues.
- **Conceptual Intuition:** The ability to grasp the underlying principles or structures of complex ideas or systems.

**Analysis:** Analysis is the process of breaking down a complex subject or concept into its basic parts to understand its nature or to determine its essential features. It is a methodical and rational approach that seeks to uncover the underlying patterns, relationships, and structures that govern a given phenomenon.

**Characteristics of Analysis:**
- **Structured:** Analysis is structured and systematic, following a defined process or method.
- **Objective:** The goal of analysis is to be objective, relying on data, evidence, and logical reasoning.
- **Detailed:** Analysis involves a detailed examination of the components and their interactions within a system.
- **Critical:** Analysis often involves evaluating the strengths and weaknesses of various approaches or solutions.

**Types of Analysis:**
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Involves understanding and interpreting non-numerical data, such as textual content or observations.
- **Quantitative Analysis:** Involves the use of numerical data to measure and evaluate phenomena.
- **Statistical Analysis:** A specific type of quantitative analysis that uses statistical methods to analyze data and make inferences.
- **Logical Analysis:** A type of analysis that focuses on the logical structure and consistency of arguments and concepts.

**Design Thinking:** Design thinking is a problem-solving methodology that integrates empathy, creativity, and rationality to explore and solve complex problems. It is a human-centered approach that focuses on understanding users' needs and creating solutions that are both innovative and practical.

**Principles of Design Thinking:**
- **Empathy:** Understanding the needs, behaviors, and emotions of users.
- **Define:** Clearly defining the problem or opportunity.
- **Ideate:** Generating a wide range of ideas and solutions.
- **Prototype:** Creating tangible representations of ideas to test and refine them.
- **Test:** Evaluating and iterating on prototypes to ensure they meet users' needs.

**Process of Design Thinking:**
1. **Empathize:** Conduct research to understand the users and their needs.
2. **Define:** Clarify the problem statement based on the research findings.
3. **Ideate:** Generate and develop a range of potential solutions.
4. **Prototype:** Build and test prototypes to refine the solutions.
5. **Test:** Evaluate the prototypes with users and gather feedback for further refinement.

### 2.1 Bergson's Methodology in Design Thinking

Henri Bergson, a prominent French philosopher of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, made significant contributions to our understanding of creativity, time, and intuition. His philosophical ideas offer a rich foundation for exploring the role of intuition in design thinking. In this section, we will delve into the key concepts and principles of Bergson's philosophy, with a focus on how they can be applied in the design process.

#### 2.1.1 Bergson's Concepts and Philosophy

Bergson's philosophy is deeply rooted in his theory of evolution and creative evolution, which he believed was a fundamental driving force in the development of life and thought. According to Bergson, evolution is not solely driven by natural selection, as proposed by Charles Darwin, but also by a creative force known as "élan vital" or "vital force." This force is an inherent part of life that drives continuous creation and transformation.

**The Theory of Evolution and Creative Evolution:**
- **Élan Vital:** Bergson's concept of élan vital is a form of life energy that underlies all living beings and drives the creative process. It is this force that enables living organisms to adapt and evolve.
- **Creative Evolution:** Creative evolution is a process of continuous innovation and transformation, driven by the élan vital. It involves the creation of new forms and structures, which are not simply variations of existing forms but are fundamentally new.

**The Role of Intuition in Creative Problem Solving:**
- **Intuition as a Cognitive Tool:** Bergson emphasized the role of intuition as a cognitive tool for understanding the complex and dynamic nature of the world. Intuition allows us to grasp the underlying patterns and structures that are not immediately apparent through rational analysis.
- **Intuition in Design Thinking:** In the context of design thinking, intuition can be seen as a powerful tool for generating innovative solutions. It helps designers to break away from conventional thinking and to see new possibilities and opportunities.

#### 2.1.2 Bergson's Methodology in Design Processes

Bergson's methodology can be applied to the design process in several ways, particularly by leveraging the power of intuition to generate ideas and insights.

**The Importance of Intuition in Generating Ideas:**
- **Pattern Recognition:** Intuition allows designers to quickly recognize patterns and connections between seemingly unrelated ideas. This pattern recognition can lead to the generation of novel and innovative solutions.
- **Bypassing Rational Constraints:** Intuition often operates outside the bounds of rational constraints, allowing designers to explore uncharted territories and untested ideas. This can be particularly valuable in design processes where conventional approaches have failed to yield satisfactory results.

**Intuition in the Design Thinking Process:**
1. **Empathize:** In the empathize phase, designers can use intuition to understand the underlying needs and motivations of users. By tapping into their intuitive understanding of users' experiences, designers can gain valuable insights that inform the problem definition and solution generation.
2. **Define:** Intuition can also be used in the define phase to crystallize the problem statement. Designers can intuitively grasp the essence of the problem, often in ways that cannot be articulated through rational analysis alone.
3. **Ideate:** In the ideate phase, intuition plays a crucial role in generating a wide range of ideas. By relying on their intuitive insights, designers can produce creative and innovative solutions that might not emerge through a purely rational process.
4. **Prototype:** Intuition continues to be relevant in the prototype phase, where designers can use their intuitive understanding to refine and develop their ideas into tangible prototypes.
5. **Test:** In the test phase, intuition can help designers to evaluate the effectiveness of their prototypes. By intuitively assessing the usability and user experience of a design, designers can make informed decisions about how to iterate and improve the solution.

### 2.2 Wittgenstein's Methodology in Design Thinking

Ludwig Wittgenstein, an influential Austrian-British philosopher, made significant contributions to our understanding of language, logic, and thought. His ideas offer valuable insights into how we can use analysis to clarify concepts and guide the design thinking process. In this section, we will delve into the key concepts and principles of Wittgenstein's philosophy, examining how his ideas can be applied in design thinking.

#### 2.2.1 Wittgenstein's Philosophical Contributions

Wittgenstein's work is divided into two distinct periods, often referred to as the "early" and "later" Wittgenstein. The early Wittgenstein, as seen in his book "Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus," focused on the relationship between language and reality, proposing a view known as logical atomism. The later Wittgenstein, as articulated in his book "Philosophical Investigations," shifted his focus to the use of language in everyday life, emphasizing the importance of language games and the role of analysis in understanding meaning.

**Logical Atomism and Language Games:**
- **Logical Atomism:** In the early Wittgenstein's work, logical atomism proposes that the world is composed of elementary facts, or "原子命题" (atomic propositions), which are the building blocks of language. These facts are directly related to language and can be clearly and unambiguously expressed.
- **Language Games:** In the later Wittgenstein's work, language games are a central concept. A language game refers to the way in which language is used in specific social contexts. Wittgenstein argued that the meaning of a word or phrase is determined by its use in language games, which can vary widely depending on the context.

**The Role of Analysis in Clarifying Concepts:**
- **Clarifying Meaning:** Wittgenstein's approach to philosophy emphasized the importance of analyzing how language is used to clarify meaning. By examining the use of language in specific contexts, we can uncover the underlying rules and conventions that govern our communication.
- **Distinguishing Between Uses of Language:** Wittgenstein argued that there are many different ways in which language can be used, and each use has its own set of rules and conventions. By understanding these different uses, we can avoid misunderstandings and confusion.
- **Practical Applications in Design Thinking:** In the context of design thinking, Wittgenstein's emphasis on analysis can be applied to clarify design problems and develop a deeper understanding of user needs and behaviors. By analyzing the specific ways in which users interact with products and services, designers can gain valuable insights that inform their design decisions.

#### 2.2.2 Applying Wittgenstein's Method in Design

Wittgenstein's methodology can be effectively applied to the design process by using analysis to clarify design problems and guide the development of innovative solutions.

**The Use of Analysis in Defining Design Problems:**
- **Analyzing User Needs:** Designers can use analysis to understand the specific needs and behaviors of users. By conducting user research and analysis, designers can identify the underlying problems and opportunities that inform their design decisions.
- **Clarifying Ambiguities:** Analysis can help designers identify ambiguities and misunderstandings in the problem definition. By breaking down complex problems into smaller, more manageable components, designers can gain a clearer understanding of the issues at hand.
- **Refining Problem Statements:** Through analysis, designers can refine their problem statements to ensure they are focused and specific. This helps to guide the ideation and development phases of the design process.

**Applying Analysis in Design Processes:**
1. **Empathize:** In the empathize phase, analysis is used to understand the users' needs, motivations, and behaviors. By analyzing user feedback and research data, designers can identify key insights that inform their problem definition.
2. **Define:** Analysis continues in the define phase, where designers refine their understanding of the problem and clarify any ambiguities. By breaking down complex problems into smaller components, designers can create a clear and concise problem statement that guides their design efforts.
3. **Ideate:** In the ideate phase, analysis is used to generate and evaluate ideas. Designers can analyze potential solutions to identify their strengths and weaknesses, guiding their decision-making process.
4. **Prototype:** Analysis is used in the prototype phase to refine and test design solutions. By analyzing user feedback and testing results, designers can make informed decisions about how to iterate and improve their prototypes.
5. **Test:** In the test phase, analysis is used to evaluate the effectiveness of the design solutions. By analyzing user behavior and performance data, designers can gain insights into the usability and user experience of their designs, guiding further refinements and improvements.
