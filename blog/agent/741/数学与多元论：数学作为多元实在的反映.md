  The derivative of a function f(x) at a point x is denoted as f'(x) and is defined as the limit:

  \[
  f'(x) = \lim_{{h \to 0}} \frac{{f(x+h) - f(x)}}{h}
  \]

- **Integrals**: Integrals represent the accumulation of change over a given interval. They are used to calculate areas, volumes, and the total amount of a quantity over time. In physics, integrals are used to find the total work done or the total distance traveled.

  The integral of a function f(x) over an interval [a, b] is denoted as \(\int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx\) and is defined as the limit of a sum:

  \[
  \int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx = \lim_{{n \to \infty}} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*) \Delta x
  \]

1. **Cryptography**

Cryptography is a field that relies heavily on multivaluation to ensure secure communication. One prominent example is the RSA encryption algorithm, which uses the mathematical properties of large prime numbers and their multiplicative inverses. The RSA algorithm involves the following steps:

- **Key Generation**: Two large prime numbers p and q are chosen, and their product n = p \* q forms part of the public key. The Euler's totient function \(\phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)\) is used to calculate the private key, which is the multiplicative inverse of a randomly chosen number e modulo \(\phi(n)\).
- **Encryption**: To encrypt a message m, it is first converted into a numeric representation. The ciphertext c is then calculated using the formula c = m^e mod n.
- **Decryption**: The recipient uses their private key d to decrypt the message, recovering the original message m with the formula m = c^d mod n.

The multivaluation aspect arises in the calculation of the private key, where the inverse of e is found using the extended Euclidean algorithm, which involves solving systems of linear equations.
