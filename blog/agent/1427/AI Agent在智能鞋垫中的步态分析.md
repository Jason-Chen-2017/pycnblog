from scipy.signal import savgol_filter

def data_preprocessing(data):
    # Data cleaning and normalization
    cleaned_data = data[data > 0]
    normalized_data = cleaned_data / cleaned_data.max()
    return normalized_data

def feature_extraction(data):
    # Smoothing using Savitzky-Golay filter
    smoothed_data = savgol_filter(data, window_length=11, polyorder=2)
    
    # Derivative
    derivative_data = np.gradient(smoothed_data)
    
    # Extract features
    step_length = np.mean(derivative_data[:10])

    step_velocity = np.mean(derivative_data[10:20])
    step_acceleration = np.mean(np.diff(derivative_data[10:20]))
    
    return step_length, stride_time, step_velocity, step_acceleration

# Example usage

### 2.3 Mathematical Modeling and Analysis

The gait analysis algorithm employed in smart insoles is grounded in a robust mathematical framework that facilitates the extraction of meaningful insights from raw sensor data. This section delves into the mathematical models and formulas that underpin the algorithm, providing a deeper understanding of its workings.

#### Mathematical Formulas (LaTeX Format)

The following LaTeX-formatted equations represent the core mathematical models used in the gait analysis algorithm:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    L &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} s_i, \\
    T &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} t_i, \\
    \dot{s} &= \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}, \\
    \ddot{s} &= \frac{\Delta^2 s}{\Delta t^2}, \\
    f_s &= \frac{1}{T}, \\
    \theta(t) &= \arcsin\left(\frac{v(t)}{L}\right),
\end{aligned}
$$

where \( L \) is the step length, \( T \) is the stride time, \( \dot{s} \) is the step velocity, \( \ddot{s} \) is the step acceleration, \( f_s \) is the stride frequency, and \( \theta(t) \) is the angle of the foot relative to the horizontal plane at time \( t \).

#### Detailed Explanation

The mathematical models used in the gait analysis algorithm are derived from classical biomechanical principles and are designed to capture the essential characteristics of human walking.

1. **Step Length and Stride Time**:
   - The average step length \( L \) is calculated as the mean of the step lengths over \( N \) steps. This provides an estimate of the typical distance covered by each step.
   - The stride time \( T \) is the average time taken to complete one step, calculated as the mean of the time intervals between successive steps. This metric is crucial for understanding the temporal aspects of gait.

2. **Step Velocity and Acceleration**:
   - The step velocity \( \dot{s} \) is determined by dividing the change in step length \( \Delta s \) by the change in time \( \Delta t \). This metric provides insights into the speed at which the foot moves during each step.
   - The step acceleration \( \ddot{s} \) is calculated as the second derivative of the step length with respect to time. This measure indicates the rate of change in the step velocity and can be indicative of the force exerted during foot contact with the ground.

3. **Stride Frequency**:
   - The stride frequency \( f_s \) is the inverse of the stride time \( T \), representing the number of steps per unit of time. This is a measure of the walking cadence and can vary depending on the context, such as walking speed or exercise intensity.

4. **Angle of the Foot**:
   - The angle \( \theta(t) \) of the foot relative to the horizontal plane at time \( t \) is determined using the inverse sine function. This angle provides information about the orientation of the foot and can be used to analyze gait patterns, such as the degree of toe-out or toe-in.

To illustrate the application of these mathematical models, consider a scenario where a user's gait data is collected over a 30-second period. The data consists of step lengths and times recorded at 100 equally spaced time points.

1. **Calculate Step Length and Stride Time**:
   - Calculate the average step length \( L \) by averaging the step lengths over the 30-second period.
   - Calculate the average stride time \( T \) by averaging the time intervals between successive steps.

2. **Compute Step Velocity and Acceleration**:
   - Compute the step velocity \( \dot{s} \) by determining the change in step length and dividing by the change in time.
   - Compute the step acceleration \( \ddot{s} \) by taking the second derivative of the step length with respect to time.

3. **Determine Stride Frequency**:
   - Calculate the stride frequency \( f_s \) by taking the inverse of the stride time \( T \).

4. **Analyze Foot Angle**:
   - Determine the foot angle \( \theta(t) \) at each time point using the inverse sine function, based on the relationship between step velocity \( v(t) \) and step length \( L \).

By performing these calculations, we can derive a comprehensive set of metrics that characterize the user's gait. ### 3. Gait Analysis Using AI Agents in Smart Insoles

#### 3.1 Data Collection and Preprocessing

The accuracy and reliability of gait analysis heavily depend on the quality of the data collected. Therefore, the first step in the process is to ensure that the data is collected efficiently and is free from noise and errors. This section discusses the methods for data collection and preprocessing used in the gait analysis performed by AI agents in smart insoles.

**Data Collection Methods**:

Data collection is a critical component of gait analysis, and it begins with the placement of sensors on the smart insoles. These sensors are designed to capture precise measurements related to the user's gait, including force distribution, pressure, and motion. Common types of sensors used in smart insoles include:

- **Pressure sensors**: These sensors measure the pressure distribution across the foot during gait. They are typically placed under the heel and the forefoot.
- **Force sensors**: These sensors measure the vertical force applied to the ground during gait. They provide insights into the loading patterns and can help identify areas of excessive force.
- **Motion sensors**: These sensors capture the three-dimensional motion of the foot and ankle. They include accelerometers, gyroscopes, and sometimes even magnetometers to provide a comprehensive view of the foot's movement.

The data collection process involves the following steps:

1. **Placement of sensors**: The sensors are carefully positioned on the smart insoles to ensure they capture the relevant data accurately. This requires a precise alignment to capture the ground reaction forces and the motion of the foot correctly.
2. **Data acquisition**: The sensors collect data at a high sampling rate, typically several hundred Hz, to capture the dynamic nature of gait. This data is transmitted in real-time to the data acquisition module integrated into the smart insoles.
3. **Data transmission**: The data is transmitted wirelessly to a central processing unit (CPU) or a cloud-based server for further analysis. This ensures that the data is securely stored and can be accessed for analysis anytime.

**Data Preprocessing Techniques**:

Once the data is collected, it undergoes preprocessing to prepare it for analysis. Preprocessing is essential to remove noise, correct for sensor misalignment, and standardize the data. The following techniques are commonly used:

1. **Noise reduction**: Sensor data is often corrupted by noise, which can obscure the underlying patterns of interest. Techniques such as filtering and smoothing are used to remove noise. Common filters include low-pass filters to remove high-frequency noise and Savitzky-Golay filters for smoothing.
2. **Normalization**: To ensure consistency across different users and conditions, the data is normalized. This involves scaling the data to a common reference point, typically the maximum value observed during data collection.
3. **Alignment**: Sensor misalignment can lead to incorrect data interpretation. Techniques such as sensor fusion and registration are used to align the data from different sensors to a common coordinate system.
4. **Data cleaning**: Data cleaning involves removing any corrupted or incomplete data points. This can include removing outliers or filling missing data using interpolation techniques.

By employing these data collection and preprocessing techniques, the AI agents in smart insoles can ensure that the gait analysis is both accurate and reliable, providing valuable insights into the user's gait patterns.

#### 3.2 Feature Extraction

Feature extraction is a crucial step in gait analysis using AI agents. Extracting meaningful features from raw sensor data allows for a more accurate and efficient analysis of the gait patterns. This section discusses the methods for feature extraction and the analysis of the extracted features.

**Feature Extraction Methods**:

Feature extraction involves transforming the raw sensor data into a set of attributes or features that can be used for analysis. The choice of features depends on the specific goals of the analysis, such as identifying gait abnormalities, assessing balance, or improving sports performance. The following methods are commonly used for feature extraction:

1. **Statistical Features**: Statistical features are derived from the statistical properties of the raw sensor data. Common statistical features include mean, median, standard deviation, skewness, and kurtosis. These features provide insights into the central tendency, variability, and shape of the data distribution.
2. **Time-Domain Features**: Time-domain features are extracted directly from the time series data. Examples include the peak value, peak time, rise time, and fall time. These features capture the temporal characteristics of the data, such as the duration of specific events or the speed of changes.
3. **Frequency-Domain Features**: Frequency-domain features are derived from the frequency components of the raw sensor data. Techniques such as fast Fourier transform (FFT) and wavelet decomposition are used to analyze the frequency content of the data. Common frequency-domain features include the dominant frequency, power spectral density, and frequency bands.
4. **Spatial Features**: Spatial features are extracted from the spatial distribution of the sensor data. These features provide insights into the spatial patterns of the data, such as the spatial extent of force distribution or the spatial variation of motion.

**Feature Analysis**:

After extracting the features, it is essential to analyze them to understand their significance and contribution to the gait analysis. The following steps are involved in feature analysis:

1. **Visualization**: Visualizing the extracted features helps in understanding their distribution and patterns. Techniques such as scatter plots, histograms, and heatmaps are commonly used for visualization.
2. **Correlation Analysis**: Correlation analysis is used to examine the relationships between different features. It helps in identifying which features are most strongly associated with specific gait characteristics or conditions.
3. **Clustering**: Clustering techniques, such as k-means clustering or hierarchical clustering, are used to group similar gait patterns based on the extracted features. This can help in identifying distinct gait patterns or classes.
4. **Classification**: Classification techniques, such as support vector machines (SVM), decision trees, or neural networks, are used to classify gait patterns into different categories or conditions based on the extracted features. This can be used for diagnosis, treatment planning, or performance assessment.

**Example Analysis**:

Consider a scenario where the AI agent extracts the following features from the raw sensor data:

- **Mean Force**: The average force applied to the ground during each step.
- **Peak Force**: The maximum force applied to the ground during each step.
- **Step Length**: The distance covered by each step.
- **Stride Time**: The duration of each step.

By analyzing these features, the AI agent can gain insights into the user's gait pattern. For example, if the mean force and peak force are consistently high, it may indicate an excessive load on the lower limbs, potentially leading to overuse injuries. If the step length is significantly shorter than the average, it may suggest a gait abnormality, such as a short stride or a gait deviation.

In conclusion, feature extraction and analysis are critical steps in gait analysis using AI agents in smart insoles. By extracting meaningful features from raw sensor data and analyzing them, the AI agents can provide valuable insights into the user's gait patterns, assisting in the diagnosis and treatment of gait-related conditions and improving sports performance.
