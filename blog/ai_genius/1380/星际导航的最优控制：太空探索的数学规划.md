The field was born out of the need to solve complex engineering problems, particularly in aerospace and control systems. Pontryagin's Maximum Principle, introduced in the early 1950s, marked a significant breakthrough in the development of optimal control theory. The principle provided a systematic approach to solving optimal control problems for systems governed by ordinary differential equations.

Over the years，optimal control has evolved and expanded to address a wide range of applications, from spacecraft navigation and robotics to economics and finance. Key contributions have come from various researchers, including Richard Bellman, who introduced the concept of dynamic programming, and Rudolf Kalman，the founder of the Kalman filter.

1. **Calculus of Variations**:
   Calculus of variations is the oldest and most fundamental approach to optimal control. 2. **Dynamic Programming**:

   Pontryagin's Maximum Principle provides a necessary condition for optimality in optimal control problems involving continuous-time systems governed by ordinary differential equations. The principle involves solving a two-point boundary value problem，yielding a set of first-order necessary conditions that must be satisfied by the optimal control law.

#### Continuous-Time Systems

Continuous-time systems are those that evolve over time continuously. In continuous-time systems, the state of the system is described by a set of ordinary differential equations (ODEs), which relate the current state to the inputs and outputs of the system. The general form of a continuous-time system can be expressed as:

\[ \dot{x}(t) = f(x(t), u(t), t) \]
\[ y(t) = g(x(t), t) \]

Where:
- \( x(t) \) is the state vector at time \( t \).
- \( u(t) \) is the control input at time \( t \).
- \( \dot{x}(t) \) is the time derivative of the state vector.
- \( f(x(t), u(t), t) \) is the state dynamics function.
- \( y(t) \) is the output vector at time \( t \).
- \( g(x(t), t) \) is the output function.

The state dynamics function \( f \) captures the relationship between the current state, control input, and time, while the output function \( g \) relates the state to the output.

#### Discrete-Time Systems

Discrete-time systems, on the other hand, evolve over time in discrete steps. These systems are commonly used in digital control systems and are represented by difference equations rather than differential equations. The general form of a discrete-time system can be expressed as:

\[ x(k+1) = f(x(k), u(k)) \]
\[ y(k) = g(x(k)) \]

Where:
- \( x(k) \) is the state vector at time step \( k \).
- \( u(k) \) is the control input at time step \( k \).
- \( x(k+1) \) is the state vector at the next time step.
- \( y(k) \) is the output vector at time step \( k \).
- \( f(x(k), u(k)) \) is the state update function.
- \( g(x(k)) \) is the output function.

The state update function \( f \) determines how the state evolves from one time step to the next based on the current state and control input, while the output function \( g \) relates the state to the output.

#### State-Space Representation

The state-space representation is a mathematical framework that can describe both continuous-time and discrete-time systems using a unified set of equations. This representation is particularly useful for analyzing and designing optimal control laws. The state-space representation is given by:

For continuous-time systems:
\[ \dot{x}(t) = A(t)x(t) + B(t)u(t) \]
\[ y(t) = C(t)x(t) + D(t)u(t) \]

For discrete-time systems:
\[ x(k+1) = A_d x(k) + B_d u(k) \]
\[ y(k) = C_d x(k) + D_d u(k) \]

Where:
- \( A(t) \), \( B(t) \), \( C(t) \), and \( D(t) \) (for continuous-time systems) or \( A_d \), \( B_d \), \( C_d \), and \( D_d \) (for discrete-time systems) are system matrices that define the system dynamics.
- \( x(t) \) and \( y(t) \) are the state vector and output vector, respectively.
- \( u(t) \) is the control input vector.

The state-space representation provides a convenient framework for describing the dynamics of a system and formulating optimal control problems. The system matrices \( A \), \( B \), \( C \), and \( D \) encapsulate the relationships between the state, inputs, and outputs, making it easy to analyze and design control laws.

In summary，mathematical models for continuous-time and discrete-time systems, along with the state-space representation，are essential tools for understanding and analyzing the dynamics of spacecraft in the context of星际导航. Calculus of variations is a mathematical technique used to find functions that minimize or maximize a given functional. The Euler-Lagrange equations are the cornerstone of calculus of variations, providing a set of differential equations that must be satisfied by the optimal control law.

Consider a continuous-time system described by:

\[ \dot{x}(t) = f(x(t), u(t), t) \]
\[ J = \int_{t_0}^{t_f} L(x(t), \dot{x}(t), u(t), t) \, dt \]

Where:

- \( u(t) \) is the control input.

The Euler-Lagrange equations are given by:

\[ \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}(t)} \right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial x(t)} = 0 \]

#### Dynamic Programming

Dynamic programming is a recursive method used to solve optimal control problems by breaking them down into smaller, more manageable subproblems. This method is particularly well-suited for discrete-time systems and problems with finite-time horizons. The core concept in dynamic programming is the Bellman equation, which defines the value function for a given control problem.

Consider a discrete-time system described by:

\[ x(k+1) = f(x(k), u(k)) \]
\[ J(x(k)) = \min_u \left[ g(x(k), u(k)) + \rho(x(k+1)) J(x(k+1)) \right] \]

Where:
- \( x(k) \) is the state vector at time step \( k \).
- \( u(k) \) is the control input at time step \( k \).
- \( g(x(k), u(k)) \) is the cost function for the current time step.
- \( \rho(x(k+1)) \) is the cost-to-go function, representing the cost of reaching the final state from the next state \( x(k+1) \).
- \( J(x(k)) \) is the value function, representing the minimum cost of reaching the final state from the initial state \( x(k) \).

The value function \( J(x(k)) \) is recursively defined as:

\[ J(x(k)) = \min_u \left[ g(x(k), u(k)) + \rho(x(k+1)) J(x(k+1)) \right] \]

By iteratively solving for the value function \( J(x(k)) \)，we can determine the optimal control input \( u(k) \) for each time step \( k \).

#### Pontryagin's Maximum Principle

Pontryagin's Maximum Principle is a necessary condition for optimality in optimal control problems involving continuous-time systems governed by ordinary differential equations. This principle provides a set of first-order necessary conditions that must be satisfied by the optimal control law.

Consider a continuous-time system described by:

\[ \dot{x}(t) = f(x(t), u(t), t) \]
\[ J = \int_{t_0}^{t_f} L(x(t), \dot{x}(t), u(t), t) \, dt \]

The necessary conditions for optimality, as given by Pontryagin's Maximum Principle, are:

\[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial x} - \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}} \right) + \frac{\partial L}{\partial u} = 0 \]

\[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}} \frac{d\dot{x}}{dt} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial u} = 0 \]

\[ \frac{\partial L}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial t} = 0 \]

\[ \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{x}} \right) \leq 0 \]

Where:
- \( L(x(t), \dot{x}(t), u(t), t) \) is the Lagrangian.
- \( \dot{x}(t) \) is the time derivative of the state vector.
- \( u(t) \) is the control input.
- \( \dot{x}(t) \) is the optimal control input.

Pontryagin's Maximum Principle can be solved using various numerical methods, such as shooting methods and collocation methods. The resulting solution provides the optimal control law and the corresponding optimal trajectory.

#### Stochastic Optimal Control

Stochastic optimal control is a branch of optimal control theory that deals with systems that are subject to random disturbances and uncertainties. These disturbances can arise from various sources, such as sensor noise, gravitational perturbations from celestial bodies, and environmental changes. Unlike deterministic optimal control, which assumes a perfectly known environment, stochastic optimal control takes into account the probabilistic nature of the system's dynamics.

**Key Concepts and Methods**

1. **Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs)**:
   Stochastic differential equations are used to model the dynamics of systems that involve random processes. The general form of an SDE is:

   \[ dx(t) = f(x(t), t) dt + g(x(t), t) dw(t) \]

   Where:
   - \( x(t) \) is the state vector.
   - \( f(x(t), t) \) is the drift coefficient, representing the deterministic part of the system dynamics.
   - \( g(x(t), t) \) is the diffusion coefficient, representing the stochastic part of the system dynamics.
   - \( dw(t) \) is the Wiener process, representing the random disturbance.

2. **Adaptive Control**:
   Adaptive control techniques are used to adjust the control inputs in real-time to handle the uncertainties and disturbances in the system. These techniques continuously update the control laws based on real-time measurements and estimates of the system's state and environment.

3. **Filtering and Estimation**:
   Filtering and estimation techniques are used to estimate the state of the system in the presence of noise and disturbances. Common techniques include the Kalman filter and the particle filter.
