**4.2 Mathematical Models and Formulas**

The core algorithms in large model vision understanding are based on mathematical models and formulas. Here, we provide an overview of the key mathematical models and their underlying formulas:

**Neural Networks:**

A neural network consists of multiple layers, each with its own set of weights and biases. The forward pass of a neural network can be represented as:

$$
\text{Output} = \sigma(\text{Weight} \cdot \text{Input} + \text{Bias})
$$

where $\sigma$ is the activation function (e.g., sigmoid, ReLU, or tanh), and $\text{Input}$ and $\text{Weight}$ represent the input to a layer and the weights connecting the input to the output, respectively.

**Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs):**

CNNs are designed to handle grid-like data, such as images. The convolution operation can be represented as:

$$
\text{Convolved Feature} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \text{Weight}_i \cdot \text{Input}_{i,*,*}
$$

where $\text{Weight}_i$ represents the filter weights and $\text{Input}_{i,*,*}$ represents the local region of the input image.

**Transformer Models:**

Transformer models use self-attention mechanisms to process input sequences. The self-attention score can be represented as:

$$
\text{Attention Score} = \text{Score}(\text{Query}, \text{Key}, \text{Value})
$$

where $\text{Query}$, $\text{Key}$, and $\text{Value}$ are the query, key, and value vectors, respectively, and $\text{Score}$ is a scoring function (e.g., dot product or scaled dot product).
