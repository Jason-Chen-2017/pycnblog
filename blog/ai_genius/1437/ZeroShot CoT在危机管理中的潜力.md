##### Section 2.1: Key Concepts

##### Section 2.2: Mathematical Models

**Model Overview:**

Zero-Shot Transfer Learning models can be categorized into two main types: metric-based models and prototype-based models.

**Metric-Based Models:**

Metric-based models compute a distance metric between the features of the source and target domains to determine the similarity between classes. The core idea is that similar classes in the source domain should be close in the feature space, while different classes should be far apart.

**Mathematical Formulation:**

Let \( X_s \) and \( X_t \) be the feature sets of the source and target domains, respectively, and \( C_s \) and \( C_t \) be the corresponding class labels. A metric-based model aims to find a distance function \( d \) that minimizes the within-class variance and maximizes the between-class variance in the feature space.

$$
\min_{\theta} \sum_{c \in C_s} \sum_{x_s \in C_s^c} d(x_s, c)^2 + \sum_{c \in C_t} \sum_{x_t \in C_t^c} d(x_t, c)^2
$$

**Prototype-Based Models:**

Prototype-based models represent each class in the source domain with a prototype (e.g., the centroid or medoid of the class's features) and use these prototypes to predict the class labels of examples in the target domain.

**Mathematical Formulation:**

Let \( \mu_c \) be the prototype of class \( c \) in the source domain, and \( x_t \) be an example in the target domain. The class label prediction is based on the closest prototype:

$$
\hat{y} = \arg\min_{c} d(x_t, \mu_c)
$$
