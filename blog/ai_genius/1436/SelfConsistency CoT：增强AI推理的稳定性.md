### 1.1.2 Basic Concepts and Key Elements

Self-Consistency CoT (Conceptual Consistency Theory) is a theoretical framework that aims to enhance the reasoning capabilities of AI systems by promoting consistency and coherence in their internal representations and decisions. To achieve this, Self-Consistency CoT employs several key mechanisms, including self-evaluation, feedback loops, and iterative refinement. #### 1.1.2.2 Core Principles of Self-Consistency CoT

The core principles of Self-Consistency CoT can be summarized as follows:

1. **Internal Consistency:** The system's reasoning should be consistent with its internal knowledge base. This means that the system's conclusions and inferences should logically follow from its existing beliefs and information.

2. **External Consistency:** The system's reasoning should also be consistent with external observations and data. This ensures that the system's internal representations align with reality.

3. **Self-Evaluation:** The system should continuously evaluate its own reasoning processes to identify inconsistencies and errors. This involves both automated checks and manual validation.

4. **Feedback Loops:** The system should incorporate feedback loops to correct inconsistencies and refine its reasoning over time. This feedback can come from both internal checks and external validation.

5. **Iterative Refinement:** The system should iteratively refine its reasoning processes based on feedback, aiming to improve consistency and accuracy over time.

$$
S_e = f(K_r, K_e)
$$

* \( S_e \) is the self-evaluation score, indicating the degree of consistency between the current reasoning state (\( K_r \)) and the expected reasoning state (\( K_e \)).
* \( f \) is a function that computes the self-evaluation score based on the input knowledge states.
* \( K_r \) is the current reasoning state.
* \( K_e \) is the expected reasoning state.

The self-evaluation function \( f \) can be defined as:

$$
f(K_r, K_e) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot d_i

* \( d_i \) is the difference between the ith knowledge component in the current reasoning state (\( K_r \)) and the expected reasoning state (\( K_e \)).

The self-evaluation score \( S_e \) ranges from 0 to 1, with higher values indicating greater consistency between the current and expected reasoning states.
