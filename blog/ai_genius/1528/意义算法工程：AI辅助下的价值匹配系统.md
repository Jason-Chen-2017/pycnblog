#### Statistical Model: Coefficient of Correlation

One of the simplest yet powerful models for quantifying significance is the coefficient of correlation. This model measures the strength and direction of the linear relationship between two variables. The most commonly used correlation coefficient is the Pearson correlation coefficient, denoted as `r`.

Mathematically, the Pearson correlation coefficient `r` is defined as:

$$
r = \frac{\sum{(x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}}{\sqrt{\sum{(x_i - \bar{x})^2}\sum{(y_i - \bar{y})^2}}}
$$

where `x_i` and `y_i` are the individual data points for variables `x` and `y`, and `\bar{x}` and `\bar{y}` are their respective means.

The value of `r` ranges from -1 to 1. A value of 1 indicates a perfect positive linear relationship, -1 indicates a perfect negative linear relationship, and 0 indicates no linear relationship.

#### Machine Learning Model: Classification and Regression Trees (CART)

Another powerful model for quantifying significance is the Classification and Regression Trees (CART) algorithm, a type of decision tree. CART is particularly useful for non-linear relationships and can handle both classification and regression tasks.

A decision tree is built by recursively splitting the data into subsets based on the values of input features. At each node of the tree, a split is made based on a feature and a threshold value. The process continues until a stopping criterion is met, such as a maximum tree depth or a minimum number of samples per leaf.

The significance of a feature in a decision tree can be quantified using the Gini impurity or information gain measures. For a feature `x_j`, the Gini impurity is defined as:

$$
Gini(x_j) = 1 - \sum_{v \in V} p_v^2
$$

where `V` is the set of possible values of feature `x_j`, and `p_v` is the probability of value `v`.

The information gain for feature `x_j` is defined as the reduction in impurity when the data is split based on `x_j`:

$$
IG(x_j) = H(D) - \sum_{v \in V} p_v H(D_v)
$$

where `H(D)` is the entropy of the dataset `D`, and `H(D_v)` is the entropy of the subset of `D` where `x_j` takes the value `v`.
