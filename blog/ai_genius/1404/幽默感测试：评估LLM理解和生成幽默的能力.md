### Evaluation Theories of Humor Sense

#### 2.1 Concept and Principles of Humor Sense Evaluation

##### 2.1.1 Definition of Humor Sense Evaluation

Humor sense evaluation is the process of measuring and assessing an individual's ability to perceive, appreciate, and generate humor. It involves the application of various methods and tools to evaluate the subject's humor perception, understanding, and production skills.

##### 2.1.2 Principles of Humor Sense Evaluation

Effective humor sense evaluation should adhere to the following principles:

- **Objectivity**: Evaluation should be unbiased and based on objective criteria.
- **Standardization**: Evaluation methods and tools should be standardized to ensure consistency.
- **Reliability**: Evaluation results should be reliable, reproducible, and valid.
- **Practicality**: Evaluation methods should be practical and feasible for real-world applications.
- **Contextualization**: Evaluation should consider the situational and cultural context in which humor is presented.

##### 2.1.3 Selection of Evaluation Methods

Selecting the appropriate evaluation method depends on several factors, including the research objectives, the nature of the humor being assessed, and the target population. Common evaluation methods include self-assessment questionnaires, third-party assessments, and performance-based assessments.

#### 2.2 Common Evaluation Tools for Humor Sense

##### 2.2.1 Self-Assessment Questionnaires

Self-assessment questionnaires involve individuals rating their own humor perception and generation skills. These questionnaires are convenient and cost-effective, but they may suffer from biases and subjectivity. Examples include the Sense of Humor Scale (SHS) and the Humor Styles Questionnaire (HSQ).

##### 2.2.2 Third-Party Evaluations

Third-party evaluations involve independent assessors rating an individual's humor sense. These assessments are more objective and reliable but require more resources and time. Examples include expert ratings and peer evaluations.

##### 2.2.3 Comparison of Evaluation Tools

The following table compares the advantages and disadvantages of different evaluation tools:

| Evaluation Tool           | Advantages                             | Disadvantages                            |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Self-Assessment Questionnaires | Convenient, cost-effective          | Bias, subjectivity                      |
| Third-Party Evaluations   | Objective, reliable                  | Resource-intensive, time-consuming      |
| Performance-Based Assessments | Practical, context-specific         | Subject to evaluation bias              |

#### 2.3 Evaluation Methods Based on Language Models

##### 2.3.1 Application of Language Models in Humor Sense Evaluation

Language models, particularly large language models (LLMs), have been increasingly used in humor sense evaluation. LLMs can analyze and process textual, speech-based, and video-based humor content, providing insights into an individual's humor perception and production skills.

##### 2.3.2 Text-Based Humor Sense Evaluation

Text-based humor sense evaluation involves analyzing an individual's responses to humorous texts generated by LLMs. This method focuses on the ability to understand and appreciate text-based humor. It can be applied to various scenarios, such as humor generation competitions, humor comprehension tests, and humor style analysis.

##### 2.3.3 Speech and Video-Based Humor Sense Evaluation

Speech and video-based humor sense evaluation extends the text-based approach to incorporate auditory and visual elements. This method is particularly useful for assessing humor production skills, as it considers the delivery and expression of humor. Applications include humor improvisation tasks, stand-up comedy performances, and video-based humor contests.

#### 2.4 Metric System for Humor Sense Evaluation

##### 2.4.1 Design of Evaluation Metrics

Evaluation metrics for humor sense should capture the essential aspects of humor perception and production, including humor recognition, appreciation, and generation. The design of evaluation metrics involves defining specific criteria and scoring systems.

##### 2.4.2 Classification of Evaluation Metrics

Evaluation metrics can be classified into several categories, including:

- **Content-based Metrics**: These metrics evaluate the humor content, such as novelty, creativity, and appropriateness.
- **Perception-based Metrics**: These metrics assess the individual's ability to perceive and appreciate humor, such as understanding, enjoyment, and emotional response.
- **Production-based Metrics**: These metrics evaluate the individual's ability to generate humor, such as creativity, originality, and humor quality.

The following table summarizes some common evaluation metrics and their computation methods:

### Evaluating LLM's Understanding and Generation of Humor

#### 3.1 Understanding Humor by LLMs

##### 3.1.1 Challenges in Understanding Humor by LLMs

Understanding humor is a complex task for LLMs due to several factors:

- **Subjectivity**: Humor is highly subjective, and what may be funny to one person might not be to another.
- **Ambiguity**: Humor often relies on ambiguity, irony, and unexpected twists, which can be challenging for LLMs to understand.
- **Contextual Dependence**: Humor is often dependent on the context in which it is presented, which LLMs may struggle to grasp.
- **Cultural Differences**: Humor can vary significantly across cultures, making it difficult for LLMs to generalize their understanding of humor.

##### 3.1.2 Methods for Evaluating LLM's Understanding of Humor

To evaluate LLM's understanding of humor, researchers have employed various methods, including:

- **Text-Based Evaluation**: This method involves presenting LLMs with text-based humor and assessing their ability to comprehend and appreciate it. It can be done using self-assessment questionnaires, expert evaluations, or crowdsourcing platforms.
- **Speech and Video-Based Evaluation**: This method extends the text-based approach to include auditory and visual elements, allowing LLMs to understand humor in different modalities. It can be applied to speech and video data, such as stand-up comedy performances or humorous video clips.
- **Performance-Based Evaluation**: This method involves measuring LLMs' performance on specific tasks related to humor understanding, such as humor comprehension tests or humor style analysis.
