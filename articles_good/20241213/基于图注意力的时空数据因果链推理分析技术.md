Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) are a class of neural networks designed to work directly on graph-structured data. #### 2.1.2 GNN Architecture and Working Principle

GNNs operate by updating the properties of nodes and edges in a graph through a series of message-passing steps. At each step, nodes compute a representation based on the features of their neighbors and the edge connections between them. This process is iterated multiple times to refine the graph representation, capturing the local and global patterns within the data.

Attention mechanisms in GNNs are designed to assign different weights to the information received from neighboring nodes or edges. This allows the network to selectively attend to the most informative parts of the graph, improving the representation quality and reducing the noise.

#### 2.2.2 Types of Attention Mechanisms

2. **Self-Attention**: Also known as intra-attention, self-attention allows the model to weigh the importance of different parts of the input sequence relative to each other.
3. **Convolutional Attention**: This type of attention mechanism uses convolutional filters to capture local patterns in the graph.

### Mathematical Formulation of Graph Attention Mechanism

In order to better understand the Graph Attention Mechanism, we will delve into its mathematical formulation. This will provide a solid foundation for the subsequent discussion on how this mechanism operates and its potential applications in temporal data causality chain reasoning.

#### 2.3.1 Scaled Dot-Product Attention

The Scaled Dot-Product Attention is one of the most commonly used attention mechanisms. It is based on the dot product of query and key vectors, followed by a scaling factor to prevent the overflow issue that can arise from large dot products.

##### Mathematical Formulation

1. **Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) Vectors**:
   - For each node \(i\) in the graph, we have query vector \(Q_i\), key vector \(K_i\), and value vector \(V_i\).
   - These vectors are typically derived from the node features and edge features in the graph.

2. **Attention Scores (Scoring Function)**:
   - The attention scores, also known as attention weights, are computed as the scaled dot-product between the query and key vectors:
     $$ a_{ij} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i \cdot K_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) $$
     where \(a_{ij}\) represents the attention weight between node \(i\) and node \(j\), and \(d_k\) is the dimension of the key vectors.

3. **Attention Output**:
   - The attention output for node \(i\) is obtained by summing the value vectors weighted by their attention scores:
     $$ \hat{h}_i = \sum_{j \in N(i)} a_{ij} V_j $$
     where \(\hat{h}_i\) is the updated representation of node \(i\), and \(N(i)\) denotes the neighbors of node \(i\).

##### Example

Consider a graph with two nodes, \(i\) and \(j\), and their corresponding query, key, and value vectors:
- \(Q_i = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\)
- \(K_j = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\)
- \(V_j = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\)

The attention score between nodes \(i\) and \(j\) would be:
$$ a_{ij} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i \cdot K_j}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{1 \cdot 0}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \text{softmax}(0) = \frac{1}{2} $$

The updated representation of node \(i\) would then be:
$$ \hat{h}_i = a_{ij} V_j = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix} $$

#### 2.3.2 Multi-head Attention

Multi-head attention extends the concept of scaled dot-product attention by allowing the model to attend to different parts of the graph simultaneously. This is achieved by applying the scaled dot-product attention multiple times, each with a different set of learned linear projections.

##### Mathematical Formulation

1. **Multi-head Attention**:
   - For each node \(i\), we compute multiple attention heads \(h\):
     $$ \text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, ..., \text{head}_h)W^O $$
     where \(\text{head}_h = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QW_Q^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)VW_V^T\) and \(W_Q\), \(W_K\), and \(W_V\) are the learned linear projections, and \(W^O\) is the output linear projection.

2. **Combined Output**:
   - The final output for node \(i\) is obtained by concatenating the outputs of all attention heads and passing it through a final linear projection:
     $$ \hat{h}_i = \text{Linear}(\text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V)) $$

##### Example

Suppose we have two attention heads for nodes \(i\) and \(j\):
- Head 1: \(Q_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\), \(K_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), \(V_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\)
- Head 2: \(Q_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), \(K_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\), \(V_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\)

The attention scores for each head would be:
$$ a_{ij}^{(1)} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_1 \cdot K_1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{1}{2} $$
$$ a_{ij}^{(2)} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_2 \cdot K_2}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{1}{2} $$

The final attention output for node \(i\) would then be:
$$ \hat{h}_i = (a_{ij}^{(1)} V_1 + a_{ij}^{(2)} V_2) = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} $$

### Temporal Data Preprocessing

#### 3.2.1 Data Cleaning

Data cleaning is the process of identifying and correcting errors, inconsistencies, and missing values in the temporal data. This step is essential because even small errors or inconsistencies can significantly impact the accuracy and reliability of the analysis.

**Common Data Cleaning Techniques**:

1. **Handling Missing Values**:
   - **Interpolation**: This technique involves estimating the missing values based on the surrounding values. For time series data, linear or spline interpolation can be used.
   - **Regression Imputation**: In this method, a regression model is trained on the available data to predict the missing values. For example, a linear regression model can be used to predict missing stock prices based on past data.
   - **Mean/Median/Mode Imputation**: Replacing missing values with the mean, median, or mode of the available data. This is a simple approach but may not be suitable for all datasets.

2. **Handling Outliers**:
   - Outliers can be detected using statistical methods like the Z-score or IQR (Interquartile Range) method. These outliers can then be handled by either removing them or transforming them to reduce their impact on the analysis.

3. **Handling Anomalies**:
   - Anomalies, which are different from outliers, are unusual patterns or events in the data that do not conform to the expected behavior. Techniques like clustering or isolation forest can be used to identify and handle anomalies.

**Example**:
Consider a stock price dataset with missing values due to trading holidays. Interpolation can be used to estimate the missing prices based on the surrounding prices:
\[ \text{Price}_{\text{missing}} = \frac{\text{Price}_{\text{before}} + \text{Price}_{\text{after}}}{2} \]

#### 3.2.2 Normalization

Normalization is the process of scaling the data to a consistent range. This step is particularly important for time series data, where the values can vary widely over time. Normalization ensures that all data points contribute equally to the analysis and prevents any single variable from dominating the results.

**Common Normalization Techniques**:

1. **Min-Max Scaling**:
   - This technique scales the data to a range between 0 and 1. The minimum and maximum values of the data are used to scale each value:
     \[ x_{\text{scaled}} = \frac{x_{\text{original}} - x_{\text{min}}}{x_{\text{max}} - x_{\text{min}}} \]

2. **Z-Score Normalization**:
   - Also known as standardization, this technique scales the data to have a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1:
     \[ x_{\text{scaled}} = \frac{x_{\text{original}} - \mu}{\sigma} \]
     where \( \mu \) is the mean and \( \sigma \) is the standard deviation of the data.

3. **Decimal Scaling**:
   - This technique involves multiplying the data by a constant factor to shift the decimal point, making the data more manageable:
     \[ x_{\text{scaled}} = x_{\text{original}} \times 10^k \]
     where \( k \) is a constant that shifts the decimal point to the right or left.

**Example**:
Consider a dataset of stock prices ranging from $10 to $100. Applying min-max scaling, we can scale the data to a range between 0 and 1:
\[ \text{Price}_{\text{scaled}} = \frac{\text{Price}_{\text{original}} - 10}{100 - 10} \]

#### 3.2.3 Feature Extraction

Feature extraction is the process of transforming raw data into a set of features that capture the underlying patterns and relationships. This step is crucial for enhancing the quality of the data and improving the performance of the graph attention mechanism.

**Common Feature Extraction Techniques**:

1. **Autoregressive Models**:
   - Autoregressive models, such as ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average), can be used to extract features from time series data by modeling the relationship between past and future values.

2. **Fourier Transforms**:
   - Fourier transforms can be used to decompose the time series data into its constituent frequencies, providing insights into the periodic patterns and trends.

3. **Wavelets**:
   - Wavelets are used to analyze both the time and frequency domains of the data, providing a localized view of the data's structure.

4. **Textual Features**:
   - For event sequence data, textual features can be extracted from the event descriptions or titles using techniques like word embeddings or topic modeling.

5. **Sequence Features**:
   - Sequence features can be extracted by analyzing the order and pattern of events. Techniques like n-gram analysis or sequence clustering can be used to identify important sequences of events.

**Example**:

#### 4.4 Mathematical Model of Graph Attention Mechanism

To understand how graph attention mechanisms operate in temporal data causality chain reasoning, we need to delve into their mathematical formulation. We will focus on the scaled dot-product attention mechanism, which is a common variant.

##### Scaled Dot-Product Attention

1. **Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V) Vectors**:
   - For each node \(i\), we have query vector \(Q_i\), key vector \(K_i\), and value vector \(V_i\).
   - These vectors are typically derived from the node features and edge features in the graph.

2. **Attention Scores (Scoring Function)**:
   - The attention scores, also known as attention weights, are computed as the scaled dot-product between the query and key vectors:
     \[ a_{ij} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i \cdot K_j}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \]
     where \(a_{ij}\) represents the attention weight between node \(i\) and node \(j\), and \(d_k\) is the dimension of the key vectors.

3. **Attention Output**:
   - The attention output for node \(i\) is obtained by summing the value vectors weighted by their attention scores:
     \[ \hat{h}_i = \sum_{j \in N(i)} a_{ij} V_j \]
     where \(\hat{h}_i\) is the updated representation of node \(i\), and \(N(i)\) denotes the neighbors of node \(i\).

##### Example

Consider a graph with two nodes, \(i\) and \(j\), and their corresponding query, key, and value vectors:
- \(Q_i = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\)
- \(K_j = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\)
- \(V_j = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\)

The attention score between nodes \(i\) and \(j\) would be:
\[ a_{ij} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_i \cdot K_j}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \text{softmax}(0) = \frac{1}{2} \]

The updated representation of node \(i\) would then be:
\[ \hat{h}_i = a_{ij} V_j = \frac{1}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 \\ 0.5 \end{bmatrix} \]

##### Multi-Head Attention

The multi-head attention mechanism extends the scaled dot-product attention by allowing the model to attend to different parts of the graph simultaneously.

1. **Multi-Head Attention**:
   - For each node \(i\), we compute multiple attention heads \(h\):
     \[ \text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, ..., \text{head}_h)W^O \]
     where \(\text{head}_h = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QW_Q^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)VW_V^T\) and \(W_Q\), \(W_K\), and \(W_V\) are the learned linear projections, and \(W^O\) is the output linear projection.

2. **Combined Output**:
   - The final output for node \(i\) is obtained by concatenating the outputs of all attention heads and passing it through a final linear projection:
     \[ \hat{h}_i = \text{Linear}(\text{MultiHead}(Q, K, V)) \]

##### Example

Suppose we have two attention heads for nodes \(i\) and \(j\):
- Head 1: \(Q_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\), \(K_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), \(V_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\)
- Head 2: \(Q_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), \(K_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\), \(V_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\)

The attention scores for each head would be:
\[ a_{ij}^{(1)} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_1 \cdot K_1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \]
\[ a_{ij}^{(2)} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q_2 \cdot K_2}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \]

The final attention output for node \(i\) would then be:
\[ \hat{h}_i = (a_{ij}^{(1)} V_1 + a_{ij}^{(2)} V_2) = \begin{bmatrix} 0.5 & 0.5 \end{bmatrix} \]

def scaled_dot_product_attention(Q, K, V):

    scores = np.dot(Q, K.T) / np.sqrt(np.shape(K)[1])
