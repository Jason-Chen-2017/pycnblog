### 2.2 Propagation of Seismic Waves

#### 2.2.1 Definition of Seismic Waves

Seismic waves are waves of energy that propagate through the Earth's crust and surface during an earthquake. There are two main types of seismic waves: body waves (P-waves and S-waves) and surface waves (Love waves and Rayleigh waves). These waves can be modeled using mathematical equations such as:

$$
c_P = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda + 2\mu}{\rho}}
$$

$$
c_S = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\rho}}
$$

where $c_P$ and $c_S$ are the velocities of P-waves and S-waves, $\lambda$ and $\mu$ are the Lame parameters, and $\rho$ is the density of the material.

#### 2.2.2 Propagation Laws of Seismic Waves

Seismic waves propagate through the Earth according to the laws of physics. The wave equation, which is a partial differential equation, describes the propagation of seismic waves. By solving this equation, engineers can predict the behavior of seismic waves in various geological environments.

#### 2.2.3 Types of Seismic Waves

Different types of seismic waves have different propagation characteristics and effects on structures. For example, surface waves can cause significant damage to buildings due to their large amplitudes and long durations.

## Seismic Design Mathematical Models

### 3.1 Mathematical Models for Earthquake Response

#### 3.1.1 Mathematical Formulas for Earthquake Response

The response of a structure to an earthquake can be modeled using mathematical formulas. One common model is the response spectrum, which relates the maximum response of a structure to its natural period. The formula for the response spectrum is:

$$
S_{max}(T) = C_{max} \cdot T_n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
$$

where $S_{max}(T)$ is the maximum response at a given period $T$, $C_{max}$ is the design response coefficient, and $T_n$ is the natural period of the structure.

#### 3.1.2 Calculation Methods for Earthquake Response

To calculate the earthquake response, engineers use numerical methods such as the finite element method (FEM) or the Newmark method. The FEM is particularly useful for complex structures with irregular geometries.

#### 3.1.3 Case Study of Earthquake Response

Consider a high-rise building with a natural period of 2.5 seconds. The design response coefficient for the region is 2.5. Using the response spectrum formula, we can calculate the maximum displacement of the building:

$$
S_{max}(2.5) = 2.5 \cdot 2.5^{-\frac{1}{2}} \approx 1.15
$$

The maximum displacement is approximately 1.15 times the building's height.
