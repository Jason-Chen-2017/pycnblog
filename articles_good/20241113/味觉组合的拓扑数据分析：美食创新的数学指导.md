**4.1.1 Flavor Intensity Model**

One of the fundamental models for gourmet taste combinations is the flavor intensity model. This model calculates the intensity of each flavor component based on the relative contributions of various ingredients. The formula for the flavor intensity model is as follows:

\[ F_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} w_{ij} \cdot F_j \]

Where:
- \( F_i \) represents the intensity of flavor \( i \).
- \( w_{ij} \) is the weight of the relationship between flavor \( i \) and flavor \( j \).
- \( n \) is the total number of flavors considered.

**Explanation**:
The flavor intensity model sums the weighted contributions of all the other flavors to determine the overall intensity of a specific flavor. For example, in a dish that contains sweet, salty, sour, bitter, and umami flavors, the formula could be used to calculate the overall intensity of each flavor based on the weights assigned to each flavor combination.

**Example**:
Consider a dish with the following flavor intensities and weight matrix:
\[ \begin{align*}
F_{\text{sweet}} &= 0.3 \\
F_{\text{salty}} &= 0.2 \\
F_{\text{sour}} &= 0.1 \\
F_{\text{bitter}} &= 0.1 \\
F_{\text{umami}} &= 0.3 \\
w_{\text{sweet, sweet}} &= 0.5 \\
w_{\text{sweet, salty}} &= 0.2 \\
w_{\text{sweet, sour}} &= 0.1 \\
w_{\text{sweet, bitter}} &= 0.1 \\
w_{\text{sweet, umami}} &= 0.1 \\
\end{align*} \]

Using the flavor intensity model, the overall intensity of sweetness in the dish would be calculated as:
\[ F_{\text{total\_sweet}} = w_{\text{sweet, sweet}} \cdot F_{\text{sweet}} + w_{\text{sweet, salty}} \cdot F_{\text{salty}} + w_{\text{sweet, sour}} \cdot F_{\text{sour}} + w_{\text{sweet, bitter}} \cdot F_{\text{bitter}} + w_{\text{sweet, umami}} \cdot F_{\text{umami}} \]

**4.1.2 Texture Profile Model**

Another essential model for gourmet taste combinations is the texture profile model. This model represents the overall texture of a dish by summing the weighted contributions of different textural attributes. The formula for the texture profile model is:

\[ T_p = \sum_{k=1}^{m} v_{kp} \cdot T_k \]

Where:
- \( T_p \) denotes the texture profile.
- \( v_{kp} \) is the weight of the relationship between texture \( k \) and the overall texture profile.
- \( m \) is the total number of textures.

**Explanation**:
The texture profile model sums the weighted contributions of all the textural attributes to determine the overall texture of the dish. For instance, in a dish that contains crunchy, smooth, and viscous textures, the formula can be used to calculate the overall texture based on the weights assigned to each texture.

**Example**:

**4.1.3 Aroma Classification Model**

The aroma classification model is used to determine the dominant aroma in a dish based on its aromatic compounds and their relative weights. The formula for the aroma classification model is:

\[ A_c = \prod_{l=1}^{p} (1 + e^{-\beta_l \cdot A_l}) \]

Where:
- \( A_c \) represents the classification probability of aroma \( l \).
- \( A_l \) is the feature vector of aroma \( l \).
- \( \beta_l \) are the weights corresponding to each aroma feature.
- \( p \) is the total number of aroma types.

**Explanation**:
The aroma classification model uses logistic regression to classify the dominant aroma in a dish. Each aroma type is represented by a feature vector, and the weights determine the contribution of each feature to the overall classification probability.

**Example**:

\[ A_{\text{spicy}} = (0.1, 0.9, 0.0) \]
\[ A_{\text{herbal}} = (0.3, 0.1, 0.6) \]

And the corresponding weights are:
\[ \beta_{\text{fruity}} = 0.5 \]
\[ \beta_{\text{spicy}} = 0.3 \]
\[ \beta_{\text{herbal}} = 0.2 \]

The classification probabilities for each aroma type would be calculated as:
\[ A_{\text{fruity\_prob}} = (1 + e^{-\beta_{\text{fruity}} \cdot A_{\text{fruity}}})^{-1} \]
\[ A_{\text{spicy\_prob}} = (1 + e^{-\beta_{\text{spicy}} \cdot A_{\text{spicy}}})^{-1} \]
\[ A_{\text{herbal\_prob}} = (1 + e^{-\beta_{\text{herbal}} \cdot A_{\text{herbal}}})^{-1} \]

```python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Load the dataset
data = pd.read_csv('gourmet_data.csv')

# Normalize the data
normalized_data = (data - data.mean()) / data.std()

# Apply PCA for dimensionality reduction
pca = PCA(n_components=2)
reduced_data = pca.fit_transform(normalized_data)

# Construct the flavor graph
import networkx as nx
G = nx.Graph()
G.add_nodes_from(range(len(reduced_data)))
G.add_edges_from([(i, j) for i in range(len(reduced_data)) for j in range(i+1, len(reduced_data))])

# Compute persistent homology
st = SimplexTree.from_vector_array(reduced_data)
persistent_pairs = st.pairs()

# Visualize the persistent homology
streamplot(persistent_pairs, reduced_data, ax=plt.gca())
plt.xlabel('PCA Component 1')
plt.ylabel('PCA Component 2')
plt.title('Persistent Homology of Gourmet Data')
plt.show()
```
