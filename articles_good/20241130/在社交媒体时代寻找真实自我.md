def pos_neg_ratio(feedback_list):
    pos_count = sum([1 for text in feedback_list if 'positive' in text])
    neg_count = sum([1 for text in feedback_list if 'negative' in text])
    return pos_count / (neg_count + 1e-6)

# Function to identify emotion indicators

    blob = TextBlob(text)

    pos_neg = pos_neg_ratio(user_data['feedback'])
    emotion = emotion_indicators(user_data['text'])

### 1.1 The Evolution of Social Media and Its Effects on Identity

#### Early Social Media and Their Influence

The inception of social media can be traced back to platforms like Geocities and MySpace in the late 1990s and early 2000s. These platforms allowed users to create personal webpages and share content with friends. Although limited in scope and functionality, they marked the beginning of a shift towards more interactive and user-driven forms of communication.

As these platforms evolved, they started to introduce features that encouraged more direct and immediate interactions, such as chat rooms and forums. This laid the groundwork for the emergence of modern social media platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram, which became ubiquitous within a decade.

#### The Rise of Social Media and Its Consequences

The meteoric rise of social media platforms has transformed the way individuals communicate, share information, and construct their identities. These platforms have become an integral part of our daily lives, with billions of people worldwide active on multiple platforms simultaneously.

One of the significant consequences of this widespread adoption is the impact on identity construction. Users often create digital personas that may differ significantly from their offline selves. This phenomenon, often referred to as the "curated self," has led to concerns about the authenticity of online identities and the potential for increased social anxiety and self-consciousness.

#### Psychological Impacts of Social Media on Youth

Social media's influence on young people's mental health has been a topic of significant concern. Studies have shown that heavy social media use is associated with increased levels of anxiety, depression, and low self-esteem, particularly among adolescents and young adults.

One reason for this is the constant exposure to idealized versions of others' lives, which can lead to social comparison and feelings of inadequacy. The fear of missing out (FOMO) is another psychological consequence, as users may feel the need to constantly keep up with the latest trends and experiences.

### 1.2 The Concept of Authenticity in the Digital Age

#### Defining Authenticity in a Social Media Context

Authenticity, in its simplest form, refers to the quality of being genuine, true, or real. In the context of social media, authenticity involves presenting oneself genuinely and transparently, without pretending to be something or someone else. It encompasses honesty, sincerity, and an open and sincere expression of one's thoughts, feelings, and experiences.

#### Challenges to Authenticity on Social Platforms

Maintaining authenticity on social media platforms can be challenging due to several factors. The desire for validation and acceptance often drives users to conform to societal expectations or to present a more favorable image of themselves. This can lead to the creation of inauthentic personas designed to impress others or to fit within certain social norms.

Another challenge is the pressure to curate content that is visually appealing and engaging. Users may edit their photos, use filters, or craft carefully constructed messages to create a perfect online persona. These practices can create a disparity between one's online and offline selves, making it difficult to maintain authenticity.

#### The Importance of Authenticity in Personal Branding

In the digital age, personal branding has become increasingly important. Authenticity is a crucial component of effective personal branding. When individuals present themselves genuinely, they build trust and credibility, which are essential for establishing a strong personal brand.

An authentic personal brand helps differentiate individuals from competitors and can lead to greater career opportunities and professional success. It also fosters deeper connections with others, as people are more likely to engage with and support those who they perceive as genuine and transparent.

Social media platforms play a significant role in shaping individuals' self-perception. The constant exposure to carefully curated images and lifestyles on social media can influence how people perceive themselves and their place in the world.

#### The Role of Social Media Analytics

#### Key Metrics for Authentic Self-Discovery

4. **Sentiment Analysis:** Analyze the sentiment of comments and feedback to gauge the emotional response of your audience to your content. This can provide insights into the authenticity and effectiveness of your self-presentation.

#### Strategies for Utilizing Social Media Analytics

3. **A/B Testing:** Experiment with different content formats, messages, and strategies to determine what resonates best with your audience and aligns with your authentic self.
4. **Adjust and Iterate:** Based on the insights gained from social media analytics, make data-driven adjustments to your content and strategy to enhance authenticity and engagement.

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

# Example user data
data = {
    'text': ['I had a great day!', 'I hate my life.'],
    'feedback': ['positive', 'negative'],
    'activity': [10, 5]
}

df = pd.DataFrame(data)

# Function to calculate the positive to negative feedback ratio
def pos_neg_ratio(feedback_list):
    pos_count = sum([1 for text in feedback_list if 'positive' in text])
    neg_count = sum([1 for text in feedback_list if 'negative' in text])
    return pos_count / (neg_count + 1e-6)

# Function to identify emotion indicators
def emotion_indicators(text):
    # Example using TextBlob to get sentiment polarity
    blob = TextBlob(text)
    return blob.sentiment.polarity

# Function to analyze behavior consistency
def behavior_consistency(activity_list):
    return np.std(activity_list)

# Calculate indicators
df['PNR'] = df['feedback'].apply(pos_neg_ratio)
df['EI'] = df['text'].apply(emotion_indicators)
df['BC'] = behavior_consistency(df['activity'])

# Weights for the indicators
weights = {'PNR': 0.4, 'EI': 0.3, 'BC': 0.3}

# Calculate the inauthenticity score
df['IA Score'] = df.apply(lambda row: sum([weights[k] * row[k] for k in weights]), axis=1)

**Case Study: The Impact of Social Media on Sarah's Mental Health**

Sarah, a 25-year-old marketing professional, has been using social media platforms like Instagram and Facebook for the past five years. Initially, she used social media to stay connected with friends and share her life experiences. However, as her usage increased, she started to feel anxious and overwhelmed by the constant pressure to maintain an idealized online image.

**Background:**

Sarah's social media activities mainly revolve around posting photos and updates about her daily life, work, and social events. She often edits her photos extensively to make them look perfect, and she carefully crafts her messages to appear positive and engaging. Her posts typically receive a mix of positive and negative feedback from her followers, with some users expressing envy and admiration while others criticize her for appearing too perfect.

**Problem Identification:**

Over time, Sarah began to notice negative effects on her mental health. She felt increasingly anxious about her social media presence and the fear of missing out (FOMO) became a frequent concern. She found herself spending more time on social media, often at the expense of her personal relationships and work. She also experienced heightened self-consciousness and self-doubt, feeling that she could never live up to the idealized images she saw online.

**Analysis:**

Several factors contributed to Sarah's mental health challenges:

1. **Social Comparison:** Sarah frequently compared her life to those of her peers on social media, leading to feelings of inadequacy and dissatisfaction with her own life.

2. **Exposure to Ideals:** Constant exposure to idealized versions of others' lives on social media created unrealistic expectations and a sense of inadequacy.

3. **Overuse and Addiction:** Sarah's excessive use of social media became an addictive behavior, leading to negative consequences in her personal and professional life.

4. **Self-Censorship:** To avoid criticism or negative feedback, Sarah started self-censoring her posts, presenting an inauthentic version of herself online.

**Intervention and Resolution:**

To address her mental health challenges, Sarah implemented several strategies:

1. **Digital Detox:** Sarah began a digital detox, reducing her social media usage to a few minutes a day. This allowed her to regain control over her time and reduce the negative impact of social media on her mental health.

2. **Mindfulness and Self-Reflection:** Sarah practiced mindfulness and self-reflection techniques to become more aware of her social media habits and their impact on her well-being. She started journaling to explore her feelings and thoughts about her online presence.

3. **Realistic Self-Image:** Sarah made a conscious effort to present herself more authentically online, sharing both positive and negative experiences without the need to appear perfect. This shift in her online behavior helped her regain a sense of authenticity and self-acceptance.

4. **Seeking Support:** Sarah sought support from friends, family, and a mental health professional to address her anxiety and self-doubt. Therapy helped her develop coping strategies and build resilience in the face of social media challenges.

**Outcome:**

By implementing these strategies, Sarah successfully improved her mental health and regained control over her social media usage. She felt more empowered to present herself authentically online and experienced a reduction in anxiety and self-consciousness. Her improved well-being extended beyond social media, positively impacting her personal relationships and professional life.

**Lessons Learned:**

Sarah's case highlights the potential negative impact of social media on mental health and the importance of maintaining authenticity. It also demonstrates the benefits of digital detox, mindfulness, and seeking support in addressing social media-related challenges.
