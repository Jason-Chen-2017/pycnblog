### **Background on AI Long-Text Generation**

AI long-text generation has become a prominent area of research and development in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for automated content creation in various domains such as journalism, content marketing, and customer service. Traditional methods of text generation, such as rule-based systems and template-based approaches, have been largely replaced by more advanced machine learning techniques, particularly deep learning models.

One of the most popular deep learning models for text generation is the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). RNNs are capable of processing sequences of data by maintaining a memory of previous inputs, allowing them to generate coherent and contextually relevant text. However, RNNs have several limitations, including vanishing gradient problems, which hinder their ability to capture long-range dependencies in text.

To overcome these limitations, researchers have developed more sophisticated models such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Transformer models. LSTMs address the vanishing gradient problem by using a complex cell state and gate mechanism to maintain information over long sequences. Transformer models, on the other hand, introduce a completely different approach by using self-attention mechanisms to weigh the influence of different parts of the input sequence, enabling them to capture long-range dependencies more effectively.

Despite these advancements, AI-generated long texts often suffer from several issues that affect their quality. One major issue is coherence. Coherence refers to the logical flow and consistency of ideas in a text. AI-generated texts often lack this coherence, resulting in sentences that do not logically follow from one another, creating a disjointed and confusing reading experience.

Another issue is grammatical correctness. While modern AI models are capable of generating grammatically correct sentences, they often fail to maintain a consistent grammatical style and structure throughout the text. This inconsistency can disrupt the reading flow and diminish the overall quality of the generated text.

Finally, relevance is a critical factor in the quality of AI-generated long texts. The generated text should be relevant to the context and provide useful and meaningful information. However, AI models can sometimes generate text that is off-topic or irrelevant, leading to a loss of information and reduced value for the user.

### Theoretical Foundations of Self-Consistency CoT

#### **Mathematical Modeling of Self-Consistency CoT**

Self-Consistency CoT can be understood through a mathematical framework that involves the optimization of a set of parameters to achieve consistent and coherent text generation. The core idea is to train the model such that it minimizes the discrepancy between the generated text and a set of consistency criteria. These criteria can be defined in terms of coherence, grammatical correctness, and relevance.

1. **Coherence Metric:**
   The coherence of a text can be quantified using various metrics, such as sentence-level coherence scores, paragraph-level coherence scores, and overall text coherence scores. These metrics evaluate how logically and semantically connected the sentences and paragraphs are within the text. One common approach is to use lexical coherence measures, such as the University of Stuttgart Coherence Score (USC), which assesses the semantic relatedness between words in a sentence.

   $$ USC(S) = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{i=1}^{|S|} \frac{R(w_i, w_{i+1})}{|w_i| \cdot |w_{i+1}|} $$

   Here, \( S \) represents a sentence, \( w_i \) and \( w_{i+1} \) are consecutive words in the sentence, and \( R(w_i, w_{i+1}) \) is a function that measures the relatedness between \( w_i \) and \( w_{i+1} \).

2. **Grammatical Correctness Metric:**
   Grammatical correctness can be evaluated using syntactic parsing and part-of-speech tagging. A common approach is to use a pre-trained parser to analyze the syntactic structure of the generated text and compare it to a set of grammatical rules. The grammatical correctness metric can be defined as the ratio of correctly parsed sentences to the total number of sentences.

   $$ GC(S) = \frac{P(S)}{|S|} $$

   Here, \( P(S) \) represents the number of sentences in \( S \) that are parsed correctly.

3. **Relevance Metric:**
   The relevance of a text can be assessed using information retrieval techniques, such as Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA) or Word2Vec. These techniques help to identify the semantic similarity between the generated text and a set of relevant documents. The relevance metric can be defined as the cosine similarity between the semantic vector representation of the generated text and the average semantic vector of the relevant documents.

   $$ R(S, D) = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\vec{S} \cdot \vec{D}}{|\vec{S}| \cdot |\vec{D}|} $$

   Here, \( \vec{S} \) and \( \vec{D} \) represent the semantic vector representations of the generated text and the relevant documents, respectively.
