### 1.1 Introduction to the Concept of Self-Consistency

Self-consistency, in its most fundamental sense, refers to the property of a system where the entire system can be explained without contradictions or discrepancies within its own principles or theories. This concept is widely used in various fields, including physics, mathematics, engineering, and computer science, where it plays a crucial role in ensuring the accuracy and reliability of models and theories.

In the context of the cosmic large-scale structure, self-consistency becomes an essential tool for understanding the complex dynamics of the universe. It allows researchers to construct models that are consistent with observational data and theoretical predictions, thereby providing a coherent framework for studying the formation and evolution of cosmic structures.

To delve deeper into the concept of self-consistency, let's start with its definition. Self-consistency can be understood as a property of a system where the system's behavior, predictions, and conclusions are consistent with each other. This means that if a system is self-consistent, any predictions or conclusions derived from the system's principles or theories should logically follow from those principles without any contradictions or discrepancies.

The definition of self-consistency can be formalized in mathematical terms. Consider a system described by a set of equations or principles, T. A system is self-consistent if the following conditions are met:

1. The system's equations or principles are consistent with each other, meaning there are no logical contradictions or inconsistencies.
2. The system's predictions or conclusions derived from these equations or principles are consistent with observational data or experimental results.

In other words, a self-consistent system is one where the overall behavior of the system can be explained without contradictions or discrepancies within its own principles or theories. This is a crucial property for any scientific theory or model, as it ensures that the model is reliable and accurate.

Self-consistency is particularly important in the field of cosmology because the study of the cosmic large-scale structure involves complex systems and phenomena that are challenging to understand.
