### Chapter 2: Friedrich Scheler's Philosophy

#### 2.1 Biography of Friedrich Scheler

Friedrich Scheler (1874-1928) was a German philosopher known for his significant contributions to value philosophy. Born in Munich, Scheler was deeply influenced by the works of Wilhelm Dilthey, who introduced him to the concept of hermeneutics. Scheler's philosophical journey was shaped by his interactions with other prominent philosophers such as Max Weber and Edmund Husserl.

Scheler's intellectual pursuits were characterized by his exploration of the nature of values, emotions, and social justice. His works, such as "The Nature of Sympathy," "The Theory of Social Justice," and "Formal Ethics and Non-Formal Ethics of Values," laid the foundation for his influential ideas in value philosophy. Scheler's philosophy emphasized the role of emotions in ethical decision-making and the importance of interpersonal relationships in shaping moral values.

#### 2.2 Key Concepts in Scheler's Work

##### 2.2.1 Value Sensation and Values

One of Scheler's fundamental contributions to value philosophy is his concept of value sensation. According to Scheler, value sensation is the immediate perception of values, which occurs independently of reason or rationality. This concept challenges traditional philosophical views that prioritize rationality and logic in ethical decision-making.

Scheler distinguished between intrinsic and extrinsic values. Intrinsic values are those that have value in themselves and are independent of human consciousness, while extrinsic values are those that have value only in relation to human needs and desires. This distinction is crucial for understanding the nature of values and their role in human life.

##### 2.2.2 Ethics of Obligation and Ethics of Responsibility

Scheler also made significant contributions to ethical theory by distinguishing between ethics of obligation and ethics of responsibility. Ethics of obligation focuses on the duties and responsibilities that individuals have towards society and their fellow human beings. It is based on the idea that individuals are bound by moral rules and principles that prescribe certain actions and behaviors.

In contrast, ethics of responsibility emphasizes the individual's capacity to make free and autonomous decisions, taking responsibility for the consequences of their actions. Scheler argued that ethics of responsibility is more important than ethics of obligation, as it requires individuals to go beyond mere adherence to rules and to engage in ethical deliberation and decision-making.

#### 2.3 Critical Evaluation of Scheler's Theories

##### 2.3.1 Contributions and Controversies

Scheler's theories have had a profound impact on value philosophy, challenging traditional moral theories and opening new avenues for understanding human behavior. His emphasis on the role of emotions in ethical decision-making has been particularly influential, as it offers a more nuanced and comprehensive view of human morality.

However, Scheler's theories have also been subject to criticism. Some philosophers argue that his emphasis on value sensation and the immediacy of values is overly subjective and undermines the role of rationality in ethical deliberation. Others question the practical implications of his distinction between ethics of obligation and ethics of responsibility, arguing that it may lead to ethical relativism and moral confusion.

##### 2.3.2 Influence on Subsequent Philosophers

Despite the controversies, Scheler's contributions to value philosophy have had a lasting impact on subsequent philosophers. His ideas have influenced a wide range of thinkers, including scholars in moral philosophy, social ethics, and political philosophy. His emphasis on the role of emotions and the importance of interpersonal relationships in shaping moral values continues to be relevant in contemporary discussions of ethics and morality.

Scheler's work also paved the way for subsequent developments in the field of hermeneutics, which explores the interpretation of human experience and culture. His ideas have been particularly influential in contemporary philosophical hermeneutics, as scholars continue to explore the relationship between language, interpretation, and meaning.

##### 3.2.2 Hermeneutics

### Chapter 4: Max Scheler and the Ethics of Emotions

#### 4.1 The Role of Emotions in Ethics

Max Scheler (1874-1928) was a German philosopher whose work significantly contributed to the understanding of emotions in ethics. Scheler argued that emotions play a fundamental role in moral judgment and ethical behavior. Contrary to the rationalist perspective that prioritizes reason over emotion, Scheler posited that emotions are integral to our moral experience and provide a foundation for ethical decision-making.

For Scheler, emotions are not merely physiological responses but have a deep ethical significance. They are the immediate perception of values and guide individuals in their moral choices. Emotions such as love, compassion, and indignation are not merely personal feelings but are connected to objective moral values and contribute to the formation of moral character.

#### 4.2 Scheler's Theory of the Ethics of Emotions

##### 4.2.1 Affective Ethics

Scheler's theory of the ethics of emotions is often referred to as affective ethics. Affective ethics emphasizes the role of emotions in moral judgment and behavior, challenging the dominant rationalist tradition in ethics. Scheler argued that emotions are the primary source of moral values and that they provide a direct connection between individuals and the world of values.

According to Scheler, there are different types of emotions, each associated with specific moral values. For example, love and compassion are associated with the value of benevolence, while indignation and anger are associated with the value of justice. Scheler believed that these emotions are not just feelings but have objective moral content and guide individuals towards moral actions.

##### 4.2.2 Emotional Dispositions and Moral Behavior

Scheler also explored the concept of emotional dispositions, which are enduring emotional tendencies that shape an individual's character and moral behavior. Emotional dispositions are not static but can change over time through moral education and experience. Scheler believed that the cultivation of positive emotional dispositions, such as compassion and humility, is essential for moral development and the formation of a virtuous character.

Scheler's theory of emotional dispositions has important implications for moral education and the cultivation of ethical behavior. By recognizing the role of emotions in moral judgment and behavior, educators can develop strategies that promote the development of positive emotional dispositions and enhance moral reasoning.

#### 4.3 Critiques and Reassessments of Scheler's Ethical Theory

While Scheler's theory of the ethics of emotions has been influential, it has also faced criticism and reassessment. Some philosophers argue that Scheler's emphasis on emotions can lead to moral subjectivism and relativism. By prioritizing emotions over reason, critics argue that Scheler's theory risks undermining the objective foundations of morality.

Others have argued that Scheler's theory overlooks the role of reason in moral judgment and that emotions alone cannot provide a complete account of ethical behavior. These critiques have led to further discussions and refinements of Scheler's ideas, with some philosophers attempting to integrate his insights into a more comprehensive moral theory.

Despite these criticisms, Scheler's theory of the ethics of emotions continues to be an important contribution to the field of ethics. It highlights the complexity and richness of moral experience and the importance of emotions in guiding ethical behavior. By recognizing the role of emotions in moral judgment, Scheler's theory offers a more nuanced and comprehensive understanding of human morality.

#### 2.1 Biography of Friedrich Scheler

Friedrich Scheler (1874-1928) was a German philosopher known for his significant contributions to value philosophy. Scheler's philosophical journey was shaped by his interactions with other prominent philosophers such as Max Weber and Edmund Husserl.

#### 2.2 Key Concepts in Scheler's Work

##### 2.2.1 Value Sensation and Values

One of Scheler's fundamental contributions to value philosophy is his concept of value sensation. According to Scheler, value sensation is the immediate perception of values, which occurs independently of reason or rationality. This concept challenges traditional philosophical views that prioritize rationality and logic in ethical decision-making.

Scheler distinguished between intrinsic and extrinsic values. Intrinsic values are those that have value in themselves and are independent of human consciousness, while extrinsic values are those that have value only in relation to human needs and desires. This distinction is crucial for understanding the nature of values and their role in human life.

##### 2.2.2 Ethics of Obligation and Ethics of Responsibility

Scheler also made significant contributions to ethical theory by distinguishing between ethics of obligation and ethics of responsibility. Ethics of obligation focuses on the duties and responsibilities that individuals have towards society and their fellow human beings. It is based on the idea that individuals are bound by moral rules and principles that prescribe certain actions and behaviors.

In contrast, ethics of responsibility emphasizes the individual's capacity to make free and autonomous decisions, taking responsibility for the consequences of their actions. Scheler argued that ethics of responsibility is more important than ethics of obligation, as it requires individuals to go beyond mere adherence to rules and to engage in ethical deliberation and decision-making.
