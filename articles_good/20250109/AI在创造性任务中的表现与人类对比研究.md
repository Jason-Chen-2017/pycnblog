#### Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)

Generative adversarial networks are a class of machine learning models that consist of two neural networks—Generator and Discriminator—training each other to generate realistic data. The Generator creates new data instances that mimic the distribution of the training data, while the Discriminator evaluates the authenticity of the generated data against real data. The training process is designed to maximize the difference between the Discriminator's ability to distinguish between real and generated data, pushing the Generator to produce more realistic outputs.

#### Algorithm Steps

1. **Initialize the Generator and Discriminator**: Both networks are randomly initialized and trained simultaneously.
2. **Generate Data**: The Generator creates new data instances by sampling from a noise distribution.
3. **Evaluate Data**: The Discriminator evaluates the generated data and real data to determine their authenticity.
4. **Update Networks**: The Generator and Discriminator are updated based on their performance in the previous step.
5. **Repeat**: Steps 2-4 are repeated for multiple iterations until the Generator produces high-quality outputs.

#### Mathematical Models

The core of GANs lies in the mathematical models that govern the interaction between the Generator and the Discriminator. The following are the key mathematical models and equations used in GANs:

1. **Generator Loss**: The loss function for the Generator is designed to minimize the likelihood that the Discriminator will identify the generated data as fake.
   $$ L_G = -\log(D(G(z))) $$
   where \( G(z) \) is the generated data and \( D \) is the Discriminator.

2. **Discriminator Loss**: The loss function for the Discriminator aims to maximize its ability to distinguish between real and generated data.
   $$ L_D = -\log(D(x)) - \log(1 - D(G(z))) $$
   where \( x \) is the real data.

3. **Minimax Objective**: The overall objective of GANs is to minimize the generator loss and maximize the discriminator loss, which leads to an equilibrium where the Generator produces high-quality outputs that are indistinguishable from real data.
   $$ \min_G \max_D L_D $$
   
4. **Gradient Descent**: Both the Generator and the Discriminator use gradient descent to update their weights based on the losses. The gradients are calculated as follows:
   $$ \theta_G \leftarrow \theta_G - \alpha \nabla_{\theta_G} L_G $$
   $$ \theta_D \leftarrow \theta_D - \beta \nabla_{\theta_D} L_D $$
   where \( \theta_G \) and \( \theta_D \) are the parameters of the Generator and the Discriminator, and \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) are the learning rates.
