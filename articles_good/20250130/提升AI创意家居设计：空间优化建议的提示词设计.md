#### 3.2.1 Mathematical Foundations: Formulating Space Optimization Problems

The space optimization problem in home design can be formulated as an optimization problem that aims to maximize the utility of the available space while adhering to various constraints. The core components of this mathematical formulation include the objective function, decision variables, and constraints.

**Objective Function**

The objective function quantifies the goal of space optimization, which is typically to minimize wasted space or to maximize the utilization of functional areas. A common objective function for space optimization can be defined as:

$$
\text{Minimize} \quad P = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(1 - x_i \cdot A_i\right)
$$

Here, \( P \) represents the total penalty for wasted space, \( x_i \) is a binary decision variable indicating whether area \( i \) is utilized (1 if utilized, 0 if not), and \( A_i \) is the area of area \( i \). The goal is to minimize \( P \), which is achieved by maximizing the utilization of each area \( i \).

**Decision Variables**

The decision variables in this formulation are binary variables that represent the allocation of space to different functions or activities within the home. For a home with \( n \) rooms or functional areas, the decision variables \( x_i \) define whether each area \( i \) is assigned to its intended purpose (1) or left unallocated (0).

**Constraints**

The constraints define the limitations and requirements that must be satisfied by the solution. Common constraints in space optimization for home design include:

1. **Room Dimension Constraints**:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \cdot A_i \leq R
$$

Here, \( R \) is the total available space in the home, ensuring that the sum of the utilized areas does not exceed the total available space.

2. **User Preference Constraints**:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \cdot P_i \geq U
$$

Here, \( P_i \) represents the user preference score for area \( i \), and \( U \) is the minimum cumulative preference score required. This constraint ensures that the optimized design meets the user's minimum preferences.

3. **Non-negativity Constraints**:
$$
x_i \geq 0 \quad \forall i = 1, 2, ..., n
$$

This constraint ensures that all decision variables are non-negative, reflecting the binary nature of the allocation problem.

**Combining the Objective and Constraints**

The complete mathematical formulation of the space optimization problem can be expressed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Minimize} \quad P &= \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(1 - x_i \cdot A_i\right) \\
\text{Subject to} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \cdot A_i &\leq R \\
\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \cdot P_i &\geq U \\
x_i &\geq 0 \quad \forall i = 1, 2, ..., n
\end{aligned}
$$

This formulation provides a clear mathematical representation of the space optimization problem, allowing for the application of various optimization algorithms to find the optimal solution.
