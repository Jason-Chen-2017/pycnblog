**Mathematical Model and Formulas**

The mathematical models and formulas used in MCT are critical for understanding how these algorithms operate. Here, we provide an overview of the key models and their associated formulas:

1. **Perception Algorithms**

   - **Sigmoid Function**:
     $$ f(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}} $$
     This function is used in various neural networks for activation purposes.

   - **Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) Filters**:
     $$ \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i * x_i + b $$
     This formula represents the dot product of the filter weights ($w_i$) and the input features ($x_i$), plus a bias term ($b$).

2. **Reasoning Algorithms**

   - **Logic Inference**:
     $$ P(A \wedge B) = P(A) \cdot P(B|A) $$
     This formula represents the probability of both events A and B occurring, based on conditional probabilities.

   - **Decision Tree**:
     $$ G(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot f_i $$
     Here, $G(n)$ is the total goodness of a decision tree, with $w_i$ being the weights and $f_i$ the features.

3. **Memory Algorithms**

   - **Hash Function**:
     $$ hash(x) = x \cdot a \mod m $$
     This formula is used to compute the hash value of an input, where $a$ is the hashing constant and $m$ is the size of the hash table.

   - **Bloom Filter**:
     $$ \sum_{i=1}^{k} b_i \cdot h_i(x) + 1 $$
     This formula generates a binary vector with hash functions $h_i(x)$ to determine if an element is likely in a set.

4. **Learning Algorithms**

   - **Supervised Learning (Linear Regression)**:
     $$ y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot x + \epsilon $$
     This is the linear regression equation, where $y$ is the predicted output, $x$ is the input feature, $\beta_0$ and $\beta_1$ are the model parameters, and $\epsilon$ is the error term.

   - **Reinforcement Learning (Q-Learning)**:
     $$ Q(s, a) = Q(s, a) + \alpha \cdot (r + \gamma \cdot \max_{a'} Q(s', a')) - Q(s, a) $$
     This is the Q-learning update rule, where $Q(s, a)$ is the expected return of taking action $a$ in state $s$, $r$ is the reward, $\gamma$ is the discount factor, and $\alpha$ is the learning rate.

**Probability and Statistics Models**

1. **Normal Distribution**:
   $$ N(\mu, \sigma^2) $$
   The normal distribution, also known as the Gaussian distribution, is a continuous probability distribution characterized by its mean ($\mu$) and variance ($\sigma^2$). It is widely used in statistical analysis for modeling random variables.

   **Example**: Suppose we have a dataset of blood pressure measurements with a mean of 120 mmHg and a standard deviation of 10 mmHg. The probability of measuring a blood pressure value less than 130 mmHg can be calculated using the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the normal distribution:
   $$ P(X < 130) = \Phi\left(\frac{130 - 120}{10}\right) $$
   where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF.

2. **Hypothesis Testing**:
   $$ H_0: \mu = \mu_0 \quad \text{vs.} \quad H_1: \mu \neq \mu_0 $$
   Hypothesis testing is a statistical method for making decisions based on data. It involves setting up a null hypothesis ($H_0$) and an alternative hypothesis ($H_1$), and then using statistical tests to determine the likelihood of the null hypothesis being true.

   **Example**: In a clinical trial, the null hypothesis might be that a new drug has no effect on blood pressure, while the alternative hypothesis is that it does. A t-test can be used to compare the mean blood pressure of patients receiving the drug to a control group.

3. **Regression Analysis**:
   $$ y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \cdot x + \epsilon $$
   Regression analysis is used to understand the relationship between a dependent variable ($y$) and one or more independent variables ($x$). Linear regression, for instance, is commonly used to predict continuous outcomes.

   **Example**: Suppose we want to predict patient recovery time based on the duration of hospital stays. The regression equation might be:
   $$ Recovery\ Time = 5 + 0.2 \cdot Hospital\ Stay $$
   where the coefficient 0.2 indicates that for each additional day in the hospital, the recovery time increases by 0.2 days.

**Machine Learning Models**

1. **Neural Networks**:
   Neural networks are computational models inspired by the structure and function of the human brain. They consist of layers of interconnected nodes (neurons) that process input data and produce output.

   **Example**:
   $$ z = \sigma(\sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \cdot x_i + b) $$
   where $\sigma$ is the activation function, $w_i$ are the weights, $x_i$ are the inputs, and $b$ is the bias term. A simple neural network can be implemented in Python using libraries like TensorFlow or PyTorch.

2. **Support Vector Machines (SVM)**:
   SVMs are a type of supervised learning model used for classification and regression analysis. They identify the hyperplane that best separates the data into different classes.

   **Example**:
   $$ \max_{\mathbf{w}, b} \left\{ \frac{1}{2} ||\mathbf{w}||^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \alpha_i y_i (\mathbf{w} \cdot \mathbf{x_i} - b) \right\} $$
   This is the optimization problem for SVMs, where $\alpha_i$ are the Lagrange multipliers and $\mathbf{x_i}$ are the input data points.

**Optimization Models**

1. **Genetic Algorithms**:

   **Example**:
