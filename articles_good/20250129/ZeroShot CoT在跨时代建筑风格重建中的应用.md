### Mathematical Model and Formulas

The Zero-Shot CoT algorithm is grounded in several mathematical models and formulas that facilitate the generation and optimization of architectural designs. These models leverage advanced techniques like few-shot learning, transfer learning, and meta-learning to achieve high accuracy and adaptability. Below, we explore the key mathematical principles and their representations.

#### Few-Shot Learning Model

Few-shot learning models are designed to train efficiently on small datasets. A common approach is to use neural networks with a small number of layers and fewer parameters to prevent overfitting. The basic mathematical model for a few-shot learning neural network can be represented as:

$$
y = \sigma(W_n \cdot x + b_n)
$$

Where:
- \( y \) is the predicted output.
- \( \sigma \) is the activation function, typically a sigmoid or ReLU.
- \( W_n \) is the weight matrix of the neural network.
- \( b_n \) is the bias term.
- \( x \) is the input feature vector.

#### Transfer Learning Model

Transfer learning leverages pre-trained models on large datasets to improve performance on similar tasks. A typical transfer learning setup involves taking a pre-trained model and adding a new layer or network on top for the specific task. The mathematical representation is:

$$
y = \sigma(W_t \cdot (f(x) + b_t))
$$

Where:
- \( f(x) \) is the output of the pre-trained network.
- \( W_t \) is the weight matrix of the new layer added for transfer learning.
- \( b_t \) is the bias term of the new layer.

#### Meta-Learning Model

Meta-learning focuses on training models that can quickly adapt to new tasks. Models like MAML (Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning) optimize the initial weights to minimize the update step required for new tasks. The meta-learning objective can be defined as:

$$
\min_{\theta} \sum_{\tau} \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}} \|\theta - \phi_t(\theta; \alpha_t)\|^2
$$

Where:
- \( \theta \) are the initial model parameters.
- \( \phi_t(\theta; \alpha_t) \) is the updated parameters after interacting with task \( t \).
- \( \alpha_t \) is the learning rate for task \( t \).
- \( \mathcal{T} \) is the set of tasks.

#### Design Generation and Optimization

Design generation and optimization involve generating initial designs and iteratively refining them based on evaluation metrics. The optimization process often uses gradient descent to minimize a loss function. The general optimization equation is:

$$
\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \alpha \cdot \nabla_{\theta_t} J(\theta_t)
$$

Where:
- \( \theta_{t+1} \) are the updated parameters.
- \( \theta_t \) are the current parameters.
- \( \alpha \) is the learning rate.
- \( \nabla_{\theta_t} J(\theta_t) \) is the gradient of the loss function with respect to the parameters.
- \( J(\theta_t) \) is the loss function, typically the difference between the predicted design and the target design.

These mathematical models and formulas form the backbone of the Zero-Shot CoT algorithm, enabling the generation and optimization of detailed architectural designs based on limited input information.
