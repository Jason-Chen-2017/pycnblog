### 2.1.1 Definition and Scope

Copyright law is a legal framework that protects the rights of creators over their original works. It grants the creator exclusive rights to reproduce, distribute, perform, display, and create derivative works based on their original creation. In the context of AIGC, copyright law plays a crucial role in determining the ownership and control of AI-generated content.

The scope of copyright law is vast and covers various types of works, including:

- **Literary Works**: This includes books, articles, stories, and other written content.
- **Visual Arts**: This encompasses paintings, sculptures, photographs, and other visual creations.
- **Performing Arts**: This involves dramatic, literary, and musical works performed by individuals or groups.
- **Sound Recordings**: This includes audio recordings, such as music albums, podcasts, and other audio content.
- **Broadcasting**: This covers the transmission of audiovisual content via radio, television, or the internet.

### 2.1.2 Basic Principles and Concepts

Several key principles and concepts underpin copyright law:

#### Originality

Originality is a fundamental requirement for a work to be protected by copyright. A work is considered original if it is the result of the author's creativity and not a mere copy of another work. In the context of AIGC, the originality of AI-generated works is a contentious issue. While AI-generated content may exhibit a high degree of creativity, it raises questions about whether it can be considered original in the traditional sense.

#### Ownership

Ownership refers to the rights that an individual or entity holds over a work. Typically, the creator of a work is the initial owner of the copyright. However, ownership can be transferred to another party through licensing agreements or sales. In the case of AI-generated content, determining ownership can be complicated, as AI systems themselves do not have legal personhood.

#### Reproduction and Distribution

Reproduction involves creating a copy of a work, while distribution refers to making the work available to the public. Copyright law grants the creator exclusive rights to reproduce and distribute their work, preventing others from making unauthorized copies or distributing the work without permission.

#### Public Performance and Display

Public performance involves presenting a work to an audience, while display involves showing a work to the public. Copyright law gives the creator the exclusive right to perform and display their work publicly, ensuring that they can control how and when their work is presented to others.

#### Derivative Works

A derivative work is a new creation based on an existing work. Copyright law allows the creator to create derivative works while retaining control over the original work. This includes adaptations, translations, and other transformative uses of the original work.

### 2.1.3 International Copyright Law

Copyright law varies across different countries and regions. However, international agreements and treaties aim to harmonize copyright laws and provide a level of protection that transcends national boundaries.

One of the most significant international agreements is the Berne Convention for the Protection of Literary and Artistic Works, which was established in 1886. The Berne Convention outlines the minimum standards of protection that countries must provide to foreign works. It emphasizes the principle of "national treatment," which means that foreign works must be treated no less favorably than national works.

Another key international agreement is the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) Copyright Treaty, which was adopted in 1996. This treaty addresses the challenges posed by the digital environment and provides additional protection for digital works, such as online databases and multimedia content.

In addition to these agreements, the European Union has implemented the Directive on Copyright in the Digital Single Market, which aims to harmonize copyright laws across member states and address new challenges arising from the digital economy.

#### 2.2.1 Originality in AIGC Works

One of the most challenging aspects of copyright law in the context of AIGC is determining the originality of AI-generated works. Traditional copyright law relies on the principle of originality, which requires a work to be the result of the author's creativity. However, the creative process in AI-generated works is fundamentally different from that of human creators.

**AI-generated art**: For instance, an AI-generated painting may exhibit a high level of creativity, but it is produced through algorithms and data, not through human intuition and inspiration. The question then becomes whether the AI's ability to generate novel and aesthetically pleasing images constitutes originality in the eyes of the law.

**AI-generated texts**: Similarly, AI-generated texts, such as articles, stories, or code, may be highly creative but lack the personal touch and perspective that human authors bring to their work. The challenge here is to define what level of creativity is necessary for a work to be considered original.

**Courts' Approach**: Some courts have taken a broader view of originality, focusing on the end product rather than the process. For example, in the case of paintings created by AI, courts may consider the final artwork's aesthetic value and uniqueness as evidence of originality. However, this approach is not universally accepted, and the question of originality remains a hotly debated topic.

#### 2.2.2 Ownership and Transfer of Rights

Determining ownership and transferring rights in AIGC works also pose unique challenges. Traditional copyright law assumes that the creator is the initial owner of the work, but this assumption is less clear in the context of AI-generated content.

**Ownership of AI-generated works**: The ownership of AI-generated works is often unclear, particularly when multiple parties are involved, such as AI developers, data providers, and the entity deploying the AI. Some legal systems, like the European Union, have adopted the "make or authorize" rule, which states that the person who makes or authorizes the creation of a work is the initial owner. However, this rule does not account for the complexity of AI-generated works, where the "creator" is not a human individual but a machine.

**Transfer of rights**: Transferring ownership of AI-generated works also presents challenges. Licensing agreements are common in the traditional creative industry, but they need to be adapted to the unique characteristics of AI-generated content. For instance, how can a licensing agreement cover all possible uses of AI-generated works, given the potential for unforeseen applications and interpretations?

#### 2.2.3 Moral Rights and the Rights of Publicity

Moral rights and the rights of publicity are additional dimensions of copyright law that come into play in the context of AIGC.

**Moral rights**: Moral rights are personal rights that attach to a work and are distinct from its economic rights. These rights include the right to be recognized as the author of the work and the right to prevent any distortion, mutilation, or other modification of the work that would be prejudicial to the author's honor or reputation. In the case of AI-generated works, the question arises as to whether moral rights can be assigned or transferred, and if so, how.

**Rights of publicity**: The rights of publicity allow individuals to control the commercial use of their name, image, or likeness. In the context of AIGC, these rights are particularly relevant when AI generates content that includes personal information or images. Determining who holds these rights and how they can be enforced requires careful consideration of privacy laws and ethical considerations.

In conclusion, the specific issues surrounding originality, ownership, and moral rights in the context of AIGC are complex and challenging. The traditional frameworks of copyright law need to be adapted to accommodate the unique characteristics of AI-generated works. By addressing these issues in a balanced and thoughtful manner, we can ensure that both the rights of creators and the benefits of AI innovation are protected. ## Ethical Foundations in AIGC
