## 2. Theoretical Framework of Self-Consistency CoT

### 2.1 Coreferral Theory Basics

Coreference resolution is a fundamental task in natural language processing (NLP) that aims to identify when and where two or more mentions in a text refer to the same entity. This task is crucial in various applications, including machine translation, information extraction, and question answering, as well as in legal document generation. Understanding coreference resolution is essential for comprehending the underlying principles of Self-Consistency Coreferral Theory (CoT).

#### Definition and Scope

At its core, coreference resolution is about linking different mentions of the same entity within a text. An entity can be a person, place, organization, or any other object of discussion. For example, in the sentence "John bought a car and he drove it home," the pronoun "he" refers back to "John." Identifying such references is the goal of coreference resolution.

Coreference resolution can be classified into two main categories:

- **Syntactic Coreference Resolution:** This approach relies on syntactic structures, such as noun phrases (NPs) or verb phrases (VPs), to identify coreferences. Techniques include parsing, part-of-speech tagging, and dependency parsing.

- **Semantic Coreference Resolution:** This approach focuses on the meaning and context of words and phrases to identify coreferences. It involves understanding the semantic relationships between entities and their mentions. Techniques include word sense disambiguation, knowledge bases, and machine learning models.

#### The Role of Coreferral in Legal Document Generation

In legal document generation, coreference resolution plays a crucial role in ensuring the coherence and consistency of the generated text. Legal documents are often structured in a way that requires references to entities to be consistent throughout the document. For example, in a contract, if a clause mentions a party, subsequent clauses should consistently refer to that party without ambiguity.

Here are some key roles of coreference resolution in legal document generation:

- **Reference Tracking:** Coreference resolution helps in tracking references to entities, such as parties, locations, dates, and terms, throughout the document. This ensures that the references remain consistent and accurate.

- **Ambiguity Resolution:** Legal documents can contain ambiguous references, which can lead to misunderstandings and legal disputes. Coreference resolution helps in resolving such ambiguities by identifying the correct entities to which the references point.

- **Clarity and Coherence:** Consistent and clear references enhance the readability and coherence of legal documents, making them easier to understand and follow.

#### Challenges in Coreference Resolution for Legal Documents

While coreference resolution is a well-studied problem in NLP, it poses unique challenges when applied to legal documents:

- **Domain-Specific Language:** Legal language is often highly specialized and contains many domain-specific terms and phrases. This requires coreference resolution systems to have a deep understanding of legal terminology and conventions.

- **Ambiguity and Disambiguation:** Legal documents can contain ambiguous references due to the use of pronouns, vague terms, and synonyms. Effective coreference resolution requires the system to disambiguate these references accurately.

- **Contextual Understanding:** Legal documents require a high level of contextual understanding to resolve references correctly. This includes understanding the intent behind legal clauses and the relationships between different entities and clauses.

In conclusion, coreference resolution is a fundamental concept in NLP that plays a vital role in legal document generation. By ensuring the consistency and coherence of references, coreference resolution helps in creating accurate, clear, and reliable legal documents.

## 3. Algorithm and Mathematical Models for Self-Consistency CoT

### 3.1 Algorithm Overview

The Self-Consistency Coreferral Theory (CoT) algorithm is designed to ensure the self-consistency of legal documents by resolving coreferences and maintaining coherence throughout the text. The algorithm follows a multi-step process that includes entity identification, reference resolution, and consistency checking. Here is an overview of the algorithm's workflow:

1. **Input Processing**: The algorithm takes a legal document as input, which can be in the form of plain text or structured data.

2. **Entity Identification**: The first step involves identifying all entities within the document. This is achieved using a combination of named entity recognition (NER) techniques and domain-specific rules. The identified entities are stored in a data structure that maps each entity to its attributes and mentions.

3. **Reference Resolution**: Once entities are identified, the algorithm proceeds to resolve references within the document. This step involves analyzing the context and semantic relationships between entities and their mentions to determine the correct entity to which each reference points.

4. **Consistency Checking**: After resolving references, the algorithm performs a consistency check to ensure that the document remains internally consistent. This involves checking for contradictions and inconsistencies in temporal references, logical flow, and entity references.

5. **Output Generation**: The final step involves generating the self-consistent legal document by replacing unresolved references with the correct entities and ensuring that all references are consistent throughout the text.

### 3.2 Mathematical Models and Formulas

The Self-Consistency CoT algorithm employs several mathematical models and formulas to facilitate the resolution and consistency checking processes. Below are some key models and their corresponding formulas:

#### Entity Identification

1. **Named Entity Recognition (NER) Probability Model**:

$$
P(E \mid T) = \frac{P(T \mid E) \cdot P(E)}{P(T)}
$$

Where:
- \( P(E \mid T) \) is the probability that a term \( T \) is an entity given the training data.
- \( P(T \mid E) \) is the probability of observing term \( T \) given that it is an entity.
- \( P(E) \) is the prior probability of an entity occurring in the text.
- \( P(T) \) is the probability of observing term \( T \) in the text.

#### Reference Resolution

2. **Coreference Resolution Similarity Score**:

$$
S(i, j) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-\theta \cdot \text{similarity}(i, j))}
$$

Where:
- \( S(i, j) \) is the similarity score between two mentions \( i \) and \( j \).
- \( \theta \) is a temperature parameter that controls the level of smoothing.
- \( \text{similarity}(i, j) \) is a measure of the similarity between mentions \( i \) and \( j \), which can be based on various features such as distance, semantic similarity, and context.

3. **Confidence Score for Reference Resolution**:

$$
C(i, j) = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N} S(i, k) \cdot w(k)}{\sum_{k=1}^{N} w(k)}
$$

Where:
- \( C(i, j) \) is the confidence score that mention \( i \) refers to mention \( j \).
- \( N \) is the number of potential entities that mention \( i \) could refer to.
- \( w(k) \) is the weight assigned to the similarity score between \( i \) and \( k \).

#### Consistency Checking

4. **Temporal Consistency Check**:

$$
C_t(d_1, d_2) = 
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } d_1 \leq d_2 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

Where:
- \( C_t(d_1, d_2) \) is the temporal consistency score between two dates \( d_1 \) and \( d_2 \).
- \( d_1 \) and \( d_2 \) are the dates associated with two references.

5. **Logical Consistency Check**:

$$
C_l(s_1, s_2) = 
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } s_1 \Rightarrow s_2 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

Where:
- \( C_l(s_1, s_2) \) is the logical consistency score between two statements \( s_1 \) and \( s_2 \).
- \( s_1 \Rightarrow s_2 \) indicates that statement \( s_1 \) logically implies statement \( s_2 \).
