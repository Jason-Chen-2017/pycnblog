#### 2.2.1 Regression Analysis

Linear regression is a simple yet powerful model that assumes a linear relationship between the variables.

**Mathematical Model:**
$$
y = \beta_0 + \beta_1x_1 + \beta_2x_2 + ... + \beta_nx_n + \epsilon
$$
where \(y\) is the dependent variable (bubble presence), \(x_1, x_2, ..., x_n\) are the independent variables (financial indicators), \(\beta_0\) is the intercept, \(\beta_1, \beta_2, ..., \beta_n\) are the coefficients, and \(\epsilon\) is the error term.

**Example:**
A linear regression model might predict the presence of a bubble based on the closing price of a stock and its trading volume. By analyzing historical data, the model can identify patterns that suggest a bubble is forming.

### 3.2 Accuracy

Accuracy is one of the most straightforward and widely used evaluation metrics in machine learning. It measures the proportion of correct predictions out of the total number of predictions made by the model. In the context of bubble detection, accuracy indicates how well the model can identify both bubble and non-bubble instances.

**Mathematical Model:**
$$
\text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{Number of Correct Predictions}}{\text{Total Number of Predictions}}
$$

**Example:**
If a bubble detection model predicts that 100 out of 200 instances are bubbles, and 90 of those predictions are correct, the accuracy of the model would be 90%.

**Mermaid Flowchart:**

### 3.3 Precision

Precision measures the proportion of correct positive predictions out of the total number of positive predictions made by the model. In the context of bubble detection, precision indicates how confident the model is when predicting a bubble.

**Mathematical Model:**
$$
\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{True Positives + False Positives}}
$$

**Example:**
If a bubble detection model predicts that 100 out of 200 instances are bubbles, and 90 of those predictions are correct, while 10 false positives are predicted, the precision of the model would be 0.9 (or 90%).

**Mermaid Flowchart:**
```mermaid
graph TD

D --> E[Calculate Precision]
```

### 3.4 Recall

Recall measures the proportion of correct positive predictions out of the total number of actual positives. In the context of bubble detection, recall indicates how well the model can identify all the bubble instances present in the data.

**Mathematical Model:**
$$
\text{Recall} = \frac{\text{True Positives}}{\text{True Positives + False Negatives}}
$$

**Example:**
If a bubble detection model predicts that 100 out of 200 instances are bubbles, and 90 of those predictions are correct, while 10 false negatives are not predicted, the recall of the model would be 0.9 (or 90%).

**Mermaid Flowchart:**
```mermaid
graph TD
A[Input Data] --> B[Predict Bubbles]
B --> C[Identify True Positives]
C --> D[Identify False Negatives]
D --> E[Calculate Recall]
```

### 3.5 F1 Score

The F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced measure of the model's performance. It is particularly useful when the costs of false positives and false negatives are comparable.

**Mathematical Model:**
$$
\text{F1 Score} = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}
$$

**Example:**
If a bubble detection model has a precision of 0.9 and a recall of 0.9, the F1 score would be 0.9.
