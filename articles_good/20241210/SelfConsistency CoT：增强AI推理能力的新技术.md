#### Mathematical Model

The mathematical model of Self-Consistency CoT is designed to ensure that the model's predictions are internally consistent and coherent. One way to achieve this is by incorporating a self-consistency loss function into the training process. Below is a simplified mathematical representation of this loss function:

$$
L_{self-consistency} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{K} ||\hat{y}_{ij} - y_{ij}||^2
$$

Where:

- \( L_{self-consistency} \) is the self-consistency loss.
- \( N \) is the number of training examples.
- \( K \) is the number of classes or predictions.
- \( \hat{y}_{ij} \) is the predicted probability for class \( j \) given example \( i \).
- \( y_{ij} \) is the true probability for class \( j \) given example \( i \).

This loss function measures the difference between the predicted probabilities and the true probabilities for each class in each training example. The goal of the training process is to minimize this loss, thus ensuring that the model's predictions are internally consistent.

#### Example

Let's consider a binary classification task where we want to predict whether an email is spam or not. Suppose we have a training dataset with \( N = 1000 \) emails, and the true labels are as follows:

| Email | Label |
|-------|-------|
| E1    | Spam  |
| E2    | Not Spam |
| ...   | ...   |
| E1000 | Spam  |

After training a model, we get the following predicted probabilities for each email:

| Email | Predicted Probability (Spam) |
|-------|-----------------------------|
| E1    | 0.95                        |
| E2    | 0.05                        |
| ...   | ...                         |
| E1000 | 0.90                        |

Using the self-consistency loss function, we calculate the loss as follows:

$$
L_{self-consistency} = \frac{1}{1000} \sum_{i=1}^{1000} \sum_{j=1}^{2} ||\hat{y}_{ij} - y_{ij}||^2
$$

$$
L_{self-consistency} = \frac{1}{1000} \left( (0.95 - 1)^2 + (0.05 - 0)^2 + ... + (0.90 - 1)^2 \right)
$$

$$
L_{self-consistency} = 0.0025
$$

The lower the value of \( L_{self-consistency} \), the more internally consistent the model's predictions are. In this example, the loss is relatively low, indicating that the model's predictions are coherent and consistent with the true labels.

By incorporating a self-consistency loss function into the training process, Self-Consistency CoT ensures that the model's internal knowledge representation is consistent and coherent, which in turn enhances its inference capabilities in complex scenarios.

**Implementation**: Introduce a self-consistency loss function, which penalizes discrepancies between the model's predictions and its internal knowledge.

**Mathematical Model**:
$$
L_{self-consistency} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{j=1}^{K} ||\hat{y}_{ij} - y_{ij}||^2
$$

Where:
- \( L_{self-consistency} \) is the self-consistency loss.
- \( N \) is the number of training examples.
- \( K \) is the number of classes.
- \( \hat{y}_{ij} \) is the predicted probability for class \( j \) given example \( i \).

#### Algorithm Principle 2: Collaborative Mechanisms

**Principle**: Leverage the strengths of multiple models through collaborative reasoning.

**Implementation**: Train multiple models and combine their predictions to improve overall inference capabilities.

**Mathematical Model**:
$$
\hat{y}_{ij} = \frac{1}{C} \sum_{k=1}^{C} \hat{y}_{ij}^k
$$

Where:
- \( \hat{y}_{ij} \) is the final prediction for class \( j \) given example \( i \).
- \( C \) is the number of collaborating models.
- \( \hat{y}_{ij}^k \) is the prediction for class \( j \) given example \( i \) from model \( k \).

**Example**: In a text classification task, three models predict the sentiment of a sentence as positive, negative, or neutral. The final sentiment is determined by averaging the predictions from all three models.

#### Algorithm Principle 3: Noise Resistance

**Principle**: Enhance the model’s ability to filter out noise and maintain consistency in predictions.

**Implementation**: Train the model on noisy data or incorporate noise filtering mechanisms during the inference process.

**Mathematical Model**:
$$
L_{noise-resistance} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} ||\hat{y}_{ij} - \hat{y}_{ij}^{noisy}||^2
$$

Where:
- \( L_{noise-resistance} \) is the noise resistance loss.
- \( \hat{y}_{ij} \) is the predicted probability for class \( j \) given example \( i \).
- \( \hat{y}_{ij}^{noisy} \) is the predicted probability for class \( j \) given example \( i \) with added noise.

**Example**: In a speech recognition task, the model is trained on speech signals with added background noise. During inference, the model uses noise filtering techniques to clean the input signal before making a prediction.
