### Chapter 1: Background and Core Concepts

#### 1.1 Introduction to Zero-Shot Learning

**Core Concepts:**
Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) is an emerging field in machine learning that focuses on enabling models to make predictions or classifications without any prior exposure to the target classes during training. Traditional machine learning relies heavily on labeled data, where models are trained on examples from known classes. However, in real-world scenarios, obtaining labeled data can be time-consuming, costly, and sometimes impossible. ZSL aims to address this limitation by enabling models to learn and generalize from limited or even no labeled data.

**Problem Background:**
The scarcity of labeled data is a significant challenge in many domains, such as healthcare, where labeled data for new diseases or conditions can be scarce, and finance, where obtaining labeled data for specific financial instruments can be difficult. Traditional machine learning models struggle with this issue, as they require a large amount of labeled data to achieve high accuracy. This dependency on labeled data not only increases the cost of data collection but also limits the applicability of these models in scenarios where labeled data is unavailable or impractical to obtain.

**Problem Description:**
The core problem in Zero-Shot Learning is to develop models that can generalize well when faced with unseen classes during inference. In other words, a model trained on a set of classes should be able to make accurate predictions for classes it has never seen before. This requires the model to learn not just from the instances it is trained on, but also to understand the underlying concept or category to which these instances belong.

**Problem Solution:**
Zero-Shot Learning models achieve this by leveraging additional sources of information, such as semantic similarity between classes, or by using a specialized training mechanism that encourages the model to learn abstract representations rather than specific examples. This allows the model to perform well even when it encounters unseen classes during inference.

**Boundary and Extension:**
While Zero-Shot Learning is primarily associated with supervised learning tasks, its principles can be extended to other paradigms such as unsupervised learning and reinforcement learning. Moreover, the scope of Zero-Shot Learning is not limited to classification tasks; it can also be applied to regression and other predictive tasks. Additionally, as the field evolves, new techniques and algorithms are emerging that push the boundaries of what is possible in Zero-Shot Learning, making it a dynamic and exciting area of research.

### Chapter 2: Principles of Zero-Shot Learning

#### 2.1 Theoretical Foundations

**Basic Principles:**
The core principle of Zero-Shot Learning is the ability of a model to generalize to unseen classes without any prior exposure during training. This is achieved through the use of additional sources of information, such as semantic similarity between classes, or by designing training mechanisms that encourage the model to learn abstract representations.

**Mathematical Models and Formulas:**

$$
P(y|\hat{x}) = \frac{e^{\theta^T \phi(x)}}{\sum_{y'} e^{\theta^T \phi(x')}}
$$

where:
- \( P(y|\hat{x}) \) is the predicted probability of class \( y \) given the input \( \hat{x} \).
- \( \theta \) represents the model parameters.
- \( \phi(x) \) is the feature representation of the input \( x \).
- The denominator is the normalization term to ensure that the probabilities sum to 1.
