### Part 1: Introduction to Multi-Agent Systems and Global Value Investing

#### 1.1 The Concept and Evolution of Multi-Agent Systems

Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) are a collection of autonomous agents working together to achieve common goals. These systems have evolved over time from the early days of artificial intelligence research to become a critical area of study in computer science and engineering. The foundational principles of MAS include autonomy, social ability, adaptability, and autonomy.

##### Terminology and Core Concepts

- **Autonomous Agent**: An agent that can initiate actions, perceive the environment, and maintain its own internal state.
- **Social Agent**: An agent capable of communicating and collaborating with other agents.
- **Adaptive Agent**: An agent that can modify its behavior based on changing conditions.
- **Multi-Agent System**: A collection of agents that interact and collaborate to achieve a common objective.

##### Background and Development

MAS originated from the need to simulate and understand the behavior of complex systems composed of many interacting components. Over the years, significant advancements have been made in agent architectures, communication protocols, and collaboration mechanisms. These advancements have enabled MAS to be applied in various fields, including economics, logistics, and finance.

#### 1.2 Global Value Investing: Theory and Practice

Global Value Investing is a strategy that involves identifying undervalued securities in the global market and purchasing them with the expectation that their market value will increase over time. This approach is grounded in the principles of Benjamin Graham and David Dodd, who emphasized the importance of intrinsic value over market price.

##### Core Principles

- **Intrinsic Value**: The true value of a security, calculated based on its future cash flows and risk.
- **Market Price**: The current price at which a security is traded in the market.
- **Margin of Safety**: The difference between a security's intrinsic value and its market price, providing a buffer against uncertainty.

##### Practical Application

Global value investors often conduct extensive research to identify companies with strong fundamentals but are temporarily undervalued by the market. They then purchase these securities with the expectation that the market will recognize their true value, leading to capital appreciation.

#### 3.1 Introduction to Agent-Based Modeling

##### Key Concepts

- **Agent-Based Modeling (ABM)**: A computational approach to modeling complex systems composed of interacting agents.
- **Individual-Based Modeling (IBM)**: A subset of ABM that focuses on individual agents and their interactions.
- **Agent-Based Simulation (ABS)**: The process of simulating the behavior of agents within a modeled system.

##### Core Principles

- **Agent-Centered Approach**: Focuses on the behavior and interactions of individual agents.
- **Bottom-Up Approach**: Begins with the individual agent and aggregates their behaviors to understand the overall system.
- **Repeatability**: Simulations can be repeated to test different scenarios and hypotheses.

#### 3.2 Key Components of Agent-Based Models

##### Core Components

- **Agents**: The individual entities that represent real-world entities (e.g., investors, firms, consumers).
- **Attributes**: Characteristics or properties of agents (e.g., wealth, risk tolerance, investment strategies).
- **Actions**: Behaviors or decisions made by agents based on their current state and environment.
- **Environment**: The context in which agents operate, including other agents and external factors (e.g., market conditions, regulations).

##### Key Concepts

- **Agent Interaction**: The communication and collaboration between agents.
- **Agent Evolution**: Changes in agent attributes and behaviors over time.
- **Agent Emergence**: The emergence of global behaviors or patterns from the interactions of individual agents.

### Part 4: Collaborative Search Algorithms for Investment Opportunities

In this section, we will discuss the algorithms and techniques used for collaborative search in investment opportunities. We will cover collaborative filtering algorithms, multi-agent reinforcement learning for opportunity discovery, and the evaluation and comparison of these algorithms.

#### 4.1 Overview of Collaborative Search Algorithms

##### Collaborative Filtering

- **Definition**: A method of making automatic predictions (filtering) about the interests of a user by collecting preferences from many users (collaborating).
- **Types**:
  - **User-Based**: Recommends items that similar users have liked.
  - **Item-Based**: Recommends items that are similar to the items a user has liked.

##### Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

- **Definition**: A type of machine learning where agents learn optimal policies through trial and error interactions with the environment.
- **Components**:
  - **Agent**: The learner that interacts with the environment.
  - **Environment**: The system with which the agent interacts.
  - **State**: The situation the agent is in.
  - **Action**: A decision made by the agent.
  - **Reward**: Feedback received by the agent for taking a particular action.

##### Key Algorithms

- **Q-Learning**: An algorithm that learns the optimal action-value function, representing the expected utility of an action in a given state.
- **SARSA**: A reinforcement learning algorithm that updates the action-value function using the current state, action, and reward.
- **Deep Q-Network (DQN)**: A neural network-based approach to estimating action-value functions.

#### 4.2 Collaborative Filtering Algorithms

##### User-Based Collaborative Filtering

- **Objective**: Recommend items based on the preferences of users with similar profiles.
- **Steps**:
  - **User Similarity**: Calculate the similarity between users based on their historical preferences.
  - **Item Selection**: Identify items that similar users have liked.
  - **Recommendation Generation**: Recommend items to the target user based on the selected items.

##### Item-Based Collaborative Filtering

- **Objective**: Recommend items that are similar to the items a user has liked.
- **Steps**:
  - **Item Similarity**: Calculate the similarity between items based on their co-occurrence patterns.
  - **User History**: Determine the items liked by the target user.
  - **Recommendation Generation**: Recommend items that are similar to the items in the user's history.

#### 4.3 Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Opportunity Discovery

##### Key Concepts

- **Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL)**: A reinforcement learning approach where multiple agents learn optimal policies by interacting with a shared environment.
- **Team Formation**: Agents form teams based on their skills, objectives, and communication capabilities.
- **Policy Learning**: Each agent learns an optimal policy to maximize its individual reward while considering the collective reward of the team.

##### Methods

- **Centralized Training**: All agents share a single policy learned by a central coordinator.
- **Decentralized Training**: Each agent learns its own policy based on local interactions and shared information.
- **Decentralized Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (DMARL)**: A framework for decentralized training where agents learn policies independently but collaborate to improve their performance.

#### 4.4 Evaluating and Comparing Collaborative Search Algorithms

##### Evaluation Metrics

- **Accuracy**: The ratio of correct predictions to the total number of predictions.
- **Precision**: The ratio of relevant predictions to the total number of positive predictions.
- **Recall**: The ratio of relevant predictions to the total number of actual relevant items.
- **F1 Score**: The harmonic mean of precision and recall.

##### Comparison Methods

- **Simulation-Based Evaluation**: Simulating the performance of algorithms in various scenarios and comparing their outcomes.
- **Real-World Experiments**: Testing the algorithms on real-world data and evaluating their practical performance.
- **Case Studies**: Analyzing the effectiveness of algorithms in specific industries or markets.
