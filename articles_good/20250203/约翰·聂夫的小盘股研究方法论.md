#### 1.2 Small-Cap Stocks: Definition and Characteristics

**1.2.1 Definition of Small-Cap Stocks**

Small-cap stocks are shares of companies with a relatively small market capitalization. Market capitalization is calculated by multiplying the current share price by the total number of outstanding shares. Generally, small-cap stocks are classified as those with a market capitalization between $300 million and $2 billion. However, this classification can vary depending on the specific market and index definitions.

**1.2.2 Characteristics of Small-Cap Stocks**

Small-cap stocks have distinct characteristics that set them apart from large-cap stocks:

1. **Market Capitalization**: As mentioned, small-cap stocks have a smaller market capitalization compared to large-cap stocks, which are typically companies with a market capitalization of over $10 billion.
2. **Volatility**: Small-cap stocks tend to be more volatile than large-cap stocks due to their smaller size and lower liquidity. This can result in wider price swings, both up and down.
3. **Growth Potential**: Small-cap stocks often have higher growth potential compared to large-cap stocks. Many small-cap companies are in their early stages of growth, and their share prices can rise significantly as they scale their operations.
4. **Liquidity**: Small-cap stocks may have lower liquidity, meaning that there may be fewer buyers and sellers in the market. This can lead to higher transaction costs and potentially larger price impacts.
5. **Risk**: Investing in small-cap stocks carries a higher level of risk compared to large-cap stocks. These companies are often less established and have a shorter track record, which can make them more susceptible to market fluctuations and economic downturns.

**1.2.3 Comparison with Large-Cap Stocks**

While both small-cap and large-cap stocks offer potential investment opportunities, they come with different risks and rewards. Here's a brief comparison:

1. **Market Capitalization**: Large-cap stocks have a market capitalization of over $10 billion, while small-cap stocks have a market capitalization between $300 million and $2 billion.
2. **Volatility**: Large-cap stocks tend to be less volatile than small-cap stocks due to their larger size and stronger financial positions.
3. **Growth Potential**: Small-cap stocks often have higher growth potential due to their smaller size and the potential for rapid expansion.
4. **Liquidity**: Large-cap stocks typically have higher liquidity, making them easier to buy and sell without significantly affecting the share price.
5. **Risk**: Large-cap stocks generally carry less risk than small-cap stocks due to their more established businesses and larger market capitalizations.

John Neff's research methodology is often referred to as the "four-point strategy." This framework encompasses four key elements that are essential for successful small-cap investing:

1. **Fundamental Analysis**: Neff believes in a bottom-up approach, starting with a thorough analysis of individual companies. He looks at key financial metrics, such as revenue growth, earnings per share (EPS), return on equity (ROE), and debt levels. By understanding a company's financial health and growth prospects, Neff can identify stocks that are undervalued by the market.
2. **Technical Analysis**: While Neff is primarily a fundamental analyst, he also incorporates technical analysis to gauge market trends and identify potential buying or selling opportunities. He looks at charts, trend lines, and other technical indicators to assess the stock's price action and market sentiment.
3. **Market Sentiment**: Neff pays close attention to market sentiment, often going against the prevailing sentiment to identify undervalued stocks. He believes that markets can be overly optimistic or pessimistic, creating opportunities for investors who can identify and capitalize on these imbalances.
4. **Company Management**: Neff places a strong emphasis on assessing the quality of a company's management team. He looks for experienced and competent leaders who have a track record of delivering strong performance and making sound strategic decisions. A strong management team can significantly impact a company's long-term success.

Stock picking is both an art and a science, and Neff has honed his skills over decades of experience. Here are some of his key principles for identifying and selecting small-cap stocks:

1. **Identifying Growth Stocks**: Neff looks for small-cap stocks with strong revenue and earnings growth. He focuses on companies that are scaling their operations and expanding their market presence. These growth stocks often have the potential to deliver significant returns over the long term.
2. **Assessing Value Stocks**: While Neff is primarily a growth investor, he also looks for value stocks that offer good value relative to their peers. He uses various valuation metrics, such as price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio, price-to-book (P/B) ratio, and enterprise value-to-EBITDA (EV/EBITDA) ratio, to assess the relative value of a stock.

A well-defined research process is essential for successful small-cap investing. Here are the key steps in Neff's research process:

1. **Setting Investment Goals**: Neff starts by setting clear investment goals, including the desired return, risk tolerance, and investment horizon. This helps him focus his research and make informed investment decisions.
2. **Creating a Watchlist**: Neff creates a watchlist of potential small-cap stocks based on his research criteria. He narrows down his list by analyzing key financial metrics, growth prospects, and market sentiment.
