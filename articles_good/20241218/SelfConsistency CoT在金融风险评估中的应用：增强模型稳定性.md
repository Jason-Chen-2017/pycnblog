To achieve this self-consistency, Self-Consistency CoT employs a set of mathematical principles and tools that enable the model to continuously self-adjust and self-validate its predictions. The mathematical foundation of Self-Consistency CoT is based on the concept of recursive equations, which allow the model to iteratively refine its predictions based on new data and changing conditions.

#### Recursive Equations

The recursive equations in Self-Consistency CoT are defined as follows:

$$
X(t+1) = f(X(t), D(t))
$$

where:

- \( X(t) \) represents the predicted state at time \( t \).
- \( D(t) \) represents the observed data at time \( t \).
- \( f \) is a function that captures the relationship between the predicted state and the observed data.

The function \( f \) is designed to ensure that the predicted state \( X(t+1) \) is consistent with the observed data \( D(t) \). If there is a discrepancy between the predicted state and the observed data, the model will adjust its predictions to minimize this discrepancy. This iterative process continues until the model achieves a high level of consistency and stability.
