## Mathematical Models and Formulas

### Neural Network Forward Propagation

The forward propagation process in a neural network involves calculating the output of each layer based on the input data and the network's parameters. The following formula illustrates the forward propagation of a single neuron:

$$
a_{ij}^{(l)} = \sigma(z_{ij}^{(l)})
$$

where:

* \(a_{ij}^{(l)}\) is the output of the \(i^{th}\) neuron in the \(l^{th}\) layer.
* \(z_{ij}^{(l)}\) is the weighted sum of the inputs and the biases.
* \(\sigma\) is the activation function, typically a sigmoid, ReLU, or tanh function.

### Backpropagation

Backpropagation is the process of calculating the gradients of the network's parameters with respect to the loss function. The following formula illustrates the backpropagation of a single neuron:

$$
\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_{ij}^{(l)}} = a_{ij}^{(l)} (1 - a_{ij}^{(l)}) \cdot \frac{\partial a_{ij}^{(l)}}{\partial z_{ij}^{(l)}} \cdot \frac{\partial z_{ij}^{(l)}}{\partial w_{ij}^{(l)})
$$

where:

* \(L\) is the loss function.
* \(w_{ij}^{(l)}\) is the weight connecting the \(i^{th}\) neuron in the \(l^{th}\) layer to the \(j^{th}\) neuron in the next layer.
* \(a_{ij}^{(l)}\) is the output of the \(i^{th}\) neuron in the \(l^{th}\) layer.
* \(z_{ij}^{(l)}\) is the weighted sum of the inputs and the biases.
* \(\sigma'\) is the derivative of the activation function.

### Loss Function

The loss function measures the difference between the predicted output and the actual output. Common loss functions in neural networks include mean squared error (MSE) and cross-entropy loss. The following formulas illustrate these loss functions:

$$
MSE = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2
$$

$$
CE = -\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{j=1}^{m} y_{ij} \log \hat{y}_{ij}
$$

where:

* \(y_i\) is the actual output.
* \(\hat{y}_i\) is the predicted output.
* \(n\) is the number of samples.
* \(m\) is the number of classes.
